# The Transformer

## 1. Overview

The Transformer, introduced in the seminal 2017 paper *"Attention Is All You Need"* by Vaswani et al. at Google, is arguably the most impactful neural architecture of the modern AI era. It discarded the complex recurrent and convolutional structures that dominated sequence modeling in favor of a purely attention-based architecture.

By relying entirely on an all-to-all sequence routing mechanism called **Self-Attention**, the Transformer achieved two massive breakthroughs simultaneously: it enabled unprecedented long-range dependency tracking and allowed for perfect parallelization during training. It is the direct ancestor of almost every modern Large Language Model, including GPT, BERT, Claude, and LLaMA.

## 2. Historical Context

Prior to 2017, sequence tasks like machine translation were dominated by Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and their variants (LSTMs, GRUs). While LSTMs were effective, they suffered from a fundamental flaw: **sequential computation**. 

To process the 10th word in a sentence, an RNN *must* first process words 1 through 9. This sequential bottleneck meant GPUs, which are designed for massive parallel computation, were severely underutilized. Furthermore, even with gating mechanisms, information degraded over long distances. In 2014, Bahdanau et al. added an "Attention" mechanism to RNNs to help them focus on specific parts of the input during translation, which drastically improved results. The Google team took this to the logical extreme: *What if we remove the RNN entirely and use ONLY the attention mechanism?*

## 3. Problem It Solves

1.  **The Parallelization Bottleneck:** By removing recurrence, every token in a sequence can be processed simultaneously during training, fully utilizing modern GPU architectures.
2.  **Long-Range Dependencies:** In an RNN, the distance between the 1st token and 1000th token is 1000 computational steps. In a Transformer, the distance between *any* two tokens is exactly **1 step** (via the self-attention matrix).
3.  **Representational Bottleneck:** Encoder-Decoder RNNs had to compress the entire source sentence into a single fixed-size vector. The Transformer allows the decoder to look back at the entire uncompressed source sequence directly.

## 4. Architecture Diagram Data

The original Transformer is an Encoder-Decoder architecture. Both sides are composed of stacked identical layers (usually $N=6$).

**Encoder Layer:**
1.  Multi-Head Self-Attention
2.  Add & Norm (Residual connection + LayerNorm)
3.  Position-wise Feed-Forward Network (FFN)
4.  Add & Norm

**Decoder Layer:**
1.  Masked Multi-Head Self-Attention
2.  Add & Norm
3.  Multi-Head Cross-Attention (attends to Encoder output)
4.  Add & Norm
5.  Position-wise Feed-Forward Network (FFN)
6.  Add & Norm

## 5. Layer-by-Layer Breakdown

### Input Embeddings & Positional Encoding
Because the network processes all tokens simultaneously, it has no inherent concept of order (unlike an RNN). To inject order, absolute **Positional Encodings** (sine and cosine functions of different frequencies) are added directly to the input token embeddings before they enter the network.

### Multi-Head Self-Attention
This is the core of the model. Each token creates three vectors: a **Query** (what I'm looking for), a **Key** (what I contain), and a **Value** (my actual content). Every token's Query is dotted with every other token's Key to generate an [Attention Heatmap](/architectures/transformer) indicating relevance. The token then updates its representation by taking a weighted sum of all Values. "Multi-Head" means this process is run in parallel (e.g., 8 times) in different projection spaces, allowing the model to simultaneously attend to different concepts (e.g., one head tracks grammar, another tracks pronoun references).

### Masked Self-Attention (Decoder)
During training, the decoder is fed the entire target sequence. To prevent it from "cheating" by looking at future tokens, a causal mask (upper triangular matrix of $-\infty$) is applied to the attention scores, forcing token $t$ to only attend to tokens $\le t$.

### Cross-Attention
The decoder uses its own internal state as Queries, but uses the Keys and Values generated by the final layer of the Encoder. This is how the decoder "reads" the original input sentence.

### Position-wise Feed-Forward Network (FFN)
A standard MLP applied independently and identically to every token position. It usually projects the $d_{model}$ dimension up by a factor of 4, applies a ReLU, and projects back down. This acts as a per-token associative memory.

## 6. Tensor Flow Walkthrough

Assume a batch size of $B$, sequence length $T$, embedding dimension $d=512$, and 8 attention heads.

```text
Input Tokens      : (B, T)
Embeddings        : (B, T, 512)
+ Positional Enc  : (B, T, 512)

--- Inside Encoder Self-Attention ---
Linear Projections: Q, K, V -> (B, T, 512)
Split to Heads    : Q, K, V -> (B, 8, T, 64)
Scores (Q * K^T)  : (B, 8, T, T)    <-- The O(T^2) Attention Matrix
Softmax           : (B, 8, T, T)
Context (Scores*V): (B, 8, T, 64)
Concat Heads      : (B, T, 512)
Output Projection : (B, T, 512)

--- Inside Encoder FFN ---
Linear 1          : (B, T, 2048)
ReLU              : (B, T, 2048)
Linear 2          : (B, T, 512)
```

## 7. Mathematical Foundations

**Scaled Dot-Product Attention:**
$$ \text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V $$
*Crucial Detail:* We divide by $\sqrt{d_k}$ (the dimension of the keys). If $d_k$ is large, the dot products grow large, pushing the [Softmax](/dojo/ml-softmax) function into regions with extremely small gradients. The scaling factor keeps the variance of the dot products near 1.

**Multi-Head Attention:**
$$ \text{MultiHead}(Q, K, V) = \text{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \dots, \text{head}_h) W^O $$
where $\text{head}_i = \text{Attention}(Q W_i^Q, K W_i^K, V W_i^V)$.

**Sinusoidal Positional Encoding:**
$$ PE_{(pos, 2i)} = \sin(pos / 10000^{2i/d_{model}}) $$
$$ PE_{(pos, 2i+1)} = \cos(pos / 10000^{2i/d_{model}}) $$
This specific continuous function allows the model to easily learn to attend by relative positions, since for any fixed offset $k$, $PE_{pos+k}$ can be represented as a linear function of $PE_{pos}$.

## 8. Forward Pass

1.  Input sequences are tokenized and embedded.
2.  Positional encodings are added.
3.  The sequence flows through $N$ encoder layers. At each layer, tokens route information to each other (Self-Attention) and process that information internally (FFN).
4.  The final encoder output (Keys and Values) is held in memory.
5.  The decoder generates output tokens autoregressively. To generate token $t$, it attends to previous outputs $1 \dots t-1$ (Masked Self-Attention), then queries the encoder memory (Cross-Attention), passes through its FFN, and projects to the vocabulary logits.

## 9. Training Process

*   **Teacher Forcing:** Because of the causal mask, the decoder does not need to run step-by-step during training. The entire target sequence is passed in at once, shifted right by one position, and the model predicts all next tokens in parallel using standard [Cross Entropy](/dojo/ml-cross-entropy).
*   **Label Smoothing:** Used to penalize overconfidence.
*   **Learning Rate Schedule:** The original paper used a highly specific warmup schedule: the learning rate increases linearly for the first `warmup_steps`, and decreases proportionally to the inverse square root of the step number thereafter.

## 10. Complexity Analysis

Let $T$ be sequence length and $D$ be representation dimension.

| Layer Type | Complexity per Layer | Sequential Operations | Max Path Length |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Self-Attention | $O(T^2 \cdot D)$ | $O(1)$ | $O(1)$ |
| Recurrent (RNN) | $O(T \cdot D^2)$ | $O(T)$ | $O(T)$ |
| Convolutional | $O(k \cdot T \cdot D^2)$ | $O(1)$ | $O(\log_k(T))$ |

**The Quadratic Bottleneck:** The $O(T^2)$ term in Self-Attention means that as sequences get very long (e.g., 100k tokens), the attention matrix ($T \times T$) completely dominates compute and memory.

## 11. Advantages

*   **Massively Parallel:** Can be trained on massive clusters using huge batches, enabling the scaling laws that define modern AI.
*   **Global Receptive Field:** Every token has direct access to every other token, capturing complex grammatical and semantic relationships seamlessly.
*   **Extremely General:** The exact same architecture works for text, images (ViT), audio, and graphs.

## 12. Limitations

*   **Quadratic Context Scaling:** Processing a 1-million token context requires extreme architectural hacks (like sparse attention or ring attention) because $O(T^2)$ is unsustainable.
*   **Autoregressive Inference Bottleneck:** While training is parallel, generation is strictly sequential. The KV-Cache must be held in VRAM for every token in the context, creating severe memory bottlenecks during serving.
*   **Lack of Inductive Bias:** Unlike CNNs (which assume spatial locality), Transformers assume nothing. They require vastly more data to learn the structure of the world.

## 13. Real World Applications

*   **Machine Translation:** The original task (e.g., Google Translate).
*   **Foundation Models:** The architecture underpinning ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and almost all contemporary AI systems.

## 14. Interview Questions

1.  **Why do we divide by $\sqrt{d_k}$ in scaled dot-product attention?**
    *   *To prevent the dot products from growing too large and pushing the softmax function into regions with vanishing gradients.*
2.  **How does a Transformer know the order of words if it has no recurrence?**
    *   *Through Positional Encodings added to the input embeddings, which provide a unique spatial fingerprint to each position.*
3.  **What is the difference between Self-Attention and Cross-Attention?**
    *   *In Self-Attention, Q, K, and V all come from the same sequence. In Cross-Attention (in the decoder), Q comes from the decoder sequence, but K and V come from the encoder's output sequence.*
4.  **Explain the $O(T^2)$ bottleneck.**
    *   *Every token must compute a dot product with every other token to form the attention matrix, resulting in $T \times T$ operations. This scales quadratically with sequence length.*
