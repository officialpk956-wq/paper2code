# Imagen

## 1. Overview
Imagen, introduced in 2022 by Chitwan Saharia, William Chan, Saurabh Saxena, Lala Li, Jay Whang, Emily Denton, Seyed Kamyar Seyed Ghasemipour, Burcu Karagol Ayan, S. Sara Mahdavi, Rapha Gontijo Lopes, Tim Salimans, Jonathan Ho, David J. Fleet, and Mohammad Norouzi (Google Research, Brain Team), achieved state-of-the-art text-to-image synthesis by proving a surprising architectural discovery: **Scaling the size of a frozen text encoder is far more impactful for image fidelity and text alignment than scaling the size of the diffusion U-Net.** Imagen paired a massive, frozen **T5-XXL text encoder (4.6 Billion parameters)** trained purely on text corpora with a **Cascaded Pixel-Space Diffusion Architecture** ($64 \times 64 \to 256 \times 256 \to 1024 \times 1024$). To prevent high classifier-free guidance values from causing color saturation and image degradation, Imagen introduced **Dynamic Thresholding**, enabling stable sampling at high guidance scales and delivering unmatched photorealism and precise text rendering.

:::tip
Imagen's core discovery: "Unreasonable effectiveness of large frozen language models: Increasing the size of the language model (T5-XXL) improves image quality and text-alignment far more than increasing the size of the image diffusion U-Net."
:::

## 2. Historical Context
In 2022, text-to-image generation was advancing rapidly across two main fronts:
- **CLIP-based Models (DALL-E 2):** Used joint multimodal CLIP embeddings trained on image-text pairs to condition diffusion models.
- **Cascaded Pixel-Space Diffusion:** Used diffusion models directly in pixel space to generate low-resolution images followed by super-resolution models.

Google researchers asked two fundamental questions: First, *are multimodal text encoders (like CLIP) necessary, or can pure text-only Large Language Models (like T5) provide superior representations for image generation?* Second, *how can we sample at ultra-high Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) scales without destroying image dynamic range?*

Imagen demonstrated that T5-XXL (trained on pure text without ever seeing an image) contained deeper compositional understanding, spatial awareness, and spelling capabilities than CLIP. When paired with **Dynamic Thresholding** (which clips pixel values dynamically at each sampling step), Imagen surpassed DALL-E 2 and Stable Diffusion on the DrawBench human evaluation benchmark.

## 3. Problem It Solves
Imagen addresses three core limitations of text-to-image models:
1. **Weak Text-Image Alignment & Spelling Failure:** CLIP-conditioned models struggle with complex spatial relations ("a red cube on top of a blue sphere") and spelling text inside images. Imagen's T5-XXL encoder (4.6B params) provides rich language representations that render legibly spelled text and complex spatial arrangements.
2. **CFG Over-Saturation Artifacts:** High Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) weights (e.g., $w > 10$) improve text alignment but push pixel values out of bounds $[-1, 1]$, causing harsh contrast, blown-out colors, and saturation. **Dynamic Thresholding** maintains realistic dynamic range at any guidance scale.
3. **High Resolution Computation:** Cascades generation into $64 \times 64$ base diffusion followed by two Efficient U-Net super-resolution stages ($64 \to 256 \to 1024$).

## 5. Layer-by-Layer Breakdown

**1. Frozen T5-XXL Text Encoder:**
- Takes text string $y$, tokenizes it to length $L_{\text{text}}$, and passes it through frozen T5-XXL Transformer layers.
- Outputs 4,096-dimensional contextual embedding vectors for each text token. Weights are completely frozen during Imagen training.

**2. Base Efficient U-Net ($64 \times 64$):**
- A 2B parameter U-Net operating directly in pixel space.
- Uses **Efficient U-Net** blocks: shifts channel capacity to lower-resolution blocks and adds self-attention and cross-attention at $32 \times 32, 16 \times 16,$ and $8 \times 8$ resolutions.
- Text embeddings are injected via Cross-Attention at all resolutions.

**3. Super-Resolution Cascade ($64 \to 256$ and $256 \to 1024$):**
- **$64 \to 256$ SR Model:** Conditioned on the $64 \times 64$ base image (concatenated along channels or upsampled) and T5 text embeddings. Uses memory-efficient cross-attention.
- **$256 \to 1024$ SR Model:** Uses noise conditioning augmentation (adding small Gaussian noise to the $256 \times 256$ low-res input) to prevent super-resolution artifact propagation.

## 6. Tensor Flow Walkthrough
Let's trace tensors through Imagen for batch size $B=2$ and text prompt length $L_{\text{text}} = 128$.

1. **Text Encoding:**
   - Text Prompt $y$: `"A panda wearing sunglasses playing guitar"`
   - T5-XXL Encoder $\rightarrow$ Embeddings: `(2, 128, 4096)`
2. **Base Diffusion ($64 \times 64$):**
   - Noise Image Input $x_t$: `(2, 3, 64, 64)`
   - Efficient U-Net Denoising + Dynamic Thresholding $\rightarrow$ Base Image $x_0$: `(2, 3, 64, 64)`
3. **Super-Resolution Stage 1 ($64 \to 256$):**
   - Upsample $x_0$ to $256 \times 256 \rightarrow$ `(2, 3, 256, 256)`
   - SR U-Net Denoising (conditioned on $x_0$ + T5 Embeds) $\rightarrow$ Medium Image: `(2, 3, 256, 256)`
4. **Super-Resolution Stage 2 ($256 \to 1024$):**
   - Upsample to $1024 \times 1024 \rightarrow$ `(2, 3, 1024, 1024)`
   - SR U-Net Denoising $\rightarrow$ Final High-Res Output Image: `(2, 3, 1024, 1024)`

## 7. Mathematical Foundations

### 1. Dynamic Thresholding Formulation
Static clipping clamps predicted pixel values $x_0$ strictly to $[-1, 1]$. When CFG scale $w$ is high, predictions explode outside $[-1, 1]$, and static clipping collapses pixel variance, causing saturated, featureless regions.

**Dynamic Thresholding Algorithm:**
At sampling step $t$, for predicted clean image $\hat{x}_0^t$:
1. Compute the $s$-th percentile absolute pixel value $s = \text{percentile}(|\hat{x}_0^t|, p)$ where $p = 99.5\%$.
2. Threshold $s = \max(s, 1.0)$.
3. Clamp predictions to $[-s, s]$ and scale back by $s$:

$$\hat{x}_0^t = \frac{\text{clamp}(\hat{x}_0^t, -s, s)}{s}$$

This rescales image dynamic range dynamically at each step, preventing saturation at high guidance scales ($w = 10 \dots 20$).

```text
  Static Clipping vs Dynamic Thresholding:
  Exploding Pixels: [-3.5, +4.0]
  Static Clamp [-1, 1]  ---> [-1.0, +1.0]  (Saturated Flat Regions!)
  Dynamic Scale (s=4.0) ---> [-0.875, +1.0] (Preserves Rich Gradient Contrast!)
```

### 2. Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)
$$\tilde{\epsilon}_\theta(x_t, t, y) = (1 + w) \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, y) - w \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \emptyset)$$

where $w \ge 0$ is the guidance scale, and $\emptyset$ is the unconditional null text token.

## 8. Training Procedure
- **T5-XXL Encoder:** 4.6B parameter model frozen completely; zero backpropagation gradients pass into T5.
- **Loss:** Standard MSE loss on predicted noise $\mathcal{L} = \|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \text{T5}(y))\|^2$.
- **Noise Conditioning Augmentation:** Super-resolution models are trained with Gaussian noise added to low-res conditional inputs, with noise level $\text{aug\_level} \in [0, 1]$ passed as a condition to the SR U-Net.

## 9. PyTorch Implementation
Here is a complete PyTorch implementation of **Imagen's Dynamic Thresholding**.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

def dynamic_thresholding(pred_x0, p=0.995, max_val=1.0):
    """
    pred_x0: Tensor of shape (B, C, H, W) representing predicted clean image
    p: Percentile threshold (e.g. 0.995 for 99.5th percentile)
    """
    B = pred_x0.shape[0]
    
    # Flatten spatial dimensions per sample
    pred_flat = pred_x0.view(B, -1)
    abs_pred = torch.abs(pred_flat)
    
    # Calculate s-th percentile per batch sample
    # torch.quantile computes percentile along dimension
    s = torch.quantile(abs_pred, p, dim=1, keepdim=True) # (B, 1)
    
    # Threshold s = max(s, max_val)
    s = torch.clamp(s, min=max_val)
    s = s.view(B, 1, 1, 1) # Reshape for broadcasting
    
    # Clamp to [-s, s] and scale by s
    clipped = torch.clamp(pred_x0, -s, s) / s
    return clipped * max_val

if __name__ == "__main__":
    B, C, H, W = 2, 3, 64, 64
    
    # Simulate exploding predicted pixels from high CFG guidance scale
    exploding_pred_x0 = torch.randn(B, C, H, W) * 3.5 # Values range from -10 to +10
    
    thresholded_x0 = dynamic_thresholding(exploding_pred_x0, p=0.995)
    
    print(f"Max Value Before Thresholding: {torch.abs(exploding_pred_x0).max().item():.2f}")
    print(f"Max Value After Thresholding:  {torch.abs(thresholded_x0).max().item():.2f}")
    
    assert torch.abs(thresholded_x0).max() <= 1.0001
    print("Dynamic Thresholding verification successful!")
```

## 10. Strengths
- **Unrivaled Text-Image Alignment:** T5-XXL provides rich spatial and conceptual language representations.
- **Precise In-Image Spelling:** Capable of rendering correctly spelled text on signs and objects.
- **Saturation-Free High Guidance Sampling:** Dynamic Thresholding enables high CFG guidance scales ($w > 10$) without color saturation.

## 11. Weaknesses
- **High VRAM Footprint:** Storing a 4.6B parameter frozen T5-XXL model alongside 3 U-Nets requires substantial GPU memory.
- **Pixel-Space Cascade Bottleneck:** Running 3 separate pixel-space diffusion models sequentially is computationally heavier than Latent Diffusion Models.

## 12. Research Evolution
Imagen proved that large text encoders are critical for diffusion alignment. It directly inspired **DeepFloyd IF**, **SDXL** (dual text encoders), and **Stable Diffusion 3**.

## 13. Interview Questions

**Q1: What was the primary surprising discovery made by the authors of Imagen?**
**A1:** They discovered that scaling the size of a frozen pure-text language model (T5-XXL, 4.6B params) improves image quality, spatial composition, and text alignment far more than scaling the size of the diffusion image U-Net.

**Q2: What is Dynamic Thresholding in Imagen and what problem does it solve?**
**A2:** High Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) scales push predicted pixel values outside bounds $[-1, 1]$. Static clipping clamps values to $[-1, 1]$, causing color saturation and flat contrast. Dynamic Thresholding calculates the 99.5th percentile absolute pixel value $s$, clamps predictions to $[-s, s]$, and divides by $s$, preserving rich contrast at high guidance scales.

**Q3: Why did Imagen use T5-XXL instead of CLIP as its text encoder?**
**A3:** T5-XXL was trained on massive pure-text corpora (C4 dataset), giving it deep syntactic, compositional, and spelling understanding. CLIP was trained on image-text pairs with short captions, making it weaker at complex spatial relations and text rendering.

**Q4: What is Noise Conditioning Augmentation in Imagen's super-resolution models?**
**A4:** When upsampling a low-resolution image ($64 \to 256$), small artifacts in the low-res image can be amplified. Noise Conditioning Augmentation adds random Gaussian noise to the low-res input image during training and conditions the SR model on the noise level, making the SR model robust to low-res artifacts.

**Q5: Compare Imagen with Stable Diffusion.**
**A5:** Imagen operates directly in pixel space using a cascaded architecture ($64 \to 256 \to 1024$) conditioned on T5-XXL. Stable Diffusion operates in an $8\times$ compressed VAE latent space ($64 \times 64$) conditioned on CLIP.
