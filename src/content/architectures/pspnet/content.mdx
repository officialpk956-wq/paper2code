# PSPNet

## 1. Overview
Pyramid Scene Parsing Network (PSPNet), introduced in 2016 by Zhao et al. from the Chinese University of Hong Kong and SenseTime, won the ImageNet Scene Parsing Challenge 2016 and achieved state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets like PASCAL VOC 2012 and Cityscapes. The core innovation of PSPNet is the **Pyramid Pooling Module (PPM)**, designed to exploit global spatial context at multiple sub-region scales. Standard FCN-based architectures struggle with scene parsing because they lack global contextual clues, often misclassifying ambiguous pixels (for example, confusing a skyscraper with a building wall or a boat with a car on water). PSPNet solves this by applying adaptive average pooling across four coarse-to-fine spatial grid scales ($1 \times 1$, $2 \times 2$, $3 \times 3$, and $6 \times 6$), upsampling the pooled features back to the original feature map resolution via bilinear interpolation, and concatenating them with the original feature representation. This provides the final classifier with both fine-grained local features and multi-scale global context.

:::note
PSPNet demonstrated that pixel classification in complex natural scenes cannot rely solely on local neighborhood features; explicit global context collected at multiple pooling resolutions is mandatory for high-precision semantic segmentation.
:::

## 2. Historical Context
By 2016, semantic segmentation had evolved from basic Fully Convolutional Networks (FCNs) to architectures using dilated convolutions (like DeepLab v1 & v2). However, models still frequently made catastrophic contextual errors. For example, a boat floating on a river might be misclassified as a car because its local texture resembles metal and glass, while the network failed to incorporate the global surrounding context of water. Traditional approaches tried using Conditional Random Fields (CRFs) as post-processing steps or multi-scale image inputs. However, image pyramids were computationally expensive, and CRFs lacked deep feature representation. The authors of PSPNet recognized that Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP), which was originally used in classification (e.g., SPP-Net) to generate fixed-length vectors from arbitrary-sized inputs, could be reformulated into a **Pyramid Pooling Module (PPM)**. Instead of flattening pooled features for FC layers, PPM upsamples them back to spatial grids, merging multi-scale regional context directly into the dense feature maps of a fully convolutional network.

## 3. Problem It Solves
PSPNet addresses three pervasive failure modes in deep semantic segmentation networks:
1. **Mismatched Relationship:** Complex scenes contain natural co-occurrence priors (e.g., a plane is usually in the sky, a boat is on water). Standard FCNs lack global receptive fields and often place "boats" on highways or "cars" in rivers.
2. **Confusion Between Similar Classes:** Classes with similar visual appearances (e.g., skyscraper vs. building, plant vs. grass) require contextual awareness to disambiguate. By analyzing the surrounding region at coarse scales ($1 \times 1$ and $2 \times 2$), PSPNet captures global class distributions.
3. **Inconspicuous Classes:** Small or distant objects (e.g., street signs, lanterns) are easily swallowed by larger background classes during repeated convolutions. Fine-grained pyramid grids ($3 \times 3$ and $6 \times 6$) isolate sub-regions, preserving the representation of small objects alongside global context.

## 5. Layer-by-Layer Breakdown
PSPNet modifies a standard ResNet into a dense feature extractor and appends the Pyramid Pooling Module:

**1. Dilated ResNet Backbone:** Standard ResNet downsamples the input image by $32\times$ using strided convolutions. PSPNet modifies stages 3 and 4 of ResNet using dilated convolutions (atrous convolution), locking the spatial reduction at $8\times$ (output stride 8). The output feature map shape is $(B, 2048, H/8, W/8)$.

**2. Pyramid Pooling Module (PPM):** The backbone feature map is split into 5 paths (the identity path + 4 pyramid branches):
- **Branch 1 ($1 \times 1$ bin):** Global average pooling over the entire feature map ($h \times w \rightarrow 1 \times 1$). Captures whole-scene global context.
- **Branch 2 ($2 \times 2$ bins):** Adaptive average pooling to a $2 \times 2$ spatial grid. Divides the scene into 4 quadrants.
- **Branch 3 ($3 \times 3$ bins):** Adaptive average pooling to a $3 \times 3$ grid.
- **Branch 4 ($6 \times 6$ bins):** Adaptive average pooling to a $6 \times 6$ grid. Fine-grained sub-region pooling.

**3. Dimension Reduction & Upsampling:** Each of the 4 pooled branches passes through a $1 \times 1$ convolution to reduce channel dimension from $C$ (2048) to $C/N$ (where $N=4$, so 512 channels per branch). Next, each branch is upsampled back to the original feature map resolution $(h, w)$ via bilinear interpolation.

**4. Concatenation & Fusion:** The original backbone feature map (2048 channels) and the 4 upsampled pyramid feature maps ($4 \times 512 = 2048$ channels) are concatenated along the channel dimension, forming a 4096-channel tensor $(B, 4096, h, w)$.

**5. Final Segmentation Head:** The concatenated tensor is processed through a $3 \times 3$ convolution (reducing channels to 512), followed by Batch Normalization, ReLU, Dropout (0.1), and a final $1 \times 1$ convolution projecting to $K$ target classes. The score map is bilinearly upsampled $8\times$ to match the original image dimensions $(H, W)$.

## 6. Tensor Flow Walkthrough
Let's trace a batch of images through PSPNet. 
Assume Input: `(B, C_in, H, W) = (2, 3, 473, 473)`. Number of classes $K = 21$.

1. **Backbone (Dilated ResNet-101):**
   - Input: `(2, 3, 473, 473)`
   - Conv1 to Layer 2: standard downsampling to stride 8 $\rightarrow$ `(2, 512, 60, 60)`
   - Layer 3 & Layer 4 (Dilated): maintaining spatial resolution $\rightarrow$ `(2, 2048, 60, 60)`
2. **Pyramid Pooling Module (PPM):**
   - Branch 1 ($1 \times 1$): AdaptiveAvgPool2d(1) $\rightarrow$ `(2, 2048, 1, 1)` $\rightarrow$ Conv1x1 $\rightarrow$ `(2, 512, 1, 1)` $\rightarrow$ Upsample $\rightarrow$ `(2, 512, 60, 60)`
   - Branch 2 ($2 \times 2$): AdaptiveAvgPool2d(2) $\rightarrow$ `(2, 2048, 2, 2)` $\rightarrow$ Conv1x1 $\rightarrow$ `(2, 512, 2, 2)` $\rightarrow$ Upsample $\rightarrow$ `(2, 512, 60, 60)`
   - Branch 3 ($3 \times 3$): AdaptiveAvgPool2d(3) $\rightarrow$ `(2, 2048, 3, 3)` $\rightarrow$ Conv1x1 $\rightarrow$ `(2, 512, 3, 3)` $\rightarrow$ Upsample $\rightarrow$ `(2, 512, 60, 60)`
   - Branch 4 ($6 \times 6$): AdaptiveAvgPool2d(6) $\rightarrow$ `(2, 2048, 6, 6)` $\rightarrow$ Conv1x1 $\rightarrow$ `(2, 512, 6, 6)` $\rightarrow$ Upsample $\rightarrow$ `(2, 512, 60, 60)`
3. **PPM Feature Fusion:**
   - Concatenate Backbone + Branch 1 + Branch 2 + Branch 3 + Branch 4
   - Shapes: `[(2, 2048, 60, 60), (2, 512, 60, 60), (2, 512, 60, 60), (2, 512, 60, 60), (2, 512, 60, 60)]`
   - Concatenated Tensor: `(2, 4096, 60, 60)`
4. **Classifier Head:**
   - Conv3x3 (4096 $\rightarrow$ 512) + BN + ReLU $\rightarrow$ `(2, 512, 60, 60)`
   - Dropout(0.1) $\rightarrow$ `(2, 512, 60, 60)`
   - Conv1x1 (512 $\rightarrow$ 21) $\rightarrow$ `(2, 21, 60, 60)`
5. **Final Output:**
   - Bilinear Interpolation ($8\times$ upsample) $\rightarrow$ `(2, 21, 473, 473)`

## 7. Mathematical Foundations
Let $X \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H' \times W'}$ be the feature map generated by the dilated ResNet backbone, where $C = 2048$, $H' = H/8$, and $W' = W/8$.

The Pyramid Pooling Module defines a set of pooling scales $\mathcal{S} = \{1, 2, 3, 6\}$. For each bin scale $s \in \mathcal{S}$:

1. **Adaptive Average Pooling:** Spatial pooling partitions $X$ into $s \times s$ uniform sub-regions and averages values within each bin:
   $$Z_s = \text{AdaptiveAvgPool}_{s \times s}(X) \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times s \times s}$$

2. **Channel Dimension Reduction:** A $1 \times 1$ convolution reduces channel depth to $C' = C / |\mathcal{S}| = 2048 / 4 = 512$:
   $$F_s = \text{ReLU}\left(\text{BN}\left(W_s * Z_s\right)\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{C' \times s \times s}$$

3. **Bilinear Upsampling:** Feature map $F_s$ is restored to spatial dimensions $(H', W')$:
   $$Y_s = \text{BilinearUpsample}\left(F_s, \text{target\_size}=(H', W')\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{C' \times H' \times W'}$$

4. **Pyramid Representation Aggregation:** The original feature map $X$ and all upsampled pyramid representations $Y_s$ are concatenated:
   $$Y_{\text{PPM}} = \text{Concat}\left(X, Y_1, Y_2, Y_3, Y_6\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{(C + 4C') \times H' \times W'} = \mathbb{R}^{4096 \times H' \times W'}$$

5. **Auxiliary Loss Formulation:** During training, an auxiliary loss is added at stage 3 of ResNet ($X_{\text{aux}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024 \times H'/2 \times W'/2}$) to optimize deep feature learning:
   $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{main}}(Y_{\text{final}}, Y_{\text{gt}}) + \alpha \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{aux}}(Y_{\text{aux}}, Y_{\text{gt}})$$
   where $\alpha = 0.4$ typically.

## 8. Training Procedure
PSPNet uses specific training techniques to handle deep dilated networks:

- **Auxiliary Loss:** Training deep networks with dilated convolutions suffers from vanishing gradients in early layers. PSPNet introduces an auxiliary loss branch attached after ResNet's `layer3` (stage 3). The auxiliary branch computes predictions and evaluates softmax cross-entropy loss weighted by $\alpha = 0.4$. During inference, the auxiliary head is discarded.
- **Poly Learning Rate Policy:** The learning rate decays according to a polynomial schedule: $\eta = \eta_0 \times \left(1 - \frac{\text{iter}}{\text{max\_iter}}\right)^{\gamma}$ with power $\gamma = 0.9$ and base learning rate $\eta_0 = 0.01$.
- **Data Augmentation:** Random cropping (e.g., $473 \times 473$ for PASCAL VOC, $713 \times 713$ for Cityscapes), random scaling (between 0.5 and 2.0), random horizontal flipping, and random color jitter.
- **Optimizer:** SGD with momentum $0.9$ and weight decay $10^{-4}$. Large crop sizes and batch size $\ge 16$ are essential for stable Batch Normalization statistics.

## 9. PyTorch Implementation
Here is a complete, standalone PyTorch implementation of PSPNet with its Pyramid Pooling Module (PPM) and shape verification.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

class PyramidPoolingModule(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, in_channels, pool_sizes=(1, 2, 3, 6)):
        super().__init__()
        out_channels = in_channels // len(pool_sizes)
        self.stages = nn.ModuleList([
            nn.Sequential(
                nn.AdaptiveAvgPool2d(output_size=(size, size)),
                nn.Conv2d(in_channels, out_channels, kernel_size=1, bias=False),
                nn.BatchNorm2d(out_channels),
                nn.ReLU(inplace=True)
            ) for size in pool_sizes
        ])

    def forward(self, x):
        h, w = x.shape[2:]
        pyramid_features = [x]
        for stage in self.stages:
            pooled = stage(x)
            upsampled = F.interpolate(pooled, size=(h, w), mode='bilinear', align_corners=True)
            pyramid_features.append(upsampled)
        return torch.cat(pyramid_features, dim=1)

class PSPNet(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, in_channels=3, num_classes=21, pool_sizes=(1, 2, 3, 6)):
        super().__init__()
        # Mock Backbone: Simulates Dilated ResNet-101 output (2048 channels, stride 8)
        self.backbone = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Conv2d(in_channels, 64, kernel_size=7, stride=2, padding=3, bias=False),
            nn.BatchNorm2d(64),
            nn.ReLU(inplace=True),
            nn.MaxPool2d(kernel_size=3, stride=2, padding=1),
            nn.Conv2d(64, 512, kernel_size=3, stride=2, padding=1, bias=False), # Stride 8 overall
            nn.BatchNorm2d(512),
            nn.ReLU(inplace=True),
            nn.Conv2d(512, 2048, kernel_size=1, bias=False),
            nn.BatchNorm2d(2048),
            nn.ReLU(inplace=True)
        )
        
        # Pyramid Pooling Module
        self.ppm = PyramidPoolingModule(in_channels=2048, pool_sizes=pool_sizes)
        
        # PPM Output: 2048 + 4 * (2048 // 4) = 4096 channels
        ppm_out_channels = 2048 + 2048
        
        # Classifier Head
        self.classifier = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Conv2d(ppm_out_channels, 512, kernel_size=3, padding=1, bias=False),
            nn.BatchNorm2d(512),
            nn.ReLU(inplace=True),
            nn.Dropout(p=0.1),
            nn.Conv2d(512, num_classes, kernel_size=1)
        )

    def forward(self, x):
        input_size = x.shape[2:]
        feat = self.backbone(x)
        ppm_feat = self.ppm(feat)
        logits = self.classifier(ppm_feat)
        # Upsample to original image resolution
        out = F.interpolate(logits, size=input_size, mode='bilinear', align_corners=True)
        return out

if __name__ == "__main__":
    device = torch.device("cuda" if torch.cuda.is_available() else "cpu")
    model = PSPNet(in_channels=3, num_classes=21).to(device)
    dummy_input = torch.randn(2, 3, 473, 473).to(device)
    
    output = model(dummy_input)
    print(f"Input Shape:  {dummy_input.shape}")
    print(f"Output Shape: {output.shape}")
    
    assert output.shape == (2, 21, 473, 473), f"Expected shape (2, 21, 473, 473), got {output.shape}"
    print("PSPNet shape verification successful!")
```

## 10. Strengths
- **Multi-Scale Context Aggregation:** Explicitly captures sub-regional context at $1 \times 1, 2 \times 2, 3 \times 3,$ and $6 \times 6$ bin scales, resolving spatial ambiguity.
- **Superior Boundary & Global Reasoning:** Reduces false positives between visually similar categories (e.g., field vs. grass, skyscraper vs. building).
- **Simple & Modular Design:** The PPM module is a lightweight plug-and-play block that can be placed on top of any CNN or ViT feature extractor.
- **Benchmark Domination:** Set historical accuracy records on PASCAL VOC 2012 (85.4% mIoU) and Cityscapes.

## 11. Weaknesses
- **High Memory Footprint:** Retaining stride-8 feature maps with 4096 concatenated channels requires large GPU memory during training.
- **Heavy Compute Overhead:** Dilated convolutions in late backbone stages ($2048$ channels at stride 8) increase FLOPs significantly compared to standard stride-32 classification networks.
- **Coarse Spatial Boundaries:** Relying on bilinear $8\times$ upsampling without a dedicated low-level feature decoder (like U-Net or DeepLabv3+) can blur fine object boundaries.

## 12. Research Evolution
PSPNet directly built upon Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP-Net, He et al., 2014) and dilated FCNs. It established that global scene context can be efficiently gathered via multi-rate adaptive pooling. PSPNet heavily influenced subsequent scene parsing networks, such as PAN (Pyramid Attention Network) and DANet (Dual Attention Network), which replaced static pooling grids with self-attention mechanisms while keeping PSPNet's foundational multi-scale design philosophy.

## 13. Interview Questions

**Q1: What is the main difference between Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP) in SPP-Net and Pyramid Pooling Module (PPM) in PSPNet?**
**A1:** SPP-Net uses pooling bins to collapse feature maps into a 1D vector for fixed-size input into Fully Connected classification layers. In contrast, PSPNet's PPM applies adaptive average pooling at multiple grid sizes ($1\times1, 2\times2, 3\times3, 6\times6$), projects channels with $1\times1$ convolutions, and *upsamples* each bin back to the original feature map's spatial dimensions using bilinear interpolation, concatenating them with the original feature map for dense pixel classification.

**Q2: Why does PSPNet use grid sizes $1\times1, 2\times 2, 3\times 3, 6\times 6$ specifically?**
**A2:** The grid sizes capture hierarchical spatial granularities. $1\times1$ captures whole-image global context. $2\times2$ divides the image into coarse quadrants. $3\times3$ and $6\times6$ capture fine-grained localized sub-regions. Together, they cover global, regional, and local contextual priors.

**Q3: What role does the auxiliary loss play in PSPNet training?**
**A3:** Because PSPNet uses deep dilated ResNet backbones (e.g., ResNet-101 at stride 8), backpropagating gradients through many dilated layers can cause vanishing gradients. An auxiliary classifier head attached after ResNet `layer3` injects supervision directly into middle layers, improving convergence. The auxiliary loss is weighted by $\alpha = 0.4$ and discarded during inference.

**Q4: How does PSPNet avoid losing spatial resolution in late backbone layers?**
**A4:** Instead of standard strided convolutions that reduce spatial resolution to stride 32 ($H/32, W/32$), PSPNet uses dilated (atrous) convolutions in layers 3 and 4 of ResNet. This maintains a stride-8 feature map ($H/8, W/8$) while expanding the receptive field of kernels without increasing parameter count.

**Q5: What happens to the channel dimensions inside the PPM module?**
**A5:** If the backbone outputs $C=2048$ channels, each of the 4 pyramid pooling branches reduces its channels to $C/N = 2048 / 4 = 512$ via a $1\times1$ conv after pooling. After upsampling, the 4 branches ($4 \times 512 = 2048$ channels) are concatenated with the original $2048$-channel feature map, producing a $4096$-channel tensor.
