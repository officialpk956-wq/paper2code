# GraphGPS

## 1. Overview
GraphGPS (General, Powerful, Scalable Graph Transformer), introduced in 2022 by Ladislav Rampášek, Mikhail Galkin, Vijay Prakash Dwivedi, Dominique Beaini, and Xavier Bresson (MILA, Nanyang Technological University, and Valence Discovery), unified graph neural network architecture design into a **Modular Hybrid Framework**. Prior to GraphGPS, the graph ML community was split into two competing camps: local **Message-Passing GNNs (MPNNs)** and global **Graph Transformers**. Rampášek et al. conducted an extensive theoretical and empirical analysis, proving that **neither pure MPNNs nor pure global Graph Transformers alone are optimal for graph learning**. GraphGPS introduced a hybrid layer architecture that **decouples and interleaves a 1-hop local MPNN module with a global Transformer attention module in parallel at every layer**, augmented with expressive **Positional and Structural Encodings (PE/SE)**. GraphGPS achieved state-of-the-art results across all benchmark suites (OGB, Long Range Graph Benchmark) while scaling linearly to large graphs.

:::note
GraphGPS core principle: "Neither pure MPNN nor pure Transformer alone is optimal. Interleave a local MPNN + global Attention module in parallel at every layer: $h_i^{(l+1)} = \text{MLP}\left( \text{MPNN}(h_i^{(l)}) + \text{Transformer}(h_i^{(l)}) \right)$."
:::

## 2. Historical Context
By 2022, graph deep learning suffered from a dichotomy:
- **Pure MPNNs (GCN, GIN):** Excellent inductive bias for local 1-hop neighborhood structures, but suffered from over-squashing over long graph diameters and bounded by the 1-WL expressiveness limit.
- **Pure Graph Transformers (Graphormer):** Excellent global long-range communication ($O(1)$ path length), but lacked local inductive bias, suffered from quadratic $O(N^2)$ compute overhead, and struggled to capture local edge connectivity.

Rampášek et al. created GraphGPS to unite both paradigms, proving that combining local MPNN messaging and global Transformer attention in parallel yielded superior performance across both local molecular tasks and long-range graph benchmarks.

## 3. Problem It Solves & Why Neither Pure MPNN nor Pure Transformer Alone Is Optimal

**Why Neither Pure MPNN nor Pure Transformer Alone Is Optimal:**
1. **The Limitations of Pure MPNNs:**
   - **Over-Squashing:** Message passing squeezes exponentially growing multi-hop neighborhood information into fixed-size node vectors, losing long-range contextual dependencies.
   - **1-WL Expressiveness Ceiling:** Incapable of distinguishing certain non-isomorphic graph structures.
   - **Local Inductive Bias Only:** Cannot establish direct $O(1)$ communication between distant nodes.

2. **The Limitations of Pure Graph Transformers:**
   - **Loss of Local Graph Inductive Bias:** All-to-all self-attention treats nodes as an unstructured bag of tokens, struggling to detect local subgraphs (like 5-ring carbon molecules) without complex positional encodings.
   - **Quadratic $O(N^2)$ Scalability Barrier:** All-to-all attention explodes in compute and memory for large graphs.

**The GraphGPS Hybrid Solution:**
GraphGPS processes **Local MPNN messaging** (preserving local graph topology) and **Global Transformer Attention** (enabling long-range $O(1)$ communication) **in parallel at every single layer**, combining their output embeddings via an MLP.

## 5. Layer-by-Layer Breakdown

**1. Positional & Structural Encoding (PE/SE) Module:**
- **Positional Encoding (PE):** Laplacian Eigenvectors (LapPE) or Random Walk Structural Encodings (RWSE) mapping global node locations.
- **Structural Encoding (SE):** Local sub-graph count statistics (triangles, cliques).
- Injected via concatenation or addition: $h_i^{(0)} = \text{Linear}\left( [X_i \;\|\; \text{LapPE}_i \;\|\; \text{RWSE}_i] \right)$.

**2. Dual Parallel GraphGPS Hybrid Layer:**
- At layer $l$, input $H^{(l)}$ is passed to two modules in parallel:
  - **Local MPNN Module:** Executes 1-hop local message passing (e.g., GINE layer with edge features):
    $$h_{i, \text{local}}^{(l+1)} = \text{MPNN}\left( H^{(l)}, A, E \right)$$
  - **Global Attention Module:** Executes Transformer self-attention (e.g., Full Attention or $O(N)$ Linear Attention like Performer/BigBird):
    $$h_{i, \text{global}}^{(l+1)} = \text{GlobalAttention}\left( H^{(l)} \right)$$

**3. Output Fusion & Feed-Forward Network:**
- Sums local and global outputs: $h_i^{\text{sum}} = h_{i, \text{local}}^{(l+1)} + h_{i, \text{global}}^{(l+1)}$.
- Passes through FFN with residual skip connections and LayerNorm/BatchNorm:
  $$h_i^{(l+1)} = \text{LayerNorm}\left( h_i^{(l)} + \text{FFN}\left( h_i^{\text{sum}} \right) \right)$$

## 6. Tensor Flow Walkthrough
Let's trace tensors through GraphGPS for mini-batch $B=16$ graphs ($N=100$ nodes per graph), raw feature dim 16, LapPE dim 8, RWSE dim 16, hidden dim $d=128$.

1. **PE/SE Injection:**
   - Raw $X$: `(16, 100, 16)`, LapPE: `(16, 100, 8)`, RWSE: `(16, 100, 16)`
   - Concat $[X, \text{LapPE}, \text{RWSE}] \to (16, 100, 40) \xrightarrow{\text{Linear}} H^{(0)}$: `(16, 100, 128)`
2. **Parallel Hybrid Layer Pass:**
   - Local MPNN Pass (GINE) $\to H_{\text{local}}$: `(16, 100, 128)`
   - Global Transformer Pass (Performer Linear Attention) $\to H_{\text{global}}$: `(16, 100, 128)`
   - Combine $H_{\text{local}} + H_{\text{global}} \to H_{\text{comb}}$: `(16, 100, 128)`
3. **FFN & LayerNorm:**
   - FFN ($128 \to 512 \to 128$) + Residual Add $\to H^{(1)}$: `(16, 100, 128)`.

## 7. Mathematical Foundations

### 1. GraphGPS Parallel Hybrid Layer Equations
For node $i$ at layer $l+1$:

$$h_{i, \text{local}}^{(l+1)} = \text{MPNN}^{(l)}\left( h_i^{(l)}, \{h_j^{(l)} \mid j \in \mathcal{N}_i\}, \{e_{ij}\} \right)$$

$$h_{i, \text{global}}^{(l+1)} = \text{Transformer}^{(l)}\left( h_i^{(l)}, \{h_j^{(l)} \mid j \in V\} \right)$$

$$h_i^{(l+1)} = \text{LayerNorm}\left( h_i^{(l)} + \text{FFN}\left( h_{i, \text{local}}^{(l+1)} + h_{i, \text{global}}^{(l+1)} \right) \right)$$

### 2. Linear Attention $O(N)$ Options in GraphGPS
To scale globally to large graphs without $O(N^2)$ memory explosion, GraphGPS incorporates linear-time attention mechanisms (e.g., Performer kernel mapping $\phi(x) = \exp(\omega^T x)$):

$$\text{Attn}_{\text{Linear}}(Q, K, V) = \frac{\phi(Q) \left( \phi(K)^T V \right)}{\phi(Q) \left( \phi(K)^T \mathbf{1}_N \right)} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$$

Matrix product $\phi(K)^T V \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ is computed in $O(N \cdot d^2)$ linear time.

## 8. Training Procedure
- Compute Random Walk Structural Encodings (RWSE) and Laplacian PE during data loading.
- Initialize GraphGPS with $L=10$ hybrid layers.
- Train using Cosine Annealing learning rate schedule with AdamW ($\text{lr} = 3 \times 10^{-4}, \text{weight\_decay} = 10^{-5}$).
- Evaluated across Long Range Graph Benchmark (LRGB) and OGB datasets.

## 9. PyTorch Implementation
Here is a complete PyTorch implementation of the **GraphGPS Hybrid Layer**.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

class GraphGPSLayer(nn.Module):
    """ GraphGPS Hybrid Layer: Local MPNN + Global Attention in Parallel """
    def __init__(self, embed_dim=128, num_heads=4, dim_feedforward=512, dropout=0.1):
        super().__init__()
        self.embed_dim = embed_dim
        
        # 1. LOCAL MPNN MODULE (Linear Message-Passing representation)
        self.local_mpnn = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(embed_dim, embed_dim),
            nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(embed_dim, embed_dim)
        )
        
        # 2. GLOBAL ATTENTION MODULE (Transformer Self-Attention)
        self.global_attn = nn.MultiheadAttention(embed_dim=embed_dim, num_heads=num_heads, batch_first=True)
        
        # 3. FFN & LAYERNORM STACK
        self.ffn = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(embed_dim, dim_feedforward),
            nn.GELU(),
            nn.Dropout(dropout),
            nn.Linear(dim_feedforward, embed_dim),
            nn.Dropout(dropout)
        )
        
        self.norm_local = nn.LayerNorm(embed_dim)
        self.norm_global = nn.LayerNorm(embed_dim)
        self.norm_ffn = nn.LayerNorm(embed_dim)
        self.dropout = nn.Dropout(dropout)

    def forward(self, x, adj_norm):
        """
        x:        (B, N, embed_dim)
        adj_norm: (B, N, N) Normalized adjacency for local MPNN
        """
        # 1. Local MPNN Branch Execution
        h_local = torch.matmul(adj_norm, x) # Neighborhood aggregation
        h_local = self.local_mpnn(h_local)  # (B, N, embed_dim)
        h_local = self.norm_local(h_local)
        
        # 2. Global Transformer Branch Execution
        h_global, _ = self.global_attn(x, x, x) # (B, N, embed_dim)
        h_global = self.norm_global(h_global)
        
        # 3. Combine Local + Global Branches
        h_combined = x + self.dropout(h_local) + self.dropout(h_global)
        
        # 4. Feed-Forward Network Pass
        out = self.norm_ffn(h_combined + self.ffn(h_combined))
        return out

if __name__ == "__main__":
    B, N, d = 16, 100, 128
    gps_layer = GraphGPSLayer(embed_dim=d, num_heads=4)
    
    x = torch.randn(B, N, d)
    adj_norm = (torch.rand(B, N, N) > 0.9).float()
    
    out = gps_layer(x, adj_norm)
    
    print(f"GraphGPS Layer Output Shape: {out.shape}")
    assert out.shape == (B, N, d)
    print("GraphGPS verification successful!")
```

## 10. Strengths
- **Best of Both Worlds:** Combines MPNN local inductive bias with Transformer global long-range communication.
- **Solves Over-Squashing and 1-WL Ceiling:** Global attention eliminates over-squashing while PE/SE breaks 1-WL expressiveness limits.
- **Linear $O(N)$ Scalability:** Supports swapping full $O(N^2)$ attention for $O(N)$ linear attention (Performer).

## 11. Weaknesses
- **High System Complexity:** Managing PE/SE pipelines, MPNNs, and Transformer backbones increases engineering complexity.

## 12. Research Evolution
GraphGPS established the current state of the art in graph Transformers, directly influencing **Exphormer**, **Polynormer**, and **UltraGNN**.

## 13. Interview Questions

**Q1: Why is neither a pure MPNN nor a pure Graph Transformer alone optimal for graph learning?**
**A1:** Pure MPNNs possess strong local 1-hop inductive biases, but suffer from over-squashing over long graph paths and are bounded by the 1-WL expressiveness ceiling. Pure Graph Transformers provide $O(1)$ global long-range communication, but lack local graph inductive bias, suffer from $O(N^2)$ computational complexity, and struggle to detect local subgraphs without heavy positional encodings.

**Q2: What is the core architectural layer structure of GraphGPS?**
**A2:** GraphGPS decouples and processes a **Local MPNN module** and a **Global Transformer Attention module** in parallel at every layer, summing their outputs and passing them through an FFN with residual connections and LayerNorm.

**Q3: How does GraphGPS scale to large graphs linearly $O(N)$?**
**A3:** By allowing the global attention module to be swapped with linear-time Transformer variants (such as Performer or BigBird) that compute kernelized attention in $O(N)$ time.

**Q4: What positional and structural encodings (PE/SE) are integrated into GraphGPS?**
**A4:** Laplacian Eigenvector Positional Encodings (LapPE) for global spatial coordinates, and Random Walk Structural Encodings (RWSE) for local subgraph topology.

**Q5: What benchmark suite was created to evaluate long-range dependencies in GraphGPS?**
**A5:** The **Long Range Graph Benchmark (LRGB)**, containing datasets specifically designed to evaluate whether GNNs can propagate information across long graph diameters without over-squashing.
