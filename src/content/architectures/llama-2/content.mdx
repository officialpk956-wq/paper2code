# LLaMA 2

## 1. Overview
LLaMA 2 (Large Language Model Meta AI) is an open-access foundational model released by Meta in July 2023. It builds upon the success of the original LLaMA model, offering significant improvements in scale, safety, and performance. Ranging from 7 billion to 70 billion parameters, LLaMA 2 was designed to democratize access to highly capable large language models for both research and commercial applications. The model boasts a context window of 4096 tokens—double that of its predecessor—and was trained on 2 trillion tokens of data, representing a 40% increase over the original dataset. A standout feature of the LLaMA 2 release is the introduction of Llama-2-Chat, a family of models specifically fine-tuned for dialogue use cases using Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Ghost Attention (GAtt). Architecturally, LLaMA 2 incorporates Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) in its larger configurations to improve inference scalability. LLaMA 2 quickly became the gold standard base model for the open-source community, driving an explosion of downstream fine-tunes and specialized applications.

:::note
While LLaMA 2 is often referred to as "open source," its license does include restrictions (e.g., users with >700 million monthly active users must request a special license), making it "open weights" or "open access" rather than strictly open source under OSI definitions.
:::

## 2. Historical Context
The release of the original LLaMA in early 2023 was a watershed moment. Its weights were leaked via BitTorrent, igniting an open-source AI revolution. Researchers realized that relatively small, highly trained models (like the LLaMA 7B and 13B) could rival much larger closed models like GPT-3 if fine-tuned correctly (e.g., Alpaca, Vicuna). However, the original LLaMA had a restrictive non-commercial license and a short 2048 token context window. Meta capitalized on this community momentum by releasing LLaMA 2. This time, they intentionally opened it for commercial use, aiming to establish LLaMA as the foundational operating system of generative AI. Crucially, in a post-ChatGPT world, releasing a raw pre-trained model wasn't enough; safety and alignment were paramount. Therefore, Meta invested heavily in the RLHF pipeline, releasing the Llama-2-Chat models alongside the base models, proving that open-access models could be both highly capable and rigorously safe for public deployment.

## 3. Problem It Solves
LLaMA 2 addresses several critical bottlenecks in the AI ecosystem. Foremost is the **accessibility and commercialization barrier**. By providing a commercially permissible license, LLaMA 2 allowed startups and enterprises to build AI products without relying on expensive, proprietary APIs (like OpenAI's) or worrying about data privacy when sending queries to the cloud. Secondly, it solves the **inference bottleneck** for large models. The 70B parameter model incorporates Grouped-Query Attention (GQA), drastically reducing the memory bandwidth required for the Key-Value (KV) cache during autoregressive generation, allowing a 70B model to be served efficiently on fewer GPUs. Thirdly, the Llama-2-Chat models address the **alignment problem**. Raw LLMs are difficult to use as assistants because they don't inherently follow instructions or adhere to safety guidelines. Through extensive RLHF and safety mitigation techniques, Meta provided an out-of-the-box model that acts as a helpful, harmless conversational agent.

## 5. Layer-by-Layer Breakdown
LLaMA 2 is a decoder-only Transformer building on the architecture of LLaMA 1. The input sequence passes through a **Token Embedding** layer. 

Inside the **Transformer Blocks**, LLaMA 2 applies pre-normalization using **RMSNorm** (Root Mean Square Normalization), which improves training stability and speed compared to standard LayerNorm. The core sequence processing is handled by the Attention mechanism. For the 34B and 70B models, LLaMA 2 uses **Grouped-Query Attention (GQA)** instead of standard Multi-Head Attention (MHA). This involves multiple query heads sharing a single Key and Value projection head, significantly reducing the memory footprint of the KV cache during generation. Positional information is injected into the attention calculation using **Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE)**, which encode absolute position with a rotation matrix, allowing for better extrapolation to longer sequences.

Following a residual connection and another RMSNorm, the data enters the Feed-Forward Network. LLaMA 2 uses a **SwiGLU** activation function (Swish-Gated Linear Unit). This replaces the standard ReLU/GELU with a gating mechanism that requires three weight matrices instead of two, but offers enhanced representational capacity. After iterating through all blocks, a **Final RMSNorm** and a **Linear Projection Head** yield the token probabilities.

## 6. Tensor Flow Walkthrough
Let's trace the tensor shapes for the LLaMA 2 70B model's Grouped-Query Attention. Assume Batch `B=2`, Sequence `T=4096`, `D_model=8192`, Query Heads `H_q=64`, KV Heads `H_kv=8`. Group size is `H_q / H_kv = 8`.

1.  **Input to Block:** `X`
    *   Shape: `(B, T, D_model)` -> `(2, 4096, 8192)`
2.  **RMSNorm 1:** `X_norm = RMSNorm(X)`
    *   Shape: `(B, T, D_model)` -> `(2, 4096, 8192)`
3.  **GQA Projections:**
    *   Head dimension `D_head = D_model / H_q = 8192 / 64 = 128`.
    *   `Q` projection: `(B, T, H_q, D_head)` -> `(2, 4096, 64, 128)`
    *   `K, V` projections: `(B, T, H_kv, D_head)` -> `(2, 4096, 8, 128)`
4.  **RoPE Application:**
    *   Rotary embeddings are applied element-wise to `Q` and `K` based on sequence position. Shapes remain identical.
5.  **Attention Calculation:**
    *   `K` and `V` are conceptually expanded/repeated 8 times to match the 64 `Q` heads.
    *   `Attention = Softmax(Q * K_expanded^T) * V_expanded`
    *   Output before projection: `(B, T, H_q, D_head)` -> `(2, 4096, 64, 128)`
6.  **GQA Output Projection:**
    *   Flatten heads and project back to `D_model`.
    *   `Attn_out` Shape: `(B, T, D_model)` -> `(2, 4096, 8192)`
7.  **Residual Add 1:** `X = X + Attn_out`
    *   Shape: `(B, T, D_model)` -> `(2, 4096, 8192)`
8.  **SwiGLU FFN:**
    *   `D_ff` is scaled up (e.g., ~28672).
    *   `FFN_out = (Swish(X * W1) * (X * W3)) * W2`
    *   Shape: `(B, T, D_model)` -> `(2, 4096, 8192)`
9.  **Residual Add 2:** `X_out = X + FFN_out`
    *   Shape: `(B, T, D_model)` -> `(2, 4096, 8192)`

## 7. Mathematical Foundations
A critical architectural decision in LLaMA 2 (70B) is the transition to **Grouped-Query Attention (GQA)** to alleviate the memory bandwidth bottleneck of the KV cache during inference.

Let's compare the three attention paradigms, assuming $h$ total query heads and $d$ head dimension.

**1. Multi-Head Attention (MHA):** (Used in LLaMA 1 and LLaMA 2 7B/13B)
Every query head has its own unique Key and Value head.
*   Number of KV heads = $h$.
*   KV Cache size per token $\propto 2 \times h \times d$.
*   *Pros:* Highest theoretical quality. *Cons:* Massive memory footprint during generation.

**2. Multi-Query Attention (MQA):** (Used in Falcon)
All $h$ query heads share exactly **one** Key head and **one** Value head.
*   Number of KV heads = $1$.
*   KV Cache size per token $\propto 2 \times 1 \times d$.
*   *Pros:* Extremely fast generation, tiny memory footprint. *Cons:* Can lead to capacity degradation and training instability.

**3. Grouped-Query Attention (GQA):** (Used in LLaMA 2 70B)
Query heads are divided into $g$ groups. Each group shares one Key and Value head. Let $1 < g < h$.
*   Number of KV heads = $g$ (e.g., 8 KV heads for 64 Query heads).
*   KV Cache size per token $\propto 2 \times g \times d$.

Mathematically, for a query head $i$ in group $j$ (where $j = \lfloor i \times \frac{g}{h} \rfloor$):
$$ Q_i = X \cdot W_Q^{(i)} $$
$$ K_j = X \cdot W_K^{(j)} $$
$$ V_j = X \cdot W_V^{(j)} $$
$$ \text{head}_i = \text{Attention}(Q_i, K_j, V_j) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_i K_j^T}{\sqrt{d}}\right)V_j $$

GQA acts as an interpolation between MHA and MQA. It provides nearly the same inference speedups and memory reductions as MQA while preserving the higher generation quality of MHA.

## 8. Training Procedure
LLaMA 2's training was an evolution in scale and safety methodology.

**Pre-training:** The base models were trained on 2 Trillion tokens from a new mix of publicly available online data (excluding Meta's user data). They utilized an optimized causal language modeling objective. Training the 70B model required massive compute (approximately 1.7M GPU hours on A100s). They doubled the context length to 4096 tokens compared to LLaMA 1, allowing the model to process larger documents natively.

**Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** For Llama-2-Chat, they started with SFT using high-quality, human-annotated dialogue data. They discovered that fewer, extremely high-quality examples (tens of thousands) yielded better results than millions of low-quality examples.

**Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF):** This is where Meta invested heavily. They collected over 1 million binary comparisons from humans (ranking which of two model responses was better). They trained two separate reward models: one optimized for **Helpfulness** and one optimized for **Safety**. The chat models were then optimized using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) against these reward models. Furthermore, they introduced **Ghost Attention (GAtt)** to help the model remember system instructions (e.g., "Act as a pirate") over multi-turn conversations, preventing character break after a few interactions.

## 9. PyTorch Implementation
Below is a minimal PyTorch implementation demonstrating RoPE (Rotary Position Embedding) and Grouped-Query Attention (GQA), which are key features of LLaMA 2.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F
import math

def apply_rotary_emb(x, freqs_cos, freqs_sin):
    # x: (B, Seq_Len, Heads, D_head)
    # Split features in half for rotation
    x1, x2 = x[..., :x.shape[-1]//2], x[..., x.shape[-1]//2:]
    # Rotate
    rotated = torch.cat((-x2, x1), dim=-1)
    return (x * freqs_cos) + (rotated * freqs_sin)

class LLaMA2_GQA(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, d_model, n_heads, n_kv_heads):
        super().__init__()
        self.n_heads = n_heads
        self.n_kv_heads = n_kv_heads
        self.n_rep = n_heads // n_kv_heads
        self.head_dim = d_model // n_heads

        self.wq = nn.Linear(d_model, n_heads * self.head_dim, bias=False)
        self.wk = nn.Linear(d_model, n_kv_heads * self.head_dim, bias=False)
        self.wv = nn.Linear(d_model, n_kv_heads * self.head_dim, bias=False)
        self.wo = nn.Linear(n_heads * self.head_dim, d_model, bias=False)

    def forward(self, x, freqs_cos, freqs_sin):
        B, Seq_Len, _ = x.shape

        # Projections
        xq = self.wq(x).view(B, Seq_Len, self.n_heads, self.head_dim)
        xk = self.wk(x).view(B, Seq_Len, self.n_kv_heads, self.head_dim)
        xv = self.wv(x).view(B, Seq_Len, self.n_kv_heads, self.head_dim)

        # Apply RoPE
        xq = apply_rotary_emb(xq, freqs_cos, freqs_sin)
        xk = apply_rotary_emb(xk, freqs_cos, freqs_sin)

        # Transpose for attention: (B, Heads, Seq_Len, Head_Dim)
        xq = xq.transpose(1, 2)
        xk = xk.transpose(1, 2)
        xv = xv.transpose(1, 2)

        # Expand KV heads to match Q heads (GQA logic)
        # (B, n_kv_heads, Seq_Len, Head_Dim) -> (B, n_kv_heads * n_rep, Seq_Len, Head_Dim)
        xk = torch.repeat_interleave(xk, self.n_rep, dim=1)
        xv = torch.repeat_interleave(xv, self.n_rep, dim=1)

        # Attention
        scores = torch.matmul(xq, xk.transpose(2, 3)) / math.sqrt(self.head_dim)
        scores = F.softmax(scores, dim=-1)
        output = torch.matmul(scores, xv)

        # Recombine heads: (B, Seq_Len, D_model)
        output = output.transpose(1, 2).contiguous().view(B, Seq_Len, -1)
        return self.wo(output)

# --- Test ---
if __name__ == "__main__":
    B, Seq_Len, D_model = 2, 64, 128
    n_heads, n_kv_heads = 8, 2 # GQA: 4 query heads share 1 KV head
    head_dim = D_model // n_heads
    
    # Dummy RoPE frequencies
    freqs_cos = torch.ones(1, Seq_Len, 1, head_dim)
    freqs_sin = torch.zeros(1, Seq_Len, 1, head_dim)

    attn = LLaMA2_GQA(D_model, n_heads, n_kv_heads)
    x = torch.randn(B, Seq_Len, D_model)
    out = attn(x, freqs_cos, freqs_sin)

    print(f"Input shape: {x.shape}")
    print(f"Output shape: {out.shape}")
    assert out.shape == (B, Seq_Len, D_model), "Shape mismatch!"
    print("Test passed: GQA with RoPE evaluated successfully.")
```

## 10. Strengths
LLaMA 2's primary strength is its status as a highly capable, commercially permissible open-weights model, which solidified it as the foundation for the open-source AI ecosystem. The 70B parameter model rivals closed-source models of its era (like GPT-3.5) while being entirely runnable on local hardware or private clouds. The architectural choice to use Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) in the 70B model drastically improved inference scalability, making it viable to serve to massive user bases without prohibitive memory costs. Furthermore, the Llama-2-Chat models demonstrated that robust safety and alignment (RLHF) could be achieved in open models, mitigating risks of toxicity and harmful outputs out-of-the-box.

## 11. Weaknesses
Despite its success, LLaMA 2 has limitations. Its 4096 token context window, while double that of LLaMA 1, quickly became a bottleneck as competitors released models with 32K, 128K, or even 1M token contexts, limiting LLaMA 2's ability to process large documents or long conversations natively. While safe, the Llama-2-Chat models were sometimes criticized for being overly cautious or "lobotomized," frequently refusing benign prompts due to aggressive safety tuning. Finally, as an older model (July 2023), its reasoning, coding, and mathematical capabilities lag significantly behind newer open models like LLaMA 3 or specialized coding models, highlighting the rapid pace of base model evolution.

## 12. Research Evolution
LLaMA 2 represents the transition of open-source AI from academic novelties to enterprise-ready infrastructure. LLaMA 1 proved that smaller, over-trained models were highly capable but was legally restricted. LLaMA 2 solved the legal barrier and introduced rigorous safety (RLHF) to the open-source paradigm, proving that alignment wasn't exclusively the domain of OpenAI or Anthropic. It also popularized architectural efficiency techniques like SwiGLU, RMSNorm, and GQA, establishing them as industry standards. The community's response to LLaMA 2's 4K context window directly fueled research into positional interpolation techniques (like YaRN or RoPE scaling) to artificially extend the context window post-training. Ultimately, LLaMA 2 set the baseline for what a foundation model should be, directly paving the way for the massive improvements in capability and scale seen in LLaMA 3.

## 13. Interview Questions

**Q1: Why does LLaMA 2 use Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) specifically for its 70B model, but standard Multi-Head Attention (MHA) for the 7B and 13B models?**
**A1:** The primary bottleneck in autoregressive LLM inference is memory bandwidth, specifically loading the KV cache for every generated token. In a 70B model, an MHA KV cache becomes massively large, severely limiting batch sizes and throughput on GPUs. GQA reduces the number of KV heads (e.g., 8 KV heads instead of 64), drastically shrinking the KV cache size and memory bandwidth requirements. For the 7B and 13B models, the KV cache is naturally much smaller, so the memory bandwidth bottleneck is less severe, allowing them to use standard MHA for maximum theoretical generation quality.

**Q2: How do Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) differ from absolute positional embeddings used in the original Transformer?**
**A2:** Absolute positional embeddings (like the sine/cosine waves in Vaswani et al.) add a positional vector directly to the token embedding at the input. RoPE is a relative positional encoding scheme applied *inside* the attention mechanism. It encodes absolute position with a rotation matrix and applies it to the Query and Key vectors. Because of the properties of rotation matrices, the dot product of a Query and Key in the attention mechanism becomes dependent only on their *relative* distance. This helps the model generalize better to sequence lengths longer than what it was trained on.

**Q3: What is "Ghost Attention" (GAtt) introduced in the Llama-2-Chat models?**
**A3:** A common problem in dialogue models is that they forget the initial system prompt (e.g., "Always answer in French") after a few turns of conversation. Ghost Attention is an RLHF technique to solve this. During fine-tuning, the system prompt is synthetically appended to all user messages in a multi-turn dialogue dataset, but the loss is only calculated on the later turns. This forces the model to heavily associate its responses across the entire conversation history with the initial system prompt, ensuring consistent persona adherence.

**Q4: Why does LLaMA 2 use RMSNorm instead of standard LayerNorm?**
**A4:** Standard LayerNorm computes both the mean and the variance of the activations to center and scale them, respectively. Researchers found that the mean-centering step is computationally expensive and provides negligible benefits to training stability or model performance. RMSNorm (Root Mean Square Normalization) simplifies this by skipping the mean calculation and only scaling by the root mean square. This makes the normalization step strictly faster, saving significant compute over a massive 2-trillion token pre-training run.

**Q5: Describe the dual reward model approach used in LLaMA 2's RLHF pipeline.**
**A5:** Meta found that helpfulness and safety are often inversely correlated (e.g., the most "safe" response is to refuse everything, which is unhelpful). To balance this, they trained two separate reward models: one optimized to score responses based on how helpful they were to the user's prompt, and another optimized to score based on safety guidelines. During the PPO reinforcement learning phase, the final reward signal was a combination of these two models, allowing fine-grained control over the trade-off between being an obedient assistant and refusing harmful requests.
