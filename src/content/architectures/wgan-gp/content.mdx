# WGAN-GP

## 1. Overview
Wasserstein GAN with Gradient Penalty (WGAN-GP), introduced in 2017 by Ishaan Gulrajani, Faruk Ahmed, Martin Arjovsky, Vincent Dumoulin, and Aaron Courville (MILA & Google Brain), solved the core practical flaw of standard WGAN: **capacity degradation and vanishing/exploding gradients caused by weight clipping**. In original WGAN, clipping Critic weights to $[-c, c]$ forced the network parameters to extreme bounds $\pm c$, drastically reducing capacity and causing optimization difficulties. Gulrajani et al. realized that the 1-Lipschitz continuity condition requires the gradient norm of the Critic to be **at most 1 almost everywhere** ($\|\nabla_{\hat{x}} f(\hat{x})\|_2 \le 1$). WGAN-GP replaces weight clipping with a soft **Gradient Penalty** term added directly to the Critic's loss function, penalizing gradients whose $L_2$ norm strays from 1 along straight lines connecting real and generated images. WGAN-GP achieved unprecedented training stability, allowing deep architectures (ResNets) to train stably without batch normalization across diverse image datasets.

:::tip
WGAN-GP's core formula: "Replace weight clipping with a Gradient Penalty: $\lambda \mathbb{E}_{\hat{x}} \left[ \left( \|\nabla_{\hat{x}} f(\hat{x})\|_2 - 1 \right)^2 \right]$ evaluated on random interpolates $\hat{x} = \epsilon x + (1-\epsilon) G(z)$."
:::

## 2. Historical Context
In early 2017, WGAN introduced Earth Mover's distance, establishing that continuous gradients could be preserved by enforcing a 1-Lipschitz constraint on the Critic. However, WGAN enforced this constraint via hard **weight clipping** ($w \in [-0.01, +0.01]$).

Weight clipping suffered from severe pathology:
- **Capacity Degradation:** Critic weights quickly saturated at extreme bounds $\pm 0.01$, turning the Critic into a primitive step function.
- **Vanishing/Exploding Gradients:** If clipping constant $c$ was too small, gradients vanished; if $c$ was too large, gradients exploded.

Ishaan Gulrajani et al. proposed penalizing the norm of the Critic's gradients directly with respect to its input pixels. By penalizing $\|\nabla_{\hat{x}} f(\hat{x})\|_2$ along interpolation lines between real and generated samples, WGAN-GP eliminated weight clipping, enabling stable training of ResNet architectures without Batch Normalization.

## 3. Problem It Solves
WGAN-GP resolves two major technical limitations of standard WGAN weight clipping:
1. **Weight Saturation & Capacity Underutilization:** Eliminates hard box clipping $[-c, c]$, allowing Critic parameters to utilize their full dynamic range and represent complex continuous functions.
2. **Vanishing/Exploding Gradients in Deep Critics:** Weight clipping caused gradients to explode or vanish as network depth increased. Gradient penalty dynamically constrains gradient norm $\|\nabla_{\hat{x}} f(\hat{x})\|_2 \approx 1$ everywhere.
3. **Batch Normalization Dependency:** Allows training deep architectures without Batch Normalization in the Critic, avoiding mini-batch sample leakage.

```text
                         +======================================+
```

## 5. Layer-by-Layer Breakdown

**1. ResNet / Convolutional Generator:**
- standard convolutional or ResNet generator mapping noise $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ to RGB images $(B, 3, H, W)$. Uses BatchNorm or LayerNorm and ReLU activations.

**2. ResNet / Convolutional Critic:**
- Operates without Batch Normalization (uses **LayerNorm** or no normalization) because BatchNorm introduces correlations between samples in a mini-batch, ruining gradient penalty calculations.
- Outputs an unconstrained scalar score $f(x) \in \mathbb{R}$.

**3. Gradient Penalty Calculation Module:**
- Samples random convex interpolates $\hat{x} = \epsilon x + (1 - \epsilon) G(z)$ for $\epsilon \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 1)$.
- Computes spatial gradients $\nabla_{\hat{x}} f_w(\hat{x})$ using PyTorch's `autograd.grad`.
- Computes gradient norm penalty: $\lambda \left( \|\nabla_{\hat{x}} f_w(\hat{x})\|_2 - 1 \right)^2$.

## 6. Tensor Flow Walkthrough
Let's trace tensors through WGAN-GP for batch size $B=4$, image resolution $64 \times 64 \times 3$.

1. **Random Interpolation:**
   - Real $x$: `(4, 3, 64, 64)`, Fake $G(z)$: `(4, 3, 64, 64)`
   - $\epsilon \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 1)$: `(4, 1, 1, 1)`
   - Interpolate $\hat{x} = \epsilon x + (1 - \epsilon) G(z) \to$ `(4, 3, 64, 64)` (`requires_grad=True`)
2. **Critic Forward Pass on Interpolates:**
   - Scores $f_w(\hat{x}) \to$ `(4, 1)`
3. **Autograd Gradient Extraction:**
   - $\nabla_{\hat{x}} f_w(\hat{x}) = \text{torch.autograd.grad}(\text{outputs}=f_w(\hat{x}), \text{inputs}=\hat{x}) \to$ `(4, 3, 64, 64)`
4. **Gradient Penalty Calculation:**
   - Flatten gradients: `(4, 3 * 64 * 64)` $= \text{`(4, 12288)`}$
   - Compute $L_2$ norm per sample: $\|\text{grad}\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum \text{grad}^2 + \epsilon} \to$ `(4, 1)`
   - Penalty: $\lambda \cdot \text{Mean}\left( (\|\text{grad}\|_2 - 1)^2 \right)$ (Scalar).

## 7. Mathematical Foundations

### 1. The 1-Lipschitz Condition
A function $f: \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}$ is 1-Lipschitz if and only if its gradient norm is at most 1 almost everywhere:

$$\|\nabla_x f(x)\|_2 \le 1 \quad \forall x \in \mathcal{X}$$

Gulrajani et al. proved that under optimal Critic conditions $f^*$, the gradient norm is **exactly 1** almost everywhere along straight lines connecting real data points $x \sim P_r$ and generated data points $y \sim P_g$.

### 2. WGAN-GP Loss Objective
The complete WGAN-GP Critic loss objective is:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Critic}} = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{x} \sim P_g}[f_w(\tilde{x})] - \mathbb{E}_{x \sim P_r}[f_w(x)]}_{\text{Standard Wasserstein Distance Loss}} + \underbrace{\lambda \, \mathbb{E}_{\hat{x} \sim P_{\hat{x}}} \left[ \left( \left\| \nabla_{\hat{x}} f_w(\hat{x}) \right\|_2 - 1 \right)^2 \right]}_{\text{Gradient Penalty Term}}$$

where $\hat{x} = \epsilon x + (1 - \epsilon) \tilde{x}$ for $\epsilon \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 1)$, and $\lambda = 10.0$ is the penalty weight.

The Generator optimizes:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Gen}} = -\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{x} \sim P_g} \left[ f_w(\tilde{x}) \right]$$

## 8. Training Procedure
For each iteration:
1. **Train Critic ($n_{\text{critic}} = 5$ steps):**
   - Sample $x \sim P_r, z \sim p_z, \epsilon \sim U(0, 1)$.
   - Compute interpolate $\hat{x} = \epsilon x + (1 - \epsilon) G(z)$.
   - Compute Critic outputs $f(x), f(G(z)), f(\hat{x})$.
   - Extract gradients $\nabla_{\hat{x}} f(\hat{x})$ via autograd.
   - Compute $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Critic}} = f(G(z)) - f(x) + \lambda (\|\nabla_{\hat{x}} f(\hat{x})\|_2 - 1)^2$.
   - Update Critic parameters using **Adam** ($\alpha = 0.0001, \beta_1 = 0, \beta_2 = 0.9$). *(No weight clipping!)*
2. **Train Generator (1 step):**
   - Sample $z \sim p_z$.
   - Compute $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Gen}} = -f(G(z))$ and update Generator.

## 9. PyTorch Implementation
Here is a complete PyTorch implementation of the **WGAN-GP Gradient Penalty Computation**.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.autograd as autograd

def compute_gradient_penalty(critic, real_samples, fake_samples, device="cpu"):
    """ Computes WGAN-GP Gradient Penalty """
    B = real_samples.shape[0]
    
    # 1. Sample random epsilon in [0, 1] for convex interpolation
    epsilon = torch.rand(B, 1, 1, 1, device=device)
    
    # 2. Compute interpolation: hat_x = eps * real + (1 - eps) * fake
    interpolates = (epsilon * real_samples + (1.0 - epsilon) * fake_samples).requires_grad_(True)
    
    # 3. Pass interpolates through Critic
    critic_interpolates = critic(interpolates)
    
    # 4. Compute spatial gradients w.r.t. interpolates
    gradients = autograd.grad(
        outputs=critic_interpolates,
        inputs=interpolates,
        grad_outputs=torch.ones_like(critic_interpolates),
        create_graph=True,
        retain_graph=True,
        only_inputs=True
    )[0]
    
    # 5. Flatten gradients to (B, channels * H * W)
    gradients = gradients.view(B, -1)
    
    # 6. Compute L2 norm per sample
    gradient_norm = gradients.norm(2, dim=1)
    
    # 7. Penalize distance from 1: (||grad||_2 - 1)^2
    gradient_penalty = torch.mean((gradient_norm - 1.0) ** 2)
    return gradient_penalty

if __name__ == "__main__":
    from wgan import WGANCritic # Reusing Critic without BatchNorm
    
    B = 4
    critic = WGANCritic(in_channels=3)
    
    real_imgs = torch.randn(B, 3, 64, 64)
    fake_imgs = torch.randn(B, 3, 64, 64)
    
    gp = compute_gradient_penalty(critic, real_imgs, fake_imgs, device="cpu")
    
    print(f"Calculated Gradient Penalty Value: {gp.item():.4f}")
    assert not torch.isnan(gp)
    print("WGAN-GP gradient penalty verification successful!")
```

## 10. Strengths
- **Superior Training Stability:** Enables stable training of deep ResNet GANs across diverse datasets without hyperparameter tuning.
- **Full Model Capacity Utilization:** Replaces weight clipping $[-c, c]$, allowing Critic parameters to utilize their full dynamic range.
- **Robust Optimizer Compatibility:** Works reliably with Adam ($\beta_1=0$) instead of RMSprop.

## 11. Weaknesses
- **Autograd Compute Overhead:** Computing spatial gradients $\nabla_{\hat{x}} f(\hat{x})$ via second-order autograd increases Critic step training time by $\approx 40\%$.

## 12. Research Evolution
WGAN-GP became the gold-standard training protocol for GANs, leading directly to **ProGAN**, **BigGAN**, and **StyleGAN**.

## 13. Interview Questions

**Q1: Why does WGAN-GP replace weight clipping with a gradient penalty?**
**A1:** Weight clipping $[-c, c]$ forces weights to extreme bounds $\pm c$, reducing network capacity and causing vanishing/exploding gradients in deep models. Gradient penalty directly constrains the norm of spatial gradients $\|\nabla_{\hat{x}} f(\hat{x})\|_2 \approx 1$ without restricting weight dynamic range.

**Q2: Why must Batch Normalization be removed from the Critic in WGAN-GP?**
**A2:** Batch Normalization introduces correlations between samples within a mini-batch. Gradient penalty requires evaluating the norm of gradients for EACH sample independently along its interpolation line. **LayerNorm** or no normalization is used instead.

**Q3: How are random interpolates $\hat{x}$ created in WGAN-GP?**
**A3:** Convex linear combinations of real images $x$ and generated images $G(z)$: $\hat{x} = \epsilon x + (1 - \epsilon) G(z)$, where $\epsilon \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 1)$ per mini-batch sample.

**Q4: What happens if penalty weight $\lambda$ is set too small in WGAN-GP?**
**A4:** If $\lambda$ is too small, the 1-Lipschitz condition is violated, causing unstable gradients and potential divergence similar to standard GANs. The standard empirical default is $\lambda = 10.0$.

**Q5: Write out the WGAN-GP Gradient Penalty loss term.**
**A5:** $\mathcal{L}_{\text{GP}} = \lambda \mathbb{E}_{\hat{x}} \left[ \left( \|\nabla_{\hat{x}} f_w(\hat{x})\|_2 - 1 \right)^2 \right]$.
