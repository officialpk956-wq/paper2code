# BERT

## 1. Overview

BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) is a groundbreaking language model introduced by researchers at Google in 2018. It fundamentally changed the landscape of Natural Language Processing by proving that self-supervised pre-training on massive amounts of text could create a universal language representation that could be easily fine-tuned for any downstream task.

Unlike earlier models (like OpenAI's GPT-1) which read text strictly left-to-right, BERT is deeply bidirectional. It uses only the **Encoder** portion of the Transformer architecture to look at the left and right context of a word simultaneously, achieving state-of-the-art results on 11 NLP tasks upon its release.

You can visualize BERT's bidirectional attention mechanisms using the [Attention Heatmap](/architectures/transformer/attention).

## 2. Historical Context

Before BERT, NLP relied heavily on word embeddings like Word2Vec and GloVe. These models assigned a single fixed vector to a word. Thus, the word "bank" in "bank account" and "river bank" had the exact same mathematical representation, ignoring context.

ELMo (2018) solved this by using bidirectional LSTMs to create context-aware embeddings, but LSTMs were sequential and slow. Around the same time, OpenAI released GPT, which used the Transformer architecture to predict the next word, but its left-to-right nature meant it couldn't see future context. BERT married the bidirectionality of ELMo with the massive parallelization and representational power of the Transformer.

## 3. Problem It Solves

How do you train a bidirectional model to understand context without letting it "cheat"?

If you train a bidirectional Transformer to predict the next word, the word to be predicted is already present in the right-side context, meaning the model trivially "sees" the answer through the unmasked attention mechanism.

BERT solves this by completely abandoning the "next word prediction" task. Instead, it uses two novel pre-training objectives:
1.  **Masked Language Modeling (MLM):** Randomly hide 15% of the words in a sentence and force the model to guess what they are based on the surrounding context.
2.  **Next Sentence Prediction (NSP):** Give the model two sentences and ask if sentence B logically follows sentence A in the original document.

## 4. Architecture Diagram Data

BERT is composed entirely of stacked Transformer **Encoder** layers.

**BERT-Base:**
*   Layers (L): 12
*   Hidden Size (H): 768
*   Attention Heads (A): 12
*   Total Parameters: 110M

**BERT-Large:**
*   Layers (L): 24
*   Hidden Size (H): 1024
*   Attention Heads (A): 16
*   Total Parameters: 340M

## 5. Layer-by-Layer Breakdown

### Input Embeddings
BERT's input is constructed by summing three distinct embeddings:
1.  **Token Embeddings:** WordPiece embeddings (a subword tokenization algorithm).
2.  **Segment Embeddings:** A learned vector indicating whether the token belongs to Sentence A or Sentence B (crucial for NSP).
3.  **Position Embeddings:** Unlike the original Transformer's sine/cosine functions, BERT uses *learned* positional embeddings for sequences up to 512 tokens.

### Special Tokens
*   `[CLS]`: Always the very first token in the sequence. Its final hidden state is used as the aggregate sequence representation for classification tasks.
*   `[SEP]`: Used to separate two sentences.
*   `[MASK]`: Used during pre-training to hide a token.

### The Encoder Stack
The sequence flows through $L$ identical Transformer encoder layers. Because there is no causal mask (unlike GPT), every token explicitly attends to every other token in the 512-length context window at every layer via Multi-Head Self-Attention.

## 7. Mathematical Foundations

**Masked Language Modeling (MLM):**
For a sequence $X = (x_1, \dots, x_T)$, we create a corrupted version $\hat{X}$ by masking a subset of tokens $M$. The training objective minimizes the negative log-likelihood of the true tokens given the corrupted context:
$$ L_{MLM} = - \sum_{i \in M} \log P(x_i | \hat{X}; \theta) $$
The probability is calculated via a Softmax over the vocabulary applied to the final hidden state of the masked position.

*Masking Strategy:* Of the 15% chosen for masking:
*   80% are replaced with `[MASK]`.
*   10% are replaced with a random word (to force the model to rely on context, not just trust the input).
*   10% are left unchanged (to minimize the mismatch between pre-training and fine-tuning, where `[MASK]` never appears).

## 8. Forward Pass

### Pre-training
1.  A massive corpus of unlabeled text (Wikipedia + BookCorpus) is processed.
2.  Pairs of sentences are packed together, with 15% of tokens masked.
3.  The model processes them bidirectionally.
4.  Gradients are calculated from the MLM loss (predicting the masked tokens) and the NSP loss (predicting if the sentences are consecutive).

### Fine-Tuning
1.  The pre-trained weights are loaded.
2.  A small, task-specific layer is added on top. (e.g., for sentiment analysis, a simple Linear layer connects the `[CLS]` token output to a 2-class Softmax).
3.  The *entire* network is trained end-to-end on the labeled dataset for 2-4 epochs.

## 9. Training Process

*   **Pre-training Compute:** Training BERT-Large took 4 days on 64 Cloud TPUs.
*   **Optimizer:** Adam with weight decay.
*   **The Fine-Tuning Revolution:** Before BERT, standard practice was to train task-specific architectures (LSTMs/CNNs) from scratch on task-specific data. BERT proved that pre-training a massive model once, and fine-tuning it quickly for specific tasks, yielded vastly superior results.

## 10. Complexity Analysis

| Model | Parameters | Attention Complexity | Context Limit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| BERT-Base | 110M | $O(T^2 \cdot H)$ | 512 |
| BERT-Large | 340M | $O(T^2 \cdot H)$ | 512 |

**Insight:** Because BERT relies on dense bidirectional attention over the entire sequence, it is constrained to a relatively short context window of 512 tokens. Extending this requires quadratic increases in memory.

## 11. Advantages

*   **Deep Bidirectionality:** Understands context from both left and right simultaneously, crucial for tasks like Named Entity Recognition and Question Answering.
*   **Universal Representation:** The same pre-trained model can be used for classification, token tagging, and question answering with minimal architectural changes.
*   **Open Source:** Google released the pre-trained weights, democratizing access to massive NLP capabilities.

## 12. Limitations

*   **Not Generative:** Because it relies on bidirectional context, BERT cannot easily generate text autoregressively (unlike GPT). You cannot ask BERT to "write a story."
*   **Pre-training / Fine-tuning Discrepancy:** The `[MASK]` token never appears during fine-tuning, creating a slight domain shift.
*   **Inefficient Pre-training:** Because only 15% of tokens are predicted per batch, it requires many more training steps to converge compared to autoregressive models that predict *every* token.

## 13. Real World Applications

*   **Google Search:** Integrated into the core search algorithm in 2019 to better understand the contextual meaning of complex queries.
*   **Information Extraction:** The industry standard for Named Entity Recognition (NER), sentiment analysis, and document classification.

## 14. Interview Questions

1.  **Why does BERT use Masked Language Modeling instead of Next Word Prediction?**
    *   *Next Word Prediction is inherently unidirectional (left-to-right). To train a deeply bidirectional model without letting the model "cheat" by looking ahead, you must hide random tokens and force it to use both left and right context.*
2.  **What is the role of the `[CLS]` token?**
    *   *It is the first token in the sequence. Because self-attention allows it to aggregate information from every other token, its final hidden state acts as a summarized representation of the entire sequence, used for classification tasks.*
3.  **Explain the 80/10/10 masking rule in BERT.**
    *   *Of the 15% chosen to be masked: 80% are replaced with `[MASK]`, 10% with a random word, and 10% kept the same. This mitigates the discrepancy between pre-training (where `[MASK]` exists) and fine-tuning (where it doesn't).*
