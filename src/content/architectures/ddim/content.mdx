# DDIM

## 1. Overview
Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM), introduced in 2020 by Jiaming Song, Chenlin Meng, and Stefano Ermon (Stanford University), solved the single largest operational bottleneck of DDPM: **prohibitively slow inference speed**. While DDPM required $T=1000$ sequential reverse Markov steps to generate a single image, DDIM demonstrated that the forward noise addition process does NOT need to be Markovian. By constructing a family of **Non-Markovian Forward Processes** that share the exact same marginal probability distributions $q(x_t \mid x_0)$ and objective function $\mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}}$ as DDPM, DDIM enables deterministic sampling. This allows DDIM to skip steps during inference, sampling high-quality images in just **10 to 50 steps ($10\times$ to $50\times$ faster)** without retraining the underlying U-Net model. Furthermore, DDIM's deterministic ODE formulation enables **consistent latent space interpolation** and image inversion.

:::tip
DDIM's core breakthrough: "DDPM's trained U-Net can be sampled deterministically using a non-Markovian ODE formulation, allowing models to generate samples in 20 steps instead of 1000 steps without retraining."
:::

## 2. Historical Context
In 2020, DDPM demonstrated that diffusion models could equal or surpass GANs in sample quality. However, DDPM's reverse sampling process was strictly Markovian: generating $x_{t-1}$ required $x_t$, forcing users to run 1,000 sequential U-Net forward passes per image. This made real-time generation, interactive editing, and large-scale deployment impossible.

Jiaming Song et al. made a profound theoretical observation: DDPM's training objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}} = \|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\|^2$ depends ONLY on the marginal distributions $q(x_t \mid x_0)$, NOT on the joint distribution $q(x_1, \dots, x_T \mid x_0)$. As long as a new forward process maintains the same marginals $q(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} x_0, (1 - \bar{\alpha}_t) \mathbf{I})$, ANY trained DDPM model can be sampled using a non-Markovian reverse process. By setting the stochastic noise parameter $\sigma_t = 0$, the reverse process becomes completely deterministic, transforming diffusion into a Neural Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE).

## 3. Problem It Solves
DDIM addresses three major challenges of standard diffusion models:
1. **$1000$-Step Inference Latency:** Reduces sampling iterations from 1000 steps down to 20–50 steps, accelerating inference speed by $20\times$ to $50\times$.
2. **Stochastic Sampling Variance:** In DDPM, passing the exact same noise vector through two different runs produces completely different images due to random Gaussian noise added at each step. DDIM's deterministic trajectories ensure that a fixed initial noise $x_T$ maps deterministically to a unique final image $x_0$.
3. **Semantically Meaningful Latent Interpolation:** Enables smooth semantic interpolation between two real images by inverting images into initial noise representations via the forward ODE trajectory.

## 5. Layer-by-Layer Breakdown
DDIM uses the exact same U-Net architecture as DDPM without any structural changes or retraining:

**1. Time-Conditioned U-Net (Pre-Trained DDPM):**
- Takes input noisy image $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 3 \times H \times W}$ and timestep $t \in [1, T]$.
- Outputs predicted noise tensor $\hat{\epsilon} = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 3 \times H \times W}$.

**2. Deterministic DDIM Sampler Module:**
- **Sub-sampled Timestep Sequence:** Defines a shortened trajectory $\tau = [\tau_1, \tau_2, \dots, \tau_S]$ of length $S \ll T$ (e.g., $S=20$ steps out of $T=1000$).
- **Clean Image Estimation:** Computes estimated clean image $\hat{x}_0(x_t)$ at each step.
- **Directional Gradient Update:** Computes the deterministic update vector pointing toward $x_{\tau_{i-1}}$ without adding random Gaussian noise.

## 6. Tensor Flow Walkthrough
Let's trace tensors through DDIM deterministic sampling for a sub-sampled trajectory $S=20$ steps and batch size $B=2$.

1. **Initialize Initial Noise:**
   - Sample $x_{\tau_S} = x_{1000} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$: `(2, 3, 64, 64)`
2. **Loop over Sub-sampled Timesteps $\tau = [1000, 950, 900, \dots, 50, 0]$:**
   - For step $i = S$ down to $1$ ($t = \tau_i, t_{\text{prev}} = \tau_{i-1}$):
     - Predict Noise: $\hat{\epsilon} = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) \rightarrow$ `(2, 3, 64, 64)`
     - Estimate Clean Image $\hat{x}_0 = \frac{x_t - \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} \hat{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}} \rightarrow$ `(2, 3, 64, 64)`
     - Compute Deterministic $x_{t_{\text{prev}}}$:
       $$x_{t_{\text{prev}}} = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t_{\text{prev}}}} \hat{x}_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_{t_{\text{prev}}}} \hat{\epsilon} \rightarrow \text{`(2, 3, 64, 64)`}$$
3. **Final Output:**
   - Output Image $x_0$: `(2, 3, 64, 64)` produced in 20 steps!

## 7. Mathematical Foundations

### 1. Non-Markovian Forward Process
DDIM defines a family of forward processes $q_\sigma(x_{1:T} \mid x_0)$ parameterized by $\sigma$:

$$q_\sigma(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, x_0) = \mathcal{N}\left(x_{t-1}; \; \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}} x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_{t-1} - \sigma_t^2} \cdot \frac{x_t - \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} x_0}{\sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}}, \; \sigma_t^2 \mathbf{I}\right)$$

When $\sigma_t^2 = \frac{1 - \bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} (1 - \frac{\bar{\alpha}_t}{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}})$, the process becomes standard Markovian DDPM.

### 2. DDIM Deterministic Sampling Update Rule ($\sigma_t = 0$)
When $\sigma_t = 0$ for all $t$, the random noise term is eliminated, yielding a **fully deterministic** reverse process:

First, estimate the clean image $\hat{x}_0$ from $x_t$ and predicted noise $\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)$:

$$\hat{x}_0(x_t) = \frac{x_t - \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}}$$

Next, compute $x_{t_{\text{prev}}}$ for any prior timestep $t_{\text{prev}} < t$:

$$x_{t_{\text{prev}}} = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t_{\text{prev}}}} \cdot \underbrace{\hat{x}_0(x_t)}_{\text{"Predicted } x_0\text{"}} + \underbrace{\sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_{t_{\text{prev}}} - \sigma_t^2} \cdot \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)}_{\text{"Direction pointing to } x_t\text{"}} + \underbrace{\sigma_t \epsilon_t}_{\text{"Random Noise"}}$$

Setting $\sigma_t = 0$ yields the **DDIM Deterministic Update Rule**:

$$x_{t_{\text{prev}}} = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t_{\text{prev}}}} \left( \frac{x_t - \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}} \right) + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_{t_{\text{prev}}}} \cdot \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)$$

This rule allows skipping timesteps arbitrarily (e.g., $t=1000 \rightarrow t_{\text{prev}}=950$).

## 8. Training Procedure
- **Zero Retraining Required:** DDIM requires **NO retraining**. It directly consumes any model trained using DDPM's standard $\mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}}$ objective.
- **Inference Time Schedule:** At inference, select a sub-sampled schedule of $S$ steps out of $T$ total steps (e.g., uniform stride $T/S$).

## 9. PyTorch Implementation
Here is a complete PyTorch implementation of the **DDIM Deterministic Sampler**.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

class DDIMSampler(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, model, timesteps=1000, beta_start=1e-4, beta_end=0.02):
        super().__init__()
        self.model = model
        self.timesteps = timesteps
        
        # Pre-calculate Alphas
        betas = torch.linspace(beta_start, beta_end, timesteps)
        alphas = 1.0 - betas
        alphas_cumprod = torch.cumprod(alphas, dim=0)
        
        self.register_buffer('alphas_cumprod', alphas_cumprod)

    @torch.no_grad()
    def ddim_sample(self, shape, num_ddim_steps=20, eta=0.0):
        """
        eta = 0.0 -> Deterministic DDIM
        eta = 1.0 -> Stochastic DDPM
        """
        device = self.alphas_cumprod.device
        B = shape[0]
        
        # Define sub-sampled timestep sequence (e.g., 20 steps out of 1000)
        c = self.timesteps // num_ddim_steps
        ddim_timesteps = torch.arange(0, self.timesteps, c, device=device)
        ddim_timesteps = torch.flip(ddim_timesteps, dims=[0]) # [950, 900, ..., 0]
        
        # Start from pure Gaussian noise x_T
        img = torch.randn(shape, device=device)
        
        for i in range(len(ddim_timesteps)):
            t = ddim_timesteps[i]
            prev_t = ddim_timesteps[i + 1] if i < len(ddim_timesteps) - 1 else torch.tensor(-1, device=device)
            
            t_batch = torch.full((B,), t, device=device, dtype=torch.long)
            
            # Predict noise
            eps_pred = self.model(img, t_batch)
            
            # Extract alpha values
            alpha_bar_t = self.alphas_cumprod[t]
            alpha_bar_prev = self.alphas_cumprod[prev_t] if prev_t >= 0 else torch.tensor(1.0, device=device)
            
            # 1. Estimate clean x0
            pred_x0 = (img - torch.sqrt(1.0 - alpha_bar_t) * eps_pred) / torch.sqrt(alpha_bar_t)
            
            # 2. Compute sigma_t (eta = 0 for deterministic)
            sigma_t = eta * torch.sqrt((1 - alpha_bar_prev) / (1 - alpha_bar_t) * (1 - alpha_bar_t / alpha_bar_prev))
            
            # 3. Compute direction to x_t
            dir_xt = torch.sqrt(1.0 - alpha_bar_prev - sigma_t**2) * eps_pred
            
            # 4. Compute x_{t_prev}
            noise = torch.randn_like(img) if eta > 0 else 0.0
            img = torch.sqrt(alpha_bar_prev) * pred_x0 + dir_xt + sigma_t * noise
            
        return img

if __name__ == "__main__":
    from ddpm import MiniUNet # Reusing MiniUNet from DDPM
    
    unet = MiniUNet(in_channels=3, time_dim=128)
    sampler = DDIMSampler(model=unet, timesteps=1000)
    
    # Generate 2 images in 20 steps
    sampled_imgs = sampler.ddim_sample(shape=(2, 3, 32, 32), num_ddim_steps=20, eta=0.0)
    
    print(f"Generated DDIM Images Shape: {sampled_imgs.shape}")
    assert sampled_imgs.shape == (2, 3, 32, 32)
    print("DDIM Sampler verification successful!")
```

## 10. Strengths
- **Fast Sampling ($10\times$ to $50\times$ Speedup):** Generates high-quality images in 20–50 steps vs. 1000 steps in DDPM.
- **Zero Retraining Required:** Direct plug-and-play sampler for any pre-trained DDPM checkpoint.
- **Deterministic & Invertible:** Identical initial noise vector yields the exact same image; enables ODE-based image inversion and latent interpolation.

## 11. Weaknesses
- **Slight Quality Drop at Ultra-Low Steps ($<10$ steps):** Quality degrades if step count drops below 10 steps (later solved by Consistency Models and Rectified Flow).

## 12. Research Evolution
DDIM established deterministic ODE diffusion sampling, enabling latent editing and inversion. It directly inspired **PLMS**, **DPM-Solver**, and latent space text-to-image models (Stable Diffusion).

## 13. Interview Questions

**Q1: Why does DDIM NOT require retraining the DDPM U-Net model?**
**A1:** DDPM's training objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}} = \|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\|^2$ depends ONLY on the marginal distributions $q(x_t \mid x_0)$, not on the joint distribution. DDIM defines a non-Markovian forward process that shares the exact same marginals $q(x_t \mid x_0)$ as DDPM, allowing any pre-trained DDPM U-Net to be sampled using DDIM rules.

**Q2: What parameter controls the trade-off between deterministic DDIM sampling and stochastic DDPM sampling?**
**A2:** The parameter $\eta \ge 0$. When $\eta = 0$, the process is fully deterministic DDIM ($\sigma_t = 0$). When $\eta = 1$, the process recovers standard stochastic Markovian DDPM.

**Q3: How does DDIM enable smooth latent space interpolation between two real images?**
**A3:** Because DDIM's reverse process is a deterministic ODE ($\eta=0$), it is fully invertible. A real image $x_0$ can be inverted back to its exact initial noise vector $x_T$ via forward ODE integration. Spherical linear interpolation (SLERP) between two inverted noise vectors $x_T^{(1)}$ and $x_T^{(2)}$ yields smooth, realistic image interpolations.

**Q4: Write out the predicted clean image $\hat{x}_0$ equation used in DDIM.**
**A4:** $\hat{x}_0(x_t) = \frac{x_t - \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}}$.

**Q5: Compare DDPM and DDIM regarding sampling speed.**
**A5:** DDPM requires evaluating all $T=1000$ sequential steps. DDIM can sub-sample the trajectory to $S=20$ or $50$ steps, speeding up inference by $20\times$ to $50\times$ with negligible quality loss.
