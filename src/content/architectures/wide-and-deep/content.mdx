# Wide & Deep

## 1. Overview
Wide & Deep Learning, introduced in 2016 by Heng-Tze Cheng et al. (Google Inc.), solved a fundamental trade-off in production recommendation systems: **Memorization versus Generalization**. Memorization (achieved via linear models over sparse cross-product feature transformations) learns historical rules from past user interactions (e.g., "user installed Netflix AND query is 'movies' $\implies$ recommend Netflix app"). Generalization (achieved via deep neural networks over dense embeddings) explores new, unseen feature combinations (e.g., "user likes sci-fi $\implies$ recommend Star Wars app"). Before Wide & Deep, recommendation systems used either purely linear models (lacking generalization) or purely deep MLPs (tending to over-recommend unrelated items). Heng-Tze Cheng et al. combined both paradigms into a **Jointly Trained Dual-Branch Architecture**: a **Wide Linear Component** (memorization) and a **Deep Feed-Forward Component** (generalization), serving over 1 Billion users on the Google Play Store.

:::note
Wide & Deep concept: "Combine a Wide Linear Model (memorizing specific feature interactions) and a Deep MLP (generalizing via dense embeddings) jointly trained using a single combined loss function."
:::

## 2. Historical Context
In 2016, large-scale industrial recommendation engines (such as Google Play App Store) relied heavily on generalized linear models (logistic regression) over sparse one-hot features. Engineers manually created thousands of cross-product feature transformations (e.g., `user_installed_app="Netflix" AND item_app="Hulu"`). While effective, manual feature engineering required massive human effort and failed to generalize to unobserved feature pairs.

Conversely, deep neural networks (MLPs) mapped sparse features to dense continuous embeddings. However, deep networks suffered when user intent was hyper-specific (e.g., searching for a specific niche app title)—the deep embedding layers smoothed out niche signals, causing irrelevant recommendations.

Cheng et al. proved that combining a Wide linear model and a Deep MLP trained jointly via backpropagation provided the ideal production solution.

## 3. Problem It Solves
Wide & Deep resolves two opposing failure modes in recommendation algorithms:
1. **Lack of Generalization in Linear Models:** Linear models cannot recommend items that do not appear together in the training dataset. Deep embeddings generalize to unseen item pairs based on latent similarity.
2. **Over-Generalization (Smoothing Out Niche Intent) in Deep MLPs:** Deep MLPs struggle to memorize exact, highly specific user-query associations. The Wide branch acts as a fast-path direct memory lookup.
3. **Manual Feature Engineering Overhead:** Reduces the need to hand-craft millions of higher-order feature combinations by allowing the Deep branch to automatically learn non-linear feature interactions.

## 5. Layer-by-Layer Breakdown

**1. Wide Component (Linear Model Branch):**
- Inputs: Raw sparse categorical features and cross-product transformations $\phi_k(x) = \prod_{j=1}^d x_j^{c_{kj}}, c_{kj} \in \{0, 1\}$.
- Output: Single linear projection scalar $y_{\text{wide}} = \mathbf{w}_{\text{wide}}^T [\mathbf{x}, \phi(\mathbf{x})] + b_{\text{wide}}$.

**2. Deep Component (Neural Network Branch):**
- **Embedding Layer:** Categorical sparse IDs map to dense continuous embeddings $e_i \in \mathbb{R}^k$.
- **Concatenation Layer:** Concatenates dense numerical features $x_{\text{dense}}$ with categorical embeddings:
  $$a^{(0)} = \left[ e_1^T, e_2^T, \dots, e_m^T, x_{\text{dense}}^T \right]^T$$
- **Deep MLP Stack:** 3 Fully Connected layers ($1024 \to 512 \to 256$ units) with ReLU activations.
- Output: Single linear scalar $y_{\text{deep}} = \mathbf{w}_{\text{deep}}^T a^{(L)} + b_{\text{deep}}$.

**3. Joint Prediction Layer:**
- Combines Wide and Deep scalar logits:
  $$P(Y=1 \mid \mathbf{x}) = \sigma\left( \mathbf{w}_{\text{wide}}^T [\mathbf{x}, \phi(\mathbf{x})] + \mathbf{w}_{\text{deep}}^T a^{(L)} + b \right)$$

## 6. Tensor Flow Walkthrough
Let's trace tensors through Wide & Deep for mini-batch $B=512$, 4 categorical features (Embedding dim $k=32$), 5 continuous numerical features, 10 cross-product wide features.

1. **Wide Branch Input:**
   - Wide Sparse/Cross Features: `(512, 10)` $\xrightarrow{\text{Linear Projection}} y_{\text{wide}}$: `(512, 1)`
2. **Deep Branch Input & Embedding:**
   - 4 Categoricals $\xrightarrow{\text{Embedding Lookup}} 4 \times (512, 32) \to$ Concat Embeddings: `(512, 128)`
   - Concat 5 Continuous Features $\to a^{(0)}$: `(512, 133)`
3. **Deep MLP Forward Pass:**
   - Layer 1 (1024): `(512, 1024)` $\to$ Layer 2 (512): `(512, 512)` $\to$ Layer 3 (256): `(512, 256)`
   - Output Projection $\to y_{\text{deep}}$: `(512, 1)`
4. **Joint Combined Output:**
   - Logits $= y_{\text{wide}} + y_{\text{deep}} \to (512, 1) \xrightarrow{\text{Sigmoid}} P(\text{Click})$: `(512, 1)`.

## 7. Mathematical Foundations

### 1. Cross-Product Transformation Formula
Let $x_j \in \{0, 1\}$ be the $j$-th feature indicator. The $k$-th cross-product transformation $\phi_k(x)$ is defined as:

$$\phi_k(x) = \prod_{j=1}^d x_j^{c_{kj}}, \quad c_{kj} \in \{0, 1\}$$

For example, if the feature is `gender="female"` and `impression_app="Netflix"`, $\phi_k(x) = 1$ if and only if both conditions are true simultaneously.

### 2. Joint Wide & Deep Prediction Equation
The combined logistic regression objective function is:

$$P(Y = 1 \mid \mathbf{x}) = \sigma \left( \mathbf{w}_{\text{wide}}^T [\mathbf{x}, \phi(\mathbf{x})] + \mathbf{w}_{\text{deep}}^T a^{(L)} + b \right)$$

### 3. Joint Optimization vs. Ensemble Training
Wide & Deep is **jointly trained** in a single backward pass, distinct from an ensemble:
- In an **Ensemble**, individual models are trained independently without knowledge of each other.
- In **Joint Training**, the Wide component only needs to complement the weaknesses of the Deep component (handling niche exceptions), reducing the required size of the Wide model.

Wide weights are optimized via FTRL-Proximal (Follow-The-Regularized-Leader) with $L_1$ regularization for sparsity, while Deep weights are optimized via AdaGrad or Adam.

## 8. Training Procedure
- **Data Preprocessing:** Extract sparse categorical IDs, generate cross-product features for Wide branch, normalize continuous features for Deep branch.
- **Joint Optimization:** Execute forward pass through both branches simultaneously. Compute Binary Cross-Entropy loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(y, \hat{y})$.
- Optimizers: Apply **FTRL** to Wide branch parameters (for $L_1$ weight sparsity) and **Adam** to Deep branch parameters.

## 9. PyTorch Implementation
Here is a complete PyTorch implementation of the **Wide & Deep Architecture**.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

class WideAndDeep(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, wide_dim, num_categorical_features, vocab_sizes, embed_dim=32, num_dense_features=5):
        super().__init__()
        # 1. WIDE BRANCH
        self.wide_linear = nn.Linear(wide_dim, 1, bias=False)
        
        # 2. DEEP BRANCH
        # Categorical Embedding Tables
        self.embeddings = nn.ModuleList([
            nn.Embedding(vocab_size, embed_dim) for vocab_size in vocab_sizes
        ])
        
        total_deep_in = num_categorical_features * embed_dim + num_dense_features
        self.deep_mlp = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(total_deep_in, 1024),
            nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(1024, 512),
            nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(512, 256),
            nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(256, 1)
        )
        
        self.bias = nn.Parameter(torch.zeros(1))

    def forward(self, wide_inputs, cat_inputs, dense_inputs):
        """
        wide_inputs: (B, wide_dim)
        cat_inputs:  (B, num_categorical_features)
        dense_inputs:(B, num_dense_features)
        """
        # Wide Branch
        y_wide = self.wide_linear(wide_inputs) # (B, 1)
        
        # Deep Branch: Lookup and Concatenate Embeddings
        embedded = [self.embeddings[i](cat_inputs[:, i]) for i in range(cat_inputs.shape[1])]
        deep_in = torch.cat(embedded + [dense_inputs], dim=-1) # (B, total_deep_in)
        y_deep = self.deep_mlp(deep_in) # (B, 1)
        
        # Joint Combination
        logits = y_wide + y_deep + self.bias
        probs = torch.sigmoid(logits)
        return probs

if __name__ == "__main__":
    B = 512
    wide_dim = 10
    vocab_sizes = [1000, 5000, 200, 50]
    num_dense = 5
    
    model = WideAndDeep(wide_dim=wide_dim, num_categorical_features=4, vocab_sizes=vocab_sizes, num_dense_features=num_dense)
    
    wide_x = torch.randn(B, wide_dim)
    cat_x = torch.cat([torch.randint(0, v, (B, 1)) for v in vocab_sizes], dim=1)
    dense_x = torch.randn(B, num_dense)
    
    pred = model(wide_x, cat_x, dense_x)
    
    print(f"Wide & Deep Prediction Output Shape: {pred.shape}")
    assert pred.shape == (B, 1)
    print("Wide & Deep verification successful!")
```

## 10. Strengths
- **Combines Memorization & Generalization:** Balances direct memory rule lookup with dense embedding generalization.
- **Production Proven:** Serves over 1 Billion users in Google Play with high click-through-rate (CTR) gains.
- **Flexible Dual Optimization:** Allows using FTRL ($L_1$ sparse) for Wide and Adam for Deep.

## 11. Weaknesses
- **Manual Feature Engineering for Wide Part:** Requires domain experts to manually define cross-product transformation rules for the Wide branch (addressed by DeepFM).

## 12. Research Evolution
Wide & Deep established dual-branch recommendation architectures, directly inspiring **DeepFM**, **xDeepFM**, and **DLRM**.

## 13. Interview Questions

**Q1: What is the main difference between "Memorization" and "Generalization" in recommendation systems?**
**A1:** Memorization is the ability to learn historical correlation rules directly from frequent sparse feature co-occurrences (achieved via linear models over cross-product features). Generalization is the ability to explore unseen feature combinations based on latent vector similarity (achieved via deep neural network embeddings).

**Q2: Why does Wide & Deep use Joint Training instead of an Ensemble of Wide and Deep models?**
**A2:** In an Ensemble, individual models are trained independently without communication, requiring a large Wide model. In Joint Training, gradients flow back simultaneously through both branches; the Wide model only needs to learn niche exceptions that the Deep model struggles with, resulting in a much smaller Wide component.

**Q3: Why is FTRL (Follow-The-Regularized-Leader) optimizer used for the Wide branch?**
**A3:** FTRL enforces strong $L_1$ regularization, forcing most wide linear weights to exactly 0.0. This yields a sparse linear model that fits efficiently in RAM for real-time latency-critical serving.

**Q4: Write out the combined Wide & Deep prediction equation.**
**A4:** $P(Y=1 \mid \mathbf{x}) = \sigma\left( \mathbf{w}_{\text{wide}}^T [\mathbf{x}, \phi(\mathbf{x})] + \mathbf{w}_{\text{deep}}^T a^{(L)} + b \right)$.

**Q5: What major limitation of Wide & Deep led to the development of DeepFM?**
**A5:** Wide & Deep still requires domain experts to manually engineer cross-product feature transformations $\phi(\mathbf{x})$ for the Wide branch. DeepFM replaced the Wide linear model with a Factorization Machine (FM) layer, automating feature interaction learning.
