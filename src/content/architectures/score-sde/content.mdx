# Score SDE

## 1. Overview
Score-based Stochastic Differential Equations (Score SDE), introduced in 2020 by Yang Song, Jascha Sohl-Dickstein, Diederik P. Kingma, Abhishek Kumar, Stefano Ermon, and Ben Poole (Stanford University & Google Brain), established a unified mathematical framework for diffusion models using continuous-time Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs). Before Score SDE, diffusion models (DDPM) and score-based generative models (SGM / SDEdit) were viewed as distinct algorithms. Song et al. proved that both DDPM and SGM are discretization instances of a continuous-time SDE that gradually transforms a data distribution into noise. Furthermore, for every forward SDE, there exists a corresponding deterministic **Probability Flow ODE** that shares the exact same marginal probability distributions. This formulation enabled numerical SDE/ODE solvers (like Predictor-Corrector and RK45), exact likelihood computation via instantaneous change-of-variables, and high-fidelity image synthesis.

:::note
Score SDE's core unification: "Model noise corruption as a continuous-time Stochastic Differential Equation $dx = f(x,t)dt + g(t)dw$, and generate data by reversing the SDE using estimated score functions $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$."
:::

## 2. Historical Context
In 2020, two independent lines of generative modeling research converged:
1. **Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM - Ho et al.):** Modeled generation via discrete-time Markov chains adding Gaussian noise step-by-step.
2. **Score-Based Generative Models (SGM - Song & Ermon, 2019):** Perturbed data with noise at multiple scales and trained a single Neural Network (Score Network) to estimate the score function $\nabla_x \log p_\sigma(x)$ via denoising score matching.

Yang Song et al. unified both paradigms under continuous-time SDE calculus. They showed that as the number of timesteps $T \to \infty$, discrete noise addition becomes a continuous SDE $dx = f(x,t)dt + g(t)dw$. DDPM corresponds to the **Variance Preserving SDE (VP-SDE)**, while SGM corresponds to the **Variance Exploding SDE (VE-SDE)**. This mathematical bridge unlocked black-box adaptive differential equation solvers for diffusion models.

## 3. Problem It Solves
Score SDE addresses three fundamental mathematical limitations of discrete diffusion models:
1. **Discrete Timestep Discretization Errors:** Eliminates fixed discrete step schedules ($T=1000$). Continuous-time formulation allows applying high-order adaptive numerical ODE/SDE solvers (e.g., Runge-Kutta 45) to trade off speed vs. precision dynamically.
2. **Fractured Model Theories:** Unifies score matching (SGM) and denoising diffusion (DDPM) into a single theoretical framework.
3. **Inability to Compute Exact Data Likelihoods:** Enables exact log-likelihood evaluation ($\log p(x)$) of generated images using the deterministic Probability Flow ODE and the Instantaneous Change-of-Variables formula.

## 5. Layer-by-Layer Breakdown
Score SDE uses a time-conditioned score network $s_\theta(x, t)$ based on U-Net or NCSNpp (Noise Conditional Score Network):

**1. Continuous Time Conditioning:**
- Continuous time $t \in [\epsilon, T]$ is embedded using Gaussian Fourier Projection embeddings:
  $$\text{Fourier}(t) = [\sin(2\pi \omega_i t), \cos(2\pi \omega_i t)]$$
- Passed through dense linear layers to produce per-block scale and shift parameters.

**2. NCSNpp / Refined U-Net Architecture:**
- **Encoder:** Residual blocks with Deformable Convolutions, Multi-Head Self-Attention, and progressive downsampling.
- **Decoder:** Residual blocks with skip connections and upsampling.
- **Output:** Score prediction matching the spatial dimensions of input $x$, estimating $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$.

**3. Predictor-Corrector (PC) Sampler:**
- **Predictor Step:** Advances time using a discretization of the Reverse SDE (e.g., Reverse Euler-Maruyama).
- **Corrector Step:** Refines the sample at the current time $t$ using Langevin MCMC iterations guided by $s_\theta(x, t)$.

## 7. Mathematical Foundations

### 1. Continuous Forward SDE
A continuous noise process is governed by the Ito SDE:

$$dx = f(x, t) dt + g(t) dw$$

where $f(x, t)$ is the drift coefficient, $g(t)$ is the diffusion coefficient, and $w$ is standard Brownian motion.

- **Variance Exploding SDE (VE-SDE / SGM):** $dx = \sqrt{\frac{d[\sigma^2(t)]}{dt}} dw$  (Noise variance explodes to infinity).
- **Variance Preserving SDE (VP-SDE / DDPM):** $dx = -\frac{1}{2} \beta(t) x dt + \sqrt{\beta(t)} dw$ (Variance is bounded to 1).

### 2. Reverse-Time SDE & Score Function
Anderson's theorem proves that the reverse of an Ito SDE is also an SDE running backward in time from $t=T$ to $t=0$:

$$dx = \left[ f(x, t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x) \right] dt + g(t) d\bar{w}$$

where $d\bar{w}$ is backward Brownian motion, and $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ is the **Score Function** (the gradient of log data density with respect to $x$).

### 3. Probability Flow ODE
For every forward SDE, there exists a deterministic Ordinary Differential Equation (Probability Flow ODE) whose trajectories share the exact same marginal probability densities $p_t(x)$ at all timesteps $t$:

$$dx = \left[ f(x, t) - \frac{1}{2} g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x) \right] dt$$

### 4. Denoising Score Matching Loss Objective
The score network $s_\theta(x, t)$ is trained using Continuous Denoising Score Matching:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{ScoreSDE}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t \sim U(0, T)} \left[ \lambda(t) \, \mathbb{E}_{x_0, x_t} \left[ \left\| s_\theta(x_t, t) - \nabla_{x_t} \log q_{0t}(x_t \mid x_0) \right\|^2 \right] \right]$$

## 8. Training Procedure
- Sample continuous timesteps $t \sim \text{Uniform}(\epsilon, T)$.
- Sample noise $x_t \sim q_{0t}(x_t \mid x_0)$ using SDE transition kernels.
- Compute score target $\nabla_{x_t} \log q_{0t}(x_t \mid x_0) = -\frac{x_t - \mu_t}{\sigma_t^2}$.
- Minimize weighted $\ell_2$ loss between predicted score $s_\theta(x_t, t)$ and true target.
- Optimizer: Adam ($\text{lr} = 2 \times 10^{-4}$).

## 9. PyTorch Implementation
Here is a complete PyTorch implementation of the **Score SDE Loss & VP-SDE Noise Sampler**.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F
import math

class VPSDE(nn.Module):
    """ Variance Preserving SDE (Continuous DDPM) """
    def __init__(self, beta_min=0.1, beta_max=20.0):
        super().__init__()
        self.beta_min = beta_min
        self.beta_max = beta_max

    def sde(self, x, t):
        # Drift f(x,t) and Diffusion g(t)
        beta_t = self.beta_min + t * (self.beta_max - self.beta_min)
        drift = -0.5 * beta_t[:, None, None, None] * x
        diffusion = torch.sqrt(beta_t)
        return drift, diffusion

    def marginal_prob(self, x_0, t):
        # Closed form mean and std at continuous time t
        log_mean_coeff = -0.25 * t**2 * (self.beta_max - self.beta_min) - 0.5 * t * self.beta_min
        mean = torch.exp(log_mean_coeff)[:, None, None, None] * x_0
        std = torch.sqrt(1.0 - torch.exp(2.0 * log_mean_coeff))[:, None, None, None]
        return mean, std

class ScoreLoss(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, sde, score_model):
        super().__init__()
        self.sde = sde
        self.score_model = score_model

    def forward(self, x_0, eps=1e-5):
        B = x_0.shape[0]
        # Sample continuous time t ~ Uniform(eps, 1.0)
        t = torch.rand(B, device=x_0.device) * (1.0 - eps) + eps
        
        noise = torch.randn_like(x_0)
        mean, std = self.sde.marginal_prob(x_0, t)
        x_t = mean + std * noise
        
        # Estimate score: grad_x log p_t(x) approx -noise / std
        predicted_score = self.score_model(x_t, t)
        target_score = -noise / std
        
        # Loss weighted by std^2
        loss = torch.square(predicted_score * std - (-noise))
        return loss.mean()

if __name__ == "__main__":
    from ddpm import MiniUNet
    
    B, C, H, W = 4, 3, 32, 32
    sde = VPSDE(beta_min=0.1, beta_max=20.0)
    score_net = MiniUNet(in_channels=C, time_dim=128)
    loss_fn = ScoreLoss(sde, score_net)
    
    dummy_x0 = torch.randn(B, C, H, W)
    loss = loss_fn(dummy_x0)
    
    print(f"Calculated Continuous Score SDE Loss: {loss.item():.4f}")
    assert not torch.isnan(loss)
    print("Score SDE verification successful!")
```

## 10. Strengths
- **Continuous-Time Unification:** Unifies DDPM and SGM under a single differential equation framework.
- **Exact Likelihood Computation:** Probability Flow ODE allows calculating exact data log-likelihood $\log p(x)$ via instantaneous change-of-variables.
- **Adaptive ODE/SDE Solvers:** Enables black-box numerical differential equation solvers (e.g., RK45) for sampling.

## 11. Weaknesses
- **Complex Stochastic Calculus:** Requires understanding Ito calculus, SDEs, and Langevin dynamics.
- **High Computational Load:** Predictor-Corrector samplers require running multiple Langevin MCMC steps per time increment.

## 12. Research Evolution
Score SDE established continuous-time diffusion theory, leading directly to **Flow Matching**, **Rectified Flows**, and **Consistency Models**.

## 13. Interview Questions

**Q1: What is the Score Function in Score SDE?**
**A1:** The Score Function is the spatial gradient of the log data probability density with respect to the data vector: $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$. It points in the direction of steepest increase in data density.

**Q2: What is the difference between VE-SDE and VP-SDE?**
**A2:** 
- **VE-SDE (Variance Exploding):** Keeps data unscaled ($f(x,t)=0$) and adds increasing noise variance ($\sigma(t) \to \infty$). Corresponds to Score-Based Generative Models (SGM).
- **VP-SDE (Variance Preserving):** Scales data down ($f(x,t) = -\frac{1}{2}\beta(t)x$) while adding noise, preserving variance to 1. Corresponds to DDPM.

**Q3: What is the Probability Flow ODE?**
**A3:** A deterministic ODE whose trajectories share the exact same marginal probability densities $p_t(x)$ as the stochastic Reverse SDE. It allows deterministic sampling and exact likelihood computation.

**Q4: What is a Predictor-Corrector (PC) Sampler in Score SDE?**
**A4:** A hybrid sampler where each time step executes a **Predictor** step (numerical Reverse SDE step) followed by several **Corrector** steps (Langevin MCMC iterations) to refine sample quality.

**Q5: How does Probability Flow ODE enable exact likelihood evaluation ($\log p(x)$)?**
**A5:** By applying the Instantaneous Change-of-Variables formula to the deterministic ODE: $\log p_0(x_0) = \log p_T(x_T) + \int_0^T \text{Tr}\left(\frac{\partial f_{\text{ODE}}}{\partial x}\right) dt$.
