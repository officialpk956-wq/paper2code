# SSD

## 1. Overview
SSD (Single Shot MultiBox Detector), introduced in 2015 by Wei Liu, Dragomir Anguelov, Dumitru Erhan, Christian Szegedy, Scott Reed, Cheng-Yang Fu, and Alexander C. Berg (UNC Chapel Hill & Google), was a landmark single-stage object detector. Published around the same time as YOLO v1, SSD surpassed both YOLO v1 and Faster R-CNN in combining high speed and accuracy. While YOLO v1 made predictions from a single coarse grid ($7 \times 7$), SSD introduced the key innovation of predicting bounding boxes and class scores across **multiple feature maps of decreasing spatial resolution** ($38 \times 38, 19 \times 19, 10 \times 10, 5 \times 5, 3 \times 3, 1 \times 1$). At each feature map level, SSD evaluates a set of pre-defined **Default Boxes** (anchors) with different aspect ratios and scales. Combined with small $3 \times 3$ convolutional filters applied directly to these multi-scale maps, SSD achieved 74.3% mAP on PASCAL VOC 2007 at 59 FPS on an NVIDIA Titan X, proving that single-stage detectors could match two-stage accuracy.

:::note
SSD's core breakthrough: "Predict multi-scale default bounding boxes across multiple intermediate feature maps of varying resolutions in a single forward pass, eliminating region proposal networks entirely."
:::

## 2. Historical Context
In 2015, object detection was sharply divided into two paradigms:
- **Two-Stage Detectors (Faster R-CNN):** Highly accurate but slow (~7–17 FPS) due to proposal generation, RoI pooling, and multi-stage heads.
- **Single-Stage Detectors (YOLO v1):** Real-time fast (45 FPS), but suffered from low accuracy (63.4% mAP) and severe failure on small objects because predictions were constrained to a single $7 \times 7$ feature grid.

Wei Liu et al. recognized that deep convolutional networks naturally construct an internal multi-scale feature hierarchy: early layers have high spatial resolution (great for small objects), while deeper layers have large receptive fields (great for large objects). SSD exploited this inherent hierarchy by attaching light $3 \times 3$ convolutional prediction heads to multiple intermediate layers of a VGG-16 backbone. This eliminated the trade-off between speed and multi-scale coverage, paving the way for modern multi-scale architectures like RetinaNet and FPN.

## 3. Problem It Solves
SSD solves two fundamental flaws of earlier object detection models:
1. **Single-Resolution Coarseness in YOLO v1:** YOLO v1 forced all predictions through a $7 \times 7$ grid, causing small objects to be swallowed by grid cells. SSD attaches detection heads to 6 feature maps ranging from $38 \times 38$ down to $1 \times 1$, enabling dense spatial detection of tiny to large objects.
2. **Proposal Dependency in Faster R-CNN:** Eliminates the Region Proposal Network (RPN) and RoI Pooling steps entirely. By evaluating default anchor boxes directly on feature maps using small $3 \times 3$ convolutions, SSD processes images in a single shot.

## 5. Layer-by-Layer Breakdown
SSD300 consists of a VGG-16 backbone, extra feature layers, and 6 multi-scale detection heads:

**1. Modified VGG-16 Backbone:**
- Converts VGG-16's `fc6` and `fc7` layers into dilated $3 \times 3$ and $1 \times 1$ convolutional layers (`Conv6` and `Conv7`).
- Changes `MaxPool5` from $2 \times 2$ (stride 2) to $3 \times 3$ (stride 1) with atrous convolution to preserve spatial resolution.
- **Conv4_3 Head:** Extracts high-resolution $38 \times 38 \times 512$ feature map for small object detection. Uses L2 Normalization because early feature magnitudes differ from deep layers.

**2. Auxiliary Multi-Scale Feature Layers:**
- **Conv8_2:** $1 \times 1$ conv followed by $3 \times 3$ stride 2 conv $\rightarrow (B, 512, 10, 10)$.
- **Conv9_2:** $1 \times 1$ conv followed by $3 \times 3$ stride 2 conv $\rightarrow (B, 256, 5, 5)$.
- **Conv10_2:** $1 \times 1$ conv followed by $3 \times 3$ padding 0 conv $\rightarrow (B, 256, 3, 3)$.
- **Conv11_2:** $1 \times 1$ conv followed by $3 \times 3$ padding 0 conv $\rightarrow (B, 256, 1, 1)$.

**3. Convolutional Detection Heads:**
- At each of the 6 feature maps, a small $3 \times 3$ convolution predicts $k$ default boxes per cell.
- For $C$ classes and $k$ default boxes, each head computes:
  - **Location Head:** $k \times 4$ offset parameters $(\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta w, \Delta h)$.
  - **Class Head:** $k \times C$ confidence scores.

## 6. Tensor Flow Walkthrough
Let's trace tensors for SSD300 with $C=21$ classes (20 PASCAL VOC + 1 background).

1. **Input Tensor:** `(B, 3, 300, 300)`
2. **Multi-Scale Feature Maps & Head Outputs:**
   - **Conv4_3** ($38 \times 38 \times 512$, $k=4$ boxes):
     - Loc: `(B, 4*4, 38, 38)` $\rightarrow$ `(B, 5776, 4)`
     - Conf: `(B, 4*21, 38, 38)` $\rightarrow$ `(B, 5776, 21)`
   - **Conv7** ($19 \times 19 \times 1024$, $k=6$ boxes):
     - Loc: `(B, 6*4, 19, 19)` $\rightarrow$ `(B, 2166, 4)`
     - Conf: `(B, 6*21, 19, 19)` $\rightarrow$ `(B, 2166, 21)`
   - **Conv8_2** ($10 \times 10 \times 512$, $k=6$ boxes):
     - Loc: `(B, 600, 4)`, Conf: `(B, 600, 21)`
   - **Conv9_2** ($5 \times 5 \times 256$, $k=6$ boxes):
     - Loc: `(B, 150, 4)`, Conf: `(B, 150, 21)`
   - **Conv10_2** ($3 \times 3 \times 256$, $k=4$ boxes):
     - Loc: `(B, 36, 4)`, Conf: `(B, 36, 21)`
   - **Conv11_2** ($1 \times 1 \times 256$, $k=4$ boxes):
     - Loc: `(B, 16, 4)`, Conf: `(B, 16, 21)`
3. **Total Prediction Concatenation:**
   - Total Default Boxes = $5776 + 2166 + 600 + 150 + 36 + 16 = \mathbf{8,732}$ boxes.
   - Concatenated Loc Shape: `(B, 8732, 4)`
   - Concatenated Conf Shape: `(B, 8732, 21)`

## 7. Mathematical Foundations

### 1. Default Box Scale and Aspect Ratio Derivation
For $m=6$ feature maps, the scale $s_k$ of default boxes for the $k$-th feature map is computed using a linear scale schedule between $s_{\text{min}} = 0.2$ and $s_{\text{max}} = 0.9$:

$$s_k = s_{\text{min}} + \frac{s_{\text{max}} - s_{\text{min}}}{m - 1} (k - 1), \quad k \in \{1, \dots, m\}$$

Aspect ratios $a_r \in \{1, 2, 3, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{3}\}$ define box width $w_k^a$ and height $h_k^a$:
$$w_k^a = s_k \sqrt{a_r}, \quad h_k^a = \frac{s_k}{\sqrt{a_r}}$$

For aspect ratio $a_r = 1$, an extra scale $s_k' = \sqrt{s_k s_{k+1}}$ is added, providing 6 default box shapes per grid cell.

### 2. Multi-Task Loss Function & Hard Negative Mining
The loss is a weighted sum of Softmax Cross-Entropy Confidence Loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{conf}}$ and Smooth $L_1$ Localization Loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{loc}}$:

$$\mathcal{L}(x, c, l, g) = \frac{1}{N} \left( \mathcal{L}_{\text{conf}}(x, c) + \alpha \mathcal{L}_{\text{loc}}(x, l, g) \right)$$

where $N$ is the number of matched positive default boxes ($N=0 \rightarrow \mathcal{L}=0$).

Because most default boxes are background, SSD uses **Hard Negative Mining**: it sorts negative default boxes by highest confidence loss and picks the top negatives to maintain a strict **3:1 negative-to-positive ratio**.

## 8. Training Procedure
- **Matching Strategy:** Default boxes are matched to ground-truth boxes with IoU overlap $\ge 0.5$.
- **Optimizer:** SGD with momentum 0.9, weight decay $5 \times 10^{-4}$, initial learning rate $10^{-3}$.
- **Data Augmentation:** Extensive data augmentation (random crops, color jittering, expansion/zoom-out to create small objects) is critical for high accuracy.

## 9. PyTorch Implementation
Here is a complete PyTorch implementation of the **SSD Multi-Scale Head Generator**.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

class SSDHead(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, num_classes=21):
        super().__init__()
        self.num_classes = num_classes
        # Number of default boxes per cell for 6 feature maps
        self.num_boxes = [4, 6, 6, 6, 4, 4]
        self.in_channels = [512, 1024, 512, 256, 256, 256]
        
        # Location and Confidence heads for 6 feature maps
        self.loc_heads = nn.ModuleList([
            nn.Conv2d(in_ch, n_box * 4, kernel_size=3, padding=1)
            for in_ch, n_box in zip(self.in_channels, self.num_boxes)
        ])
        self.conf_heads = nn.ModuleList([
            nn.Conv2d(in_ch, n_box * num_classes, kernel_size=3, padding=1)
            for in_ch, n_box in zip(self.in_channels, self.num_boxes)
        ])

    def forward(self, feature_maps):
        loc_preds = []
        conf_preds = []
        
        for feat, loc_head, conf_head in zip(feature_maps, self.loc_heads, self.conf_heads):
            B = feat.shape[0]
            # Predict location deltas: (B, num_boxes*4, H, W) -> (B, H*W*num_boxes, 4)
            loc = loc_head(feat).permute(0, 2, 3, 1).contiguous().view(B, -1, 4)
            # Predict confidences: (B, num_boxes*num_classes, H, W) -> (B, H*W*num_boxes, num_classes)
            conf = conf_head(feat).permute(0, 2, 3, 1).contiguous().view(B, -1, self.num_classes)
            
            loc_preds.append(loc)
            conf_preds.append(conf)
            
        # Concatenate predictions across all 6 feature maps
        loc_preds = torch.cat(loc_preds, dim=1)   # (B, 8732, 4)
        conf_preds = torch.cat(conf_preds, dim=1) # (B, 8732, 21)
        return loc_preds, conf_preds

if __name__ == "__main__":
    B = 2
    head = SSDHead(num_classes=21)
    
    # Dummy feature maps matching SSD300 resolutions
    f1 = torch.randn(B, 512, 38, 38)  # Conv4_3
    f2 = torch.randn(B, 1024, 19, 19) # Conv7
    f3 = torch.randn(B, 512, 10, 10)  # Conv8_2
    f4 = torch.randn(B, 256, 5, 5)    # Conv9_2
    f5 = torch.randn(B, 256, 3, 3)    # Conv10_2
    f6 = torch.randn(B, 256, 1, 1)    # Conv11_2
    
    loc_preds, conf_preds = head([f1, f2, f3, f4, f5, f6])
    
    print(f"Total Bounding Box Loc Shape:  {loc_preds.shape}")
    print(f"Total Bounding Box Conf Shape: {conf_preds.shape}")
    assert loc_preds.shape == (B, 8732, 4)
    assert conf_preds.shape == (B, 8732, 21)
    print("SSD Multi-Scale Head verification successful!")
```

## 10. Strengths
- **Multi-Scale Detection:** First single-stage model to detect objects at multiple scale resolutions ($38 \times 38$ to $1 \times 1$).
- **High Real-Time Speed:** 59 FPS on Titan X GPU for SSD300.
- **No Proposal Network:** Completely eliminates RPN and RoI Pooling overhead.

## 11. Weaknesses
- **Struggles on Small Objects vs. FPN:** Early layers ($Conv4\_3$) lack deep semantic features; FPN later outperformed SSD on small objects by propagating deep features top-down.
- **Sensitive to Hyperparameters:** Requires careful tuning of default box scales and aspect ratios.

## 12. Research Evolution
SSD proved multi-scale feature maps were essential for single-stage detection. It directly inspired **RetinaNet** (which added Focal Loss and FPN) and **RefineDet**.

## 13. Interview Questions

**Q1: How does SSD calculate the total number of default boxes (8,732) in SSD300?**
**A1:** Sum over 6 feature maps: $(38^2 \times 4) + (19^2 \times 6) + (10^2 \times 6) + (5^2 \times 6) + (3^2 \times 4) + (1^2 \times 4) = 5776 + 2166 + 600 + 150 + 36 + 16 = \mathbf{8,732}$ default boxes.

**Q2: What is Hard Negative Mining in SSD and why is it necessary?**
**A2:** Most of the 8,732 default boxes are background, causing severe class imbalance. Hard Negative Mining sorts background default boxes by highest loss and selects the top negatives to maintain a strict **3:1 negative-to-positive ratio** during training.

**Q3: Why does SSD apply L2 Normalization to the Conv4_3 feature map?**
**A3:** Conv4_3 is an early layer with a different feature scale magnitude than deeper layers. L2 Normalization scales vector norms to a learnable parameter scale (initialized to 20) to prevent Conv4_3 gradients from dominating training.

**Q4: Compare SSD with YOLO v1.**
**A4:** YOLO v1 made predictions from a single $7 \times 7$ feature map using FC layers. SSD made predictions across 6 multi-scale feature maps ($38 \times 38$ to $1 \times 1$) using small $3 \times 3$ convolutions and default anchor boxes, achieving higher accuracy and speed.

**Q5: How are default box scales scheduled across SSD layers?**
**A5:** Default box scales follow a linear schedule: $s_k = s_{\text{min}} + \frac{s_{\text{max}} - s_{\text{min}}}{m-1}(k-1)$ with $s_{\text{min}}=0.2, s_{\text{max}}=0.9$ across $m=6$ layers.
