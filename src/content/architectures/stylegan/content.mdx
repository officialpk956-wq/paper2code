# StyleGAN

## 1. Overview
StyleGAN, introduced in 2018 by Tero Karras, Samuli Laine, and Timo Aila (NVIDIA), completely transformed generative modeling by introducing a **Style-Based Generator Architecture** that provides unprecedented control over image attributes at different semantic scales (coarse pose, fine facial features, hair texture, color scheme). Traditional GAN generators fed latent noise $z$ directly into the input layer of a convolutional network. StyleGAN completely abandoned feeding noise $z$ into the input layer. Instead, it introduced a **Two-Network Structure**:
1. **Mapping Network ($f$):** An 8-layer MLP mapping noise $z \in \mathcal{Z}$ to an intermediate disentangled style space $w \in \mathcal{W}$.
2. **Synthesis Network ($g$):** Starts from a **learned constant $4 \times 4 \times 512$ tensor** and injects style vector $w$ at every convolutional layer via **Adaptive Instance Normalization (AdaIN)**, alongside stochastic per-pixel Gaussian noise maps for fine details (hair strands, skin pores).

:::note
StyleGAN's architectural paradigm: "Discard input noise $z$ in the synthesis network. Start from a learned constant tensor, map $z \to w$ via an 8-layer MLP, inject $w$ at every layer via AdaIN styles, and add per-pixel noise maps for stochastic variation."
:::

## 2. Historical Context
Prior to StyleGAN, GAN generators treated latent space $\mathcal{Z}$ as a black box. Input noise $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ was fed into the first layer, forcing the network to perform two tasks simultaneously: warp the hyperspherical Gaussian noise distribution to match the dataset's complex feature distribution, and synthesize pixels. This caused severe **entanglement** in $\mathcal{Z}$ (e.g., attempting to change hair color accidentally changed facial age or gender).

Tero Karras et al. drew inspiration from neural style transfer. They realized that decoupling style control from spatial resolution allows intermediate latent space $\mathcal{W}$ to become highly disentangled. StyleGAN enabled intuitive feature mixing, semantic latent space manipulation, and unmatched photorealism on the FFHQ (Flickr-Faces-HQ) dataset at $1024 \times 1024$.

## 3. Problem It Solves
StyleGAN addresses three major limitations of traditional GAN generators:
1. **Latent Space Entanglement:** Standard Gaussian space $\mathcal{Z}$ forces features to be entangled. Mapping $z \to w$ via an 8-layer MLP creates a disentangled intermediate space $\mathcal{W}$ where individual vector dimensions correspond to isolated semantic concepts (e.g., eyeglasses, smile, pose).
2. **Lack of Multi-Scale Attribute Control:** Traditional generators control features globally. StyleGAN's AdaIN layers inject style at specific spatial resolutions: coarse layers ($4^2 \dots 8^2$) control pose/face shape, medium layers ($16^2 \dots 32^2$) control facial features/eyes, fine layers ($64^2 \dots 1024^2$) control color schemes/micro-textures.
3. **Deterministic Texture Rigidness:** Adding explicit per-pixel Gaussian noise maps ($B_i$) allows stochastic variations (e.g., exact placement of individual hair strands or freckles) without altering underlying facial geometry.

## 5. Layer-by-Layer Breakdown

**1. Mapping Network ($f$):**
- Takes noise vector $z \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$ and passes it through an 8-layer fully connected MLP (512 units per layer, LeakyReLU $\alpha=0.2$).
- Outputs intermediate disentangled style vector $w \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$.

**2. Synthesis Network ($g$):**
- Starts with a **fixed, learned constant tensor** of shape $(1, 512, 4, 4)$ initialized to 1s.
- Contains 18 layers (2 layers per resolution from $4 \times 4$ to $1024 \times 1024$).
- At each layer:
  1. Add scaled per-pixel Gaussian noise map $B_i \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times H \times W}$ multiplied by learned channel weights.
  2. Apply **AdaIN**: style vector $w$ is projected via a linear layer to scale $y_{s,i}$ and bias $y_{b,i}$ scalars per channel.

## 6. Tensor Flow Walkthrough
Let's trace tensors through StyleGAN for batch size $B=2$, resolution $64 \times 64 \times 3$.

1. **Mapping Network:**
   - Input $z$: `(2, 512)` $\xrightarrow{\text{8-Layer MLP}} w$: `(2, 512)`
2. **Synthesis Network Execution:**
   - Constant Input: `(2, 512, 4, 4)`
   - Layer 1 ($4 \times 4$): Add Noise `(2, 512, 4, 4)` $\to$ AdaIN($w$) $\to$ `(2, 512, 4, 4)`
   - Layer 2 ($8 \times 8$): Upsample $\to$ Conv $3 \times 3 \to$ Add Noise $\to$ AdaIN($w$) $\to$ `(2, 512, 8, 8)`
   - Layer 3 ($16 \times 16$): Upsample $\to$ Conv $3 \times 3 \to$ Add Noise $\to$ AdaIN($w$) $\to$ `(2, 256, 16, 16)`
   - Layer 4 ($32 \times 32$): Upsample $\to$ Conv $3 \times 3 \to$ Add Noise $\to$ AdaIN($w$) $\to$ `(2, 128, 32, 32)`
   - Layer 5 ($64 \times 64$): Upsample $\to$ Conv $3 \times 3 \to$ Add Noise $\to$ AdaIN($w$) $\to$ `(2, 64, 64, 64)`
   - ToRGB Conv $1 \times 1 \to$ Output RGB Image: `(2, 3, 64, 64)`.

## 7. Mathematical Foundations

### 1. Adaptive Instance Normalization (AdaIN)
Feature map $x_i$ at channel $i$ is normalized to zero mean and unit variance, then scaled and shifted using style parameters $(y_{s,i}, y_{b,i})$ generated from $w$:

$$\text{AdaIN}(x_i, y) = y_{s,i} \cdot \left( \frac{x_i - \mu(x_i)}{\sigma(x_i)} \right) + y_{b,i}$$

where $\mu(x_i)$ and $\sigma(x_i)$ are spatial scalar mean and standard deviation computed independently per channel per sample.

```text
  AdaIN Step-by-Step:
  Feature Map x_i ---> InstanceNorm(x_i) ---> Scale by y_{s,i}(w) ---> Shift by y_{b,i}(w)
```

### 2. Per-Pixel Stochastic Noise Injection
Uncorrelated per-pixel Gaussian noise map $N \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ of shape $(1, 1, H, W)$ is broadcast and multiplied by learned per-channel scaling factors $\mu_i$:

$$x_i' = x_i + \mu_i \cdot N$$

This modulates high-frequency stochastic details without perturbing global feature geometry.

## 8. Training Procedure
- **Style Mixing Regularization:** During training, two latent vectors $z_1, z_2$ are mapped to $w_1, w_2$. A random crossover point is selected, feeding $w_1$ to lower layers and $w_2$ to higher layers, forcing adjacent layers to learn independent styles.
- **Loss Objective:** WGAN-GP or Non-Saturating GAN loss with $R_1$ regularization.

## 9. PyTorch Implementation
Here is a complete PyTorch implementation of **StyleGAN's AdaIN Layer and Mapping Network**.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

class MappingNetwork(nn.Module):
    """ 8-Layer FC Mapping Network mapping z -> w """
    def __init__(self, z_dim=512, w_dim=512, num_layers=8):
        super().__init__()
        layers = []
        for i in range(num_layers):
            in_d = z_dim if i == 0 else w_dim
            layers.append(nn.Linear(in_d, w_dim))
            layers.append(nn.LeakyReLU(0.2))
        self.net = nn.Sequential(*layers)

    def forward(self, z):
        return self.net(z)

class AdaIN(nn.Module):
    """ Adaptive Instance Normalization Block """
    def __init__(self, in_channels, w_dim=512):
        super().__init__()
        self.norm = nn.InstanceNorm2d(in_channels, affine=False)
        self.style_proj = nn.Linear(w_dim, in_channels * 2)

    def forward(self, x, w):
        # x: (B, C, H, W), w: (B, w_dim)
        normalized = self.norm(x)
        
        # Project w to scale (ys) and bias (yb)
        style = self.style_proj(w).unsqueeze(-1).unsqueeze(-1) # (B, 2*C, 1, 1)
        ys, yb = style.chunk(2, dim=1)                         # (B, C, 1, 1) each
        
        return ys * normalized + yb

if __name__ == "__main__":
    B, C, H, W = 2, 512, 16, 16
    mapping = MappingNetwork(z_dim=512, w_dim=512)
    adain = AdaIN(in_channels=C, w_dim=512)
    
    z = torch.randn(B, 512)
    x = torch.randn(B, C, H, W)
    
    w = mapping(z)
    out = adain(x, w)
    
    print(f"Intermediate Style Vector w Shape: {w.shape}")
    print(f"AdaIN Output Shape:               {out.shape}")
    assert w.shape == (B, 512)
    assert out.shape == (B, C, H, W)
    print("StyleGAN Mapping and AdaIN verification successful!")
```

## 10. Strengths
- **Disentangled Latent Space ($\mathcal{W}$):** Enables independent control of specific visual attributes (pose, age, hair, expression).
- **Multi-Scale Style Control:** AdaIN injections regulate features at specific resolution scales.
- **Stochastic Variation Control:** Explicit per-pixel noise maps generate natural high-frequency variations (hair strands, freckles).

## 11. Weaknesses
- **Water Droplet Artifacts:** AdaIN normalization creates characteristic "blob" or "water droplet" artifacts in feature maps (fixed in StyleGAN2).

## 12. Research Evolution
StyleGAN established style-based image synthesis. It directly led to **StyleGAN2**, **StyleGAN2-ADA**, and **StyleGAN3**.

## 13. Interview Questions

**Q1: Why does StyleGAN discard feeding input noise $z$ into the first layer of the synthesis network?**
**A1:** Feeding $z$ into the first layer forces the network to warp hyperspherical Gaussian noise to match dataset feature distributions while simultaneously synthesizing pixels, leading to entangled representations. StyleGAN starts from a learned constant tensor and injects style vector $w$ via AdaIN at every layer.

**Q2: What is the difference between latent space $\mathcal{Z}$ and intermediate space $\mathcal{W}$?**
**A2:** $\mathcal{Z}$ is the standard Gaussian noise space $\mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$, which is inherently entangled. $\mathcal{W}$ is the intermediate space produced by passing $z$ through an 8-layer MLP. $\mathcal{W}$ does not need to follow a Gaussian distribution, making it unconstrained and highly disentangled.

**Q3: How does StyleGAN control features at different resolution scales?**
**A3:** By controlling which layers receive style vector $w$: Coarse layers ($4^2 \dots 8^2$) control pose and face shape; Medium layers ($16^2 \dots 32^2$) control eyes and expression; Fine layers ($64^2 \dots 1024^2$) control color schemes and micro-textures.

**Q4: Write out the AdaIN equation.**
**A4:** $\text{AdaIN}(x_i, y) = y_{s,i} \cdot \left( \frac{x_i - \mu(x_i)}{\sigma(x_i)} \right) + y_{b,i}$.

**Q5: What is Style Mixing Regularization in StyleGAN?**
**A5:** During training, two latent vectors $z_1, z_2$ are mapped to $w_1, w_2$, and fed into different layers of the synthesis network. This prevents adjacent layers from assuming correlated styles and enforces independent multi-scale control.
