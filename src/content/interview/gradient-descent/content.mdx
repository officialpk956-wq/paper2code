# Interview Question: What Is Gradient Descent?

> "Explain gradient descent. How would you explain it to someone non-technical, and what details matter in practice?"

## The Core Answer

Gradient descent minimizes a loss function by repeatedly stepping in the direction of steepest decrease:

$$
\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}(\theta_t)
$$

where $\eta$ is the learning rate and $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}$ is the gradient of the loss with respect to the parameters.

**The non-technical version**: you're on a foggy mountain trying to reach the valley. You can't see far, but you can feel the slope under your feet — so you repeatedly take a step downhill. Step size matters: too small and you'll take forever; too large and you'll leap across the valley and miss it.

## What Practitioners Are Expected to Know

### Learning Rate Behavior

| Learning rate | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Too low | Painfully slow convergence; may stall on plateaus |
| Too high | Loss oscillates or diverges to NaN |
| Well-tuned + schedule | Fast early progress, fine convergence late |

### Stochastic vs Batch

Computing the exact gradient over millions of examples per step is wasteful. **SGD** estimates it from a mini-batch (e.g. 32–4096 examples):

- Orders of magnitude cheaper per step
- Gradient noise acts as implicit regularization and helps escape sharp minima

```python
for batch in dataloader:
    loss = model.loss(batch)
    loss.backward()            # gradients via backpropagation
    optimizer.step()           # θ ← θ - η·∇L
    optimizer.zero_grad()
```

### Momentum and Adam

- **Momentum** accumulates a velocity vector — smooths oscillations, accelerates along consistent directions.
- **Adam** maintains per-parameter learning rates from first and second moment estimates — the default for training Transformers (typically with weight decay, as AdamW).

:::note
A common follow-up: "Why does Adam usually need a learning-rate warmup for Transformers?" — early Adam steps have poorly-estimated second moments, producing destructively large updates.
:::

## Red Flags

- Unable to state what the gradient *is* (vector of partial derivatives pointing uphill)
- Claiming SGD noise is purely a downside
- Not knowing why learning rate schedules exist
