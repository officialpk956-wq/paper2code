# Interview Question: Explain Attention

> "Explain how self-attention works, as if to a junior engineer who knows basic neural networks."

This question tests whether you *understand* attention or have merely memorized the formula. Strong answers work at multiple levels of depth.

## Level 1: The Intuition (30 seconds)

Each word in a sentence builds its meaning by looking at other words. In "The animal didn't cross the street because **it** was too tired", resolving "it" requires looking at "animal". Self-attention is a learned mechanism that lets every token gather information from every other token, weighted by relevance.

## Level 2: The Mechanism (2 minutes)

Each token emits three vectors via learned projections:

- **Query** — what am I looking for?
- **Key** — what do I contain?
- **Value** — what do I contribute if selected?

Relevance between token $i$ and $j$ is the dot product $q_i \cdot k_j$. Softmax turns the scores into weights, and the output is the weighted sum of values:

$$
\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V
$$

A good analogy: a soft, differentiable hash-map lookup — instead of retrieving one value, you retrieve a weighted blend of all values.

## Level 3: The Details (follow-up depth)

**Why $\sqrt{d_k}$?** Dot products of random $d_k$-dimensional vectors have variance proportional to $d_k$. Without scaling, softmax saturates and gradients vanish.

**Why multiple heads?** One softmax distribution can only express one mixing pattern per token. Eight heads in parallel subspaces let the model track syntax, coreference, position, and semantics simultaneously.

**Why is it $O(n^2)$?** The score matrix $QK^T$ has an entry for every token pair. This is the bottleneck FlashAttention (better memory access) and sparse/linear attention variants (approximation) attack.

:::tip
Interviewers consistently reward candidates who *volunteer the why* — why scaling, why softmax, why multiple heads — without being prompted.
:::

## Red Flags Interviewers Watch For

- Reciting the formula without explaining Q/K/V roles
- Confusing self-attention (one sequence) with cross-attention (decoder attending to encoder)
- Not knowing that the same input produces Q, K, and V via three different learned projections
