# LLaMA: Open and Efficient Foundation Language Models

**Touvron et al. · arXiv:2302.13971 · Meta AI**

---

## 1. Overview

LLaMA (Large Language Model Meta AI) catalyzed the open-source LLM revolution. While Chinchilla (2022) proved that large models like GPT-3 were undertrained given their parameter counts, LLaMA took this a step further: it deliberately *over-trained* smaller models far beyond the Chinchilla-optimal point.

The goal was inference efficiency. A 13B parameter model trained on 1 Trillion tokens is not compute-optimal for training, but it is much cheaper to serve at inference time than a larger model matching its performance. LLaMA-13B outperformed the much larger GPT-3 (175B) on most benchmarks, and LLaMA-65B was competitive with Chinchilla-70B and PaLM-540B.

By releasing the weights to the research community, LLaMA became the de facto foundation for thousands of fine-tuned models (Alpaca, Vicuna), kicking off a massive wave of open-source innovation.

---

## 2. Historical Context

By early 2023, closed APIs (OpenAI, Anthropic) dominated the LLM landscape. The best open-source models (OPT, BLOOM) were weak compared to proprietary counterparts. The community lacked a strong, capable foundation model to experiment on.

Meta aimed to provide models trained exclusively on publicly available data (no proprietary datasets) to prove that open data could yield SOTA performance, while deliberately targeting smaller parameter footprints (7B, 13B) that could run on consumer hardware.

---

## 3. Research Problem

How to maximize the performance of a language model at a specific inference budget (e.g., a model that fits on a single GPU) by training it longer than compute-optimal scaling laws recommend.

## 5. Why Previous Methods Failed

Prior open models either lacked sufficient training data or used subpar architectures compared to closed SOTA. They followed the pre-Chinchilla trend of scaling parameters rather than data.

---

## 6. Core Innovation

**Over-training for Inference Efficiency:**
Chinchilla recommends ~260B tokens for a 13B model. LLaMA trained its 13B model on 1 Trillion tokens. While the marginal training return diminishes, performance still improves, resulting in a highly capable model with a tiny inference footprint.

---

## 7. Architecture Breakdown

LLaMA is a decoder-only Transformer incorporating the best architectural improvements since GPT-3:

- **Pre-normalization (RMSNorm):** Normalizes input to each sub-layer, replacing standard LayerNorm for better training stability and speed.
- **SwiGLU Activation Function:** Replaces ReLU, borrowing from PaLM, to improve performance.
- **Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE):** Replaces absolute positional embeddings, injecting positional information at each layer, allowing better extrapolation to longer sequences.

---

## 8. Mathematical Foundations

**Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE):**
Instead of adding a position vector to the token embedding, RoPE rotates the query and key representations in a complex plane by an angle proportional to their absolute position, naturally encoding relative distances.

---

## 9. Key Experiments

Evaluated on common sense reasoning, reading comprehension, mathematical reasoning, and code generation.

---

## 10. Benchmark Results

- **LLaMA-13B vs GPT-3:** LLaMA-13B surpasses GPT-3 (175B) on most benchmarks despite being 10x smaller.
- **LLaMA-65B vs Chinchilla/PaLM:** Highly competitive with Chinchilla-70B and PaLM-540B.

---

## 11. Ablation Studies

The paper tracks training loss over massive token counts, showing that even at 1T tokens (and later 2T tokens in LLaMA-2), the loss for the 7B and 13B models was still steadily decreasing without plateauing.

---

## 12. Implementation Details

- **Dataset:** 1T to 1.4T tokens drawn purely from public sources: CommonCrawl, C4, GitHub, Wikipedia, Books, ArXiv, StackExchange.
- **Optimizer:** AdamW.
- **Hardware:** Trained on 2048 A100 GPUs (80GB).

---

## 13. Engineering Insights

- **The Inference Premium:** If a model will be run millions of times (e.g., an open-source model used globally), spending extra compute on training a smaller model is economically optimal.
- **Architecture convergence:** RMSNorm, SwiGLU, and RoPE became the standard triumvirate for nearly all subsequent LLM architectures.

---

## 14. Limitations

- **Not Instruction Tuned:** The base LLaMA models are plain text continuers. They required the community (and later Meta with LLaMA-2-Chat) to apply SFT and RLHF.
- **Context Window:** Initially limited to 2048 tokens.

---

## 15. Influence on Later Research

LLaMA fundamentally changed the AI research landscape. It spawned Stanford's Alpaca (instruction tuning LLaMA with synthetic data) and led directly to LLaMA-2 and LLaMA-3, cementing open-weights models as a viable alternative to proprietary APIs.
