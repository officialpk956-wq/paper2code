# PaLM: Scaling Language Modeling with Pathways

**Chowdhery et al. · JMLR 2023 · arXiv:2204.02311 · Google Research**

---

## 1. Overview

PaLM (Pathways Language Model) is Google's 540 billion parameter dense autoregressive language model, trained using the Pathways system — a novel ML infrastructure enabling training across thousands of TPU chips simultaneously with efficient inter-device communication. Released in April 2022, it demonstrated that scaling dense Transformers beyond 500B parameters produced discontinuous capability jumps, particularly on multi-step reasoning tasks.

PaLM's landmark result: on the BIG-Bench suite of 150 tasks, PaLM 540B surpassed human-average performance on 58% of tasks — the first model to do so. On mathematical chain-of-thought reasoning (with prompting), it outperformed fine-tuned models. It established that chain-of-thought prompting was a qualitative capability that emerged at scale.

---

## 2. Historical Context

By April 2022, the LLM landscape had:
- GPT-3 (175B, OpenAI) — the few-shot benchmark
- Gopher (280B, DeepMind) — strong factual performance
- Chinchilla (70B, DeepMind) — just published, showing parameter efficiency
- MT-NLG (530B, NVIDIA/Microsoft) — largest at the time

Google had been training large models (LaMDA, GLaM) but hadn't published a fully transparent scaling study at GPT-3's level. PaLM was Google's comprehensive answer: a 540B dense model trained on the most diverse dataset assembled, using infrastructure that allowed training across 6,144 TPU v4 chips.

---

## 3. Research Problem

Do quality improvements from scaling dense Transformers continue beyond 500B parameters? Do new capabilities emerge discontinuously (rather than smoothly) at extreme scale? Can chain-of-thought reasoning emerge from scale alone?

## 5. Why Previous Methods Failed

**Dense scaling plateau concern:** Kaplan's original scaling laws (pre-Chinchilla) suggested returns diminish. Whether 540B dense would outperform 280B was empirically unknown — no one had published results at this scale.

**Reasoning at scale was unproven.** Chain-of-thought prompting (Wei et al., 2022 — concurrent) required sufficient scale to work. PaLM was the vehicle for proving this empirically.

---

## 6. Core Innovation

**Architecture — Decoder-only with key improvements:**
- **SwiGLU activation** (replaces ReLU/GELU; empirically better at large scale)
- **Parallel attention + FFN** (instead of sequential): compute attention and FFN simultaneously, merge results. Equivalent mathematically at initialization, but 15% faster due to better parallelism.
- **Multi-Query Attention (MQA):** All query heads share a single key/value head. Reduces KV-cache size dramatically during inference.
- **RoPE Positional Embeddings** (rotary — same as LLaMA, better extrapolation)
- **No biases** in any linear layers (reduces memory)

**Training — Pathways infrastructure:**
PaLM was trained across two TPU v4 Pods simultaneously (3,072 chips each, 6,144 total). The Pathways system managed cross-pod communication for data parallelism, enabling the largest dense model training run at the time.

**Chain-of-thought evaluation:**
PaLM was systematically evaluated with "Let's think step by step" chain-of-thought prompting — the first large-scale demonstration that reasoning emerges from scale.

## 7. Architecture Breakdown

| Property | PaLM-8B | PaLM-62B | PaLM-540B |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Layers | 32 | 64 | 118 |
| Width | 4096 | 8192 | 18432 |
| Heads (Q/KV) | 16/1 | 32/1 | 48/1 |
| FFN Dim | 16384 | 32768 | 73728 |
| Parameters | 8B | 62B | 540B |
| Sequence Length | 2048 | 2048 | 2048 |

**Multi-Query Attention:** All query heads attend to the same K and V projections. This reduces KV-cache size from $O(n \cdot h)$ to $O(n)$ per layer — critical for 540B inference.

---

## 8. Mathematical Foundations

**Parallel Attention + FFN:**

Standard Transformer:
$$x' = x + \text{Attn}(\text{LN}(x))$$
$$x'' = x' + \text{FFN}(\text{LN}(x'))$$

PaLM (parallel):
$$x' = x + \text{Attn}(\text{LN}(x)) + \text{FFN}(\text{LN}(x))$$

The FFN and attention branches read from the same normalized input, and their outputs are summed together with the residual. Communication cost is halved because you only need to all-reduce once.

**SwiGLU FFN:**
$$\text{SwiGLU}(x, W, V, W_2) = (\text{Swish}(xW) \otimes xV) W_2$$

Where $\text{Swish}(x) = x \cdot \sigma(x)$. Three weight matrices instead of two, but consistently outperforms GELU at scale.

## 12. Implementation Details

- **Dataset:** 780B tokens; multilingual (English 78%, multilingual 10%, source code 13%)
- **Sources:** social media, filtered webpages, books, GitHub, Wikipedia, news
- **Optimizer:** Adafactor (memory-efficient variant of Adam)
- **Infrastructure:** Pathways; 6,144 TPU v4 chips
- **Training time:** ~54 days for 540B
- **Batch size:** 2048 sequences × 2048 tokens = ~4M tokens/step

---

## 13. Engineering Insights

- **Multi-Query Attention halves KV-cache.** For serving 540B at inference, the KV-cache grows with sequence length. MQA reduces its size from $2 \times n_{heads} \times seq\_len \times head\_dim$ to $2 \times 1 \times seq\_len \times head\_dim$ — a 48× reduction for PaLM-540B's 48 heads. This enabled serving long contexts without OOM.
- **Parallel Attn+FFN is a free 15% throughput gain.** With no quality cost, this became standard in subsequent models (Falcon, some Mistral variants).
- **Chain-of-thought prompting is a scale-dependent emergent capability.** At 8B parameters, CoT prompting barely helps. At 540B, it gives +29 percentage points on GSM8K. This is one of the clearest demonstrations of emergent behavior.

---

## 14. Limitations

- **540B requires extreme hardware to serve.** Even quantized to INT8, 540B needs 540GB of accelerator memory — over 6 fully-loaded A100-80GB GPUs just to hold the weights.
- **English-heavy training.** Despite multilingual data, 78% English creates a bias toward English tasks.
- **Dense model at a compute cost Chinchilla would consider suboptimal.** PaLM-540B trained on 780B tokens — Chinchilla's rule would suggest 10.8T tokens for a 540B model.
