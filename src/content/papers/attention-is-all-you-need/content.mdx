# Attention Is All You Need

**Vaswani, Shazeer, Parmar et al. · NeurIPS 2017 · arXiv:1706.03762 · ~84,000 citations**

---

## 1. Overview

"Attention Is All You Need" is the most consequential machine learning paper since backpropagation. Published by researchers at Google Brain and Google Research, it introduced the **Transformer** — an architecture built entirely on self-attention, discarding recurrence and convolution from sequence modeling entirely.

Every frontier AI system built after 2017 — GPT-3, BERT, Codex, Claude, Gemini, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, AlphaFold — uses the Transformer as its core computational engine. The paper's central claim, stated in its title, proved to be an understatement: attention isn't just *sufficient* for sequence modeling, it's demonstrably superior in quality, training speed, and scalability to everything that came before it.

---

## 2. Historical Context

In 2017, the dominant paradigm for sequence tasks (translation, summarization, question answering) was the **encoder-decoder LSTM with Bahdanau attention** (2014). These models:

- Encode the source sequence into a final hidden state
- Decode autoregressively, with attention over encoder states at each step
- Require sequential computation ($O(n)$ sequential operations for length-$n$ sequences)

The core bottleneck was **sequential dependence**: computing hidden state $h_t$ requires $h_{t-1}$, which requires $h_{t-2}$, and so on. On a GPU designed for massive parallelism, you could only use the GPU sequentially during the RNN's recurrent computation — a profound mismatch.

Convolutional approaches (WaveNet, ByteNet, ConvS2S) allowed parallelism but required $O(\log n)$ or $O(n)$ layers to connect distant positions. Nobody had yet asked: what if we just removed recurrence and convolution entirely?

---

## 3. Research Problem

Can a purely attention-based model, with no recurrence and no convolution:

1. **Parallelize** the full training computation across sequence positions?
2. **Connect** any two positions in the sequence with a constant number of operations?
3. **Match or surpass** state-of-the-art recurrent models on machine translation quality?

The skepticism was warranted: attention had only ever been used as a *supplement* to recurrence, not as a replacement.

## 5. Why Previous Methods Failed

**RNNs:** Sequential computation is antithetical to GPU parallelism. Training on modern hardware is orders of magnitude slower than theoretically possible. Long-range dependencies require gradient flow through hundreds of recurrent steps, causing vanishing gradients despite gating (LSTM, GRU).

**CNNs (ConvS2S, ByteNet):** Convolutions parallelize over positions, but connecting position 1 to position 512 requires $O(\log n)$ layers (dilated convolutions) or $O(n)$ layers (standard convolutions). The effective receptive field grows slowly with depth.

**The core insight:** Self-attention connects every pair of positions with a single matrix operation. Maximum path length is always $O(1)$, regardless of sequence length.

---

## 6. Core Innovation

Three jointly sufficient innovations:

**1. Scaled Dot-Product Self-Attention:**
Every token generates three vectors: Query ($Q$), Key ($K$), Value ($V$). The attention scores are:
$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$
The $\sqrt{d_k}$ scaling prevents saturation in the softmax when $d_k$ is large.

**2. Multi-Head Attention:**
Run $h$ attention functions in parallel in different learned subspaces, then concatenate:
$$\text{MultiHead}(Q,K,V) = \text{Concat}(\text{head}_1,\dots,\text{head}_h)W^O$$
Different heads can attend to different types of relationships simultaneously (syntax, semantics, coreference).

**3. Positional Encoding:**
Since attention is permutation-invariant, position information must be injected:
$$PE_{(pos, 2i)} = \sin(pos/10000^{2i/d_{model}})$$
$$PE_{(pos, 2i+1)} = \cos(pos/10000^{2i/d_{model}})$$

---

## 7. Architecture Breakdown

**Encoder (6 layers):**
- Multi-Head Self-Attention → Add & LayerNorm → Feed-Forward → Add & LayerNorm

**Decoder (6 layers):**
- Masked Multi-Head Self-Attention → Add & LayerNorm
- Multi-Head Cross-Attention (over encoder output) → Add & LayerNorm
- Feed-Forward → Add & LayerNorm

**Key hyperparameters (base model):**
- $d_{model} = 512$, $d_{ff} = 2048$, $h = 8$ heads, $d_k = d_v = 64$
- ~65M parameters

See [Transformer architecture page](/architectures/transformer) for full tensor-flow walkthrough.

## 8. Mathematical Foundations

**Complexity comparison:**

| Layer Type | Complexity per Layer | Sequential Ops | Max Path Length |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Self-Attention | $O(n^2 \cdot d)$ | $O(1)$ | $O(1)$ |
| Recurrent | $O(n \cdot d^2)$ | $O(n)$ | $O(n)$ |
| Convolutional | $O(k \cdot n \cdot d^2)$ | $O(1)$ | $O(\log_k n)$ |

The $O(n^2)$ attention cost is the price paid for $O(1)$ path length — the fundamental trade-off that defined the next decade of research (flash attention, sparse attention, linear attention).

**Training schedule (the warmup schedule):**
$$lrate = d_{model}^{-0.5} \cdot \min(step^{-0.5},\ step \cdot warmup\_steps^{-1.5})$$

This specific schedule — warmup then inverse-sqrt decay — became a standard in LLM training.

## 9. Key Experiments

- **WMT 2014 English→German:** 28.4 BLEU (big model) — new SOTA
- **WMT 2014 English→French:** 41.0 BLEU — new SOTA
- **English Constituency Parsing:** Transferred to parsing without task-specific tuning

Training: 8× P100 GPUs. Base model: 12 hours. Big model: 3.5 days.

|:---|:---|
| Full model (base) | 25.8 |

| Replaced learned with fixed PE | 25.7 (≈ same) |
| Removed residual dropout | 23.7 |

**Key insight:** Every component contributes. Positional encoding is crucial. Sinusoidal and learned PE are equally good.

## 12. Implementation Details

- **Regularization:** Dropout 0.1 on attention weights, FFN outputs, and embedding sums
- **Label Smoothing:** $\epsilon_{ls} = 0.1$ — hurts perplexity but improves BLEU
- **Optimizer:** Adam ($\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.98$, $\epsilon=10^{-9}$) with warmup schedule
- **Beam search:** Beam size 4, length penalty $\alpha=0.6$
- **Weight sharing:** Input and output embedding matrices share weights

---

## 13. Engineering Insights

- **The FFN is a key-value memory.** Each position passes through a 2048-dim MLP independently. This is where factual knowledge is stored in language models — not the attention heads.
- **Multi-head attention = ensemble of soft routing functions.** Different heads specialize in different linguistic relationships. Visualizing attention heads reveals syntactic (subject-verb agreement), semantic (coreference), and positional patterns.
- **Residual connections + LayerNorm are structural load-bearing elements.** Removing either causes immediate training failure in deep models.
- **The encoder-decoder design proved unnecessary for many tasks.** GPT showed that decoder-only works for generation; BERT showed encoder-only works for understanding. The full encoder-decoder is optimal only for tasks with clear source and target sequences (translation, summarization).

---

## 14. Limitations

- **$O(n^2)$ memory and compute** for attention makes very long sequences (>4096 tokens) prohibitively expensive without approximations.
- **No structural inductive bias:** Transformers learn everything from data. They require 100× more data than humans to learn basic linguistic structure.
- **Autoregressive inference is sequential.** Token-by-token generation cannot be parallelized, creating latency bottlenecks at serving time.
- **Positional encoding limitation:** Sinusoidal encoding extrapolates poorly beyond the training sequence length. Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE, 2021) later solved this.
