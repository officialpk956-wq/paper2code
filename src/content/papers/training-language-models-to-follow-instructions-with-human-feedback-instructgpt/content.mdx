# Training Language Models to Follow Instructions with Human Feedback

**Ouyang, Wu, Jiang et al. (OpenAI) · arXiv 2022 · arXiv:2203.02155 · ~8,500 citations**

---

## 1. Overview

This paper introduced **InstructGPT**, the direct predecessor to ChatGPT. It presented the first large-scale validation of **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)** to align Large Language Models (LLMs) with human intent. While raw base models like GPT-3 excel at next-token prediction, they often generate outputs that are toxic, unhelpful, or fail to follow instructions.

To solve this misalignment, the authors developed a three-stage pipeline (Supervised Fine-Tuning, Reward Modeling, and PPO Reinforcement Learning) to optimize models based on human preferences. The resulting 1.3B parameter InstructGPT model outperformed the much larger 175B GPT-3 base model in human evaluation, proving that alignment is a highly efficient way to unlock LLM utility.

---

## 2. Historical Context

In 2020, GPT-3 demonstrated that scaling autoregressive models yields emergent few-shot capabilities. However, these models were not aligned: their objective was to predict the next token on the internet, not to answer questions helpful to a user. If a user prompted: *"Write a Python function to sort a list,"* a base model might respond by listing more prompt variations, or write a blog post about sorting, rather than writing the code.

Aligning models required moving beyond next-token prediction. While Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) helped, gathering high-quality target demonstrations for every possible user prompt was too expensive. A more scalable approach was needed to teach models human preferences across open-ended tasks.

---

## 3. Research Problem

How do we align Large Language Models to follow instructions safely, accurately, and helpfully while minimizing harmful outputs? Specifically:
1. How do we collect and incorporate human preferences into a scalable loss function?
2. Can reinforcement learning optimize language model policies without causing training instability?
3. What is the impact of alignment on the model's performance on core academic NLP benchmarks (the "alignment tax")?

---

## 4. Prior Work

| Method | Scaling Objective | Alignment Feedback | Out-of-Distribution Safety |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| GPT-3 Pre-training | Next-token prediction | Implicit (Web text) | Poor |
| Supervised Fine-Tuning | Cross-entropy loss | Explicit (Human demonstrations) | Moderate |
| **RLHF (InstructGPT)** | **PPO Reinforcement Learning** | **Explicit (Human preferences)** | **High** |

---

## 5. Why Previous Methods Failed

**Pure Pre-training:** Base models replicate the biases, errors, and toxic content found on the web. They are completion engines, not assistants, requiring complex prompt engineering to follow instructions.

**Standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** SFT is limited by the creativity and expertise of the labelers. For complex tasks (e.g., writing a summary), multiple different outputs may be correct. Standard cross-entropy loss penalizes correct outputs that do not match the exact target text, restricting model capability.

---

## 6. Core Innovation

InstructGPT uses a **three-stage alignment pipeline**:

```
[Base Model] ---> Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)
                       |
                       v
[SFT Model]  ---> Stage 2: Reward Model (RM) Training
                       |
                       v
[RM + SFT]   ---> Stage 3: Reinforcement Learning via PPO
```

**Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
Fine-tune the base model on a small dataset of human-written instruction demonstrations.

**Stage 2: Reward Model (RM) Training:**
Prompt the SFT model to generate multiple completions for a prompt. Human labelers rank these completions from best to worst. Train a scalar reward model $r_\psi(x, y)$ to predict these rankings using a pairwise loss function.

**Stage 3: RL Fine-Tuning (PPO):**
Optimize the SFT model's policy using the Reward Model as the reward generator. To prevent the model from drifting too far from the original distribution (which leads to gibberish), add a KL divergence penalty between the active RL policy and the initial SFT policy.

---

## 7. Architecture Breakdown

- **SFT Model:** GPT-3 architecture, initialized from pre-trained weights, fine-tuned for 16 epochs.
- **Reward Model (RM):** A 6B parameter GPT model (downscaled to improve training speed and stability). It takes a prompt $x$ and completion $y$ as input and outputs a scalar reward score.
- **RL Policy (PPO):** A copy of the SFT model optimized using PPO updates. It includes a value network initialized from the reward model.

---

## 8. Mathematical Foundations

**Pairwise Loss for the Reward Model:**
The loss function for training the reward model $r_\psi(x, y)$ on a dataset of pairwise comparisons is:
$$\mathcal{L}(\psi) = -\mathbb{E}_{(x, y_w, y_l) \sim D} \left[ \log \sigma(r_\psi(x, y_w) - r_\psi(x, y_l)) \right]$$
where $y_w$ is the preferred completion and $y_l$ is the less preferred completion.

**PPO Objective Function:**
The objective optimized during Stage 3 is:
$$\text{obj}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(x, y) \sim D_{\pi_\theta}} \left[ r_\psi(x, y) - \beta D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_\theta(y|x) \,\|\, \pi^{\text{SFT}}(y|x)) \right] + \gamma \mathbb{E}_{x \sim D_{\text{pretrain}}} \left[ \log \pi_\theta(x) \right]$$
- $D_{\text{KL}}$ is the KL divergence penalty preventing the model from exploiting the reward model.
- The final term is a pre-training gradient correction to prevent the model's capabilities on standard tasks from degrading.

---

## 9. Key Experiments

The authors evaluated model outputs on prompts submitted to the OpenAI API:
- Labelers rated outputs based on **Helpfulness, Honesty, and Safety**.
- Tested model outputs against base GPT-3 models on standard academic benchmarks (HellaSwag, TruthfulQA, RealToxicityPrompts).

---

## 10. Benchmark Results

- **Human Preferences:** The 1.3B parameter InstructGPT model was preferred by human evaluators over the 175B parameter GPT-3 base model on 85% of test prompts.
- **Toxicity Reduction:** InstructGPT generated 25% fewer toxic outputs than GPT-3 on RealToxicityPrompts.
- **Truthfulness:** InstructGPT scored significantly higher on TruthfulQA compared to GPT-3.

---

## 11. Ablation Studies

**The Alignment Tax:**
Optimizing purely for human preferences using PPO causes the model's performance on academic benchmarks (like GSM8K math or code generation) to degrade. The authors showed that adding the pre-training gradient correction term ($\gamma \mathbb{E} \left[ \log \pi_\theta(x) \right]$) to the PPO objective successfully mitigated this tax, preserving the model's general capabilities while maintaining alignment.

---

## 12. Implementation Details

- **Labeler Selection:** Hired 40 contract labelers based on performance on screening tests, emphasizing sensitivity to toxicity and bias.
- **Hyperparameters:** $\beta$ (KL coefficient) set to 0.02, $\gamma$ (pre-training correction) set to 27.8.
- **RL Training:** Optimized for 20,000 steps using PPO with a rollout batch size of 512.

---

## 13. Engineering Insights

- **Scale mismatch:** A small reward model (6B) is sufficient to optimize a large policy model (175B). This saves GPU memory during RL training, which requires holding multiple copies of the model in VRAM (policy, reference, reward, value).
- **KL divergence is critical:** Without the KL penalty, the policy model quickly learns to exploit the reward model's weaknesses (e.g., generating gibberish tokens that score high on safety keywords), causing model performance to collapse.
- **Alignment does not create knowledge:** RLHF does not teach the model new information; instead, it teaches the model how to expose and format the knowledge it already acquired during the pre-training phase.

---

## 14. Limitations

- **Labeler Bias:** The model is aligned to the preferences of a small group of contract workers, which may not represent general human values.
- **Honesty is hard to evaluate:** Labelers cannot verify the truth of complex technical claims, meaning the model can learn to generate confident-sounding but incorrect information (hallucinations).
- **Safety Over-refusal:** Aggressive safety alignment can lead to the model refusing benign prompts that contain sensitive words.

---

## 15. Influence on Later Research

- **ChatGPT (2022) & GPT-4 (2023):** Applied the same RLHF pipeline to chat-based architectures, sparking the generative AI wave.
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO, 2023):** Showed that the RLHF objective can be optimized directly using preference pairs without training an explicit reward model or using PPO.
- **Constitutional AI (Anthropic, 2022):** Replaced human labelers in Stage 2/3 with AI feedback guided by a set of safety rules.
