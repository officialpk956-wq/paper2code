# BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding

**Devlin, Chang, Lee & Toutanova · NAACL 2019 · arXiv:1810.04805 · ~90,000 citations**

---

## 1. Overview

BERT demolished 11 NLP benchmarks simultaneously upon release in October 2018. It achieved state-of-the-art on SQuAD (reading comprehension), GLUE (language understanding), NER (named entity recognition), and more — all from a single pre-trained model with minimal task-specific fine-tuning.

The key insight: the Transformer's encoder is deeply bidirectional. Every token attends to every other token simultaneously. But GPT-1 (released two months before BERT) was trained autoregressively — left to right — losing the right context. BERT resolved this by introducing **Masked Language Modeling (MLM)**: randomly hide tokens, force the model to predict them using *both* left and right context. The resulting representations are richer, contextual, and dramatically more transferable.

BERT launched the era of **fine-tuning pre-trained language models** as the universal paradigm for NLP. Every subsequent language model — RoBERTa, XLNet, ALBERT, DistilBERT, and eventually GPT-3 — is defined in relation to BERT.

---

## 2. Historical Context

Before BERT, NLP transfer learning followed the **feature-based** approach:
- Train word embeddings (Word2Vec, GloVe, FastText) on large corpora
- Use the fixed embeddings as input features for task-specific models
- These embeddings were **context-free**: "bank" had one vector for both "river bank" and "bank account"

ELMo (2018) improved this with **contextual** embeddings from a bidirectional LSTM, generating different representations of "bank" depending on its context. But ELMo's LSTM architecture couldn't scale to Transformer-level capacity, and its features were used as fixed representations (not fine-tuned end-to-end).

GPT (OpenAI, June 2018) showed that Transformer-based pre-training + fine-tuning worked powerfully — but its left-to-right language modeling limited its contextual richness. Google's BERT answered: what if we pre-train a Transformer encoder *bidirectionally*?

---

## 3. Research Problem

How can we design a pre-training objective for a **bidirectional Transformer encoder** without allowing the model to "see" the answer through future context?

The standard language modeling objective (predict next token) is inherently unidirectional: token $t$ cannot attend to tokens $>t$ during training, or it would trivially see the answer. This prevents standard LM pre-training from training a bidirectional model.

## 4. Prior Work

| Method | Model | Pre-training Objective | Limitation |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Word2Vec (2013) | Shallow NN | Predict surrounding words | Context-free; shallow |
| GloVe (2014) | Matrix factorization | Co-occurrence statistics | Context-free |
| ELMo (2018) | Bidirectional LSTM | Forward + backward LM | Feature-based only; LSTM-limited |
| GPT-1 (2018) | Transformer decoder | Left-to-right LM | Unidirectional; loses right context |

---

## 5. Why Previous Methods Failed

**Unidirectional models (GPT-1)** generate left-to-right — each token sees only its preceding context. This is suboptimal for understanding tasks:

- In "The **bank** by the river", understanding "bank" requires seeing "river" (to its right)
- In question answering, the model must attend to the *entire* question and context bidirectionally

**Feature-based models (ELMo)** use bidirectional LSTMs but:
- Representations are fixed after pre-training — the model doesn't adapt to the task
- LSTM capacity is fundamentally limited compared to Transformers

**The core problem with naïve bidirectional training:** If you train a bidirectional Transformer on "predict the next token," the token $t+1$ is already visible in the right context — the model trivially reads the answer. You need a training objective that doesn't expose the target to the model.

---

## 6. Core Innovation

**Masked Language Modeling (MLM):**
Randomly mask 15% of input tokens. Replace them with `[MASK]`. Train the model to predict the original tokens using the surrounding context — attending to *all* non-masked positions on both sides.

**Masking strategy (80/10/10 rule):**
Of the 15% chosen to be "masked":
- 80% replaced with `[MASK]`
- 10% replaced with a random word
- 10% kept unchanged

The 10/10 split prevents the model from assuming every `[MASK]` token is a masked word during fine-tuning (where `[MASK]` never appears).

**Next Sentence Prediction (NSP):**
Train a binary classifier: given sentences A and B, does B follow A in the original document? Uses the `[CLS]` token's representation. (Later shown by RoBERTa to be mostly unnecessary.)

**Input representation:** Sum of three learned embeddings:
- Token embedding (WordPiece, 30K vocabulary)
- Segment embedding (A or B)
- Position embedding (learned, not sinusoidal)

## 7. Architecture Breakdown

BERT uses the Transformer **encoder** only (no decoder, no causal masking):

| Model | Layers | Hidden | Heads | Parameters |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| BERT-Base | 12 | 768 | 12 | 110M |
| BERT-Large | 24 | 1024 | 16 | 340M |

Full bidirectional attention: every token attends to every other token in the 512-token context window.

**Special tokens:**
- `[CLS]` — First token; its final hidden state used for classification tasks
- `[SEP]` — Separator between sentence A and B
- `[MASK]` — Used during pre-training only

## 8. Mathematical Foundations

**MLM Objective:**

Let $\hat{x}$ be the masked input (with some tokens replaced by `[MASK]`). Let $M$ be the set of masked positions. BERT minimizes:
$$L_{MLM} = -\sum_{i \in M} \log P(x_i | \hat{x}; \theta)$$

Each position's probability is a softmax over the full vocabulary, applied to its final hidden state:
$$P(x_i = w | \hat{x}) = \text{softmax}(E \cdot h_i)[w]$$

where $E$ is the token embedding matrix and $h_i$ is the final hidden state at position $i$.

**NSP Objective:**

Binary cross-entropy on the `[CLS]` token output:
$$L_{NSP} = -[\mathbb{1}_{IsNext}\log P(\text{IsNext}|h_{CLS}) + \mathbb{1}_{NotNext}\log P(\text{NotNext}|h_{CLS})]$$

**Total pre-training loss:**
$$L = L_{MLM} + L_{NSP}$$

**Conclusion:** Both pre-training and bidirectionality are critical. NSP helps modestly.

## 12. Implementation Details

- **Pre-training data:** Wikipedia (2.5B words) + BooksCorpus (800M words)
- **Pre-training compute:** BERT-Large on 64 Cloud TPUs for 4 days
- **Fine-tuning:** 2–4 epochs, LR 2e-5 to 5e-5, batch size 16–32
- **Tokenizer:** WordPiece with 30K vocabulary (handles out-of-vocabulary via subwords)
- **Max sequence length:** 512 tokens
- **Masking ratio:** 15% of tokens per sequence

---

## 13. Engineering Insights

- **The `[CLS]` token is a learned sentinel.** Because it participates in full bidirectional attention with all other tokens, its final state naturally aggregates sequence-level information. This architectural choice — a special leading token whose representation summarizes the sequence — was adopted by all subsequent models.
- **Pre-training once, fine-tune many times.** BERT's pre-training is expensive (4 days on 64 TPUs), but fine-tuning is cheap (minutes on a single GPU). This democratized NLP: anyone could download BERT weights and adapt them to a new task.
- **WordPiece tokenization handles unknown words gracefully.** "unbelievably" → ["un", "##believ", "##ably"]. No out-of-vocabulary issue, unlike word-level tokenization.
- **The 80/10/10 masking rule matters.** If you only replace with `[MASK]`, the model learns to ignore `[MASK]` tokens at fine-tuning time (they never appear). The 10% random + 10% unchanged tokens force the model to build representations for all tokens, not just `[MASK]`.

---

## 14. Limitations

- **Not generative.** BERT cannot generate fluent text. Its bidirectional attention breaks autoregressive generation.
- **`[MASK]` is absent at fine-tuning time.** A distribution mismatch exists between pre-training (15% masked tokens) and fine-tuning (no masked tokens). XLNet (2019) addressed this with permutation language modeling.
- **Inefficient pre-training.** Only 15% of tokens are predicted per step — 85% contribute to context but not to the loss. GPT-style models predict every token.
- **Fixed context window (512 tokens).** Long documents must be split, losing cross-chunk context. Longformer and BigBird extended this to 4096+ tokens.
- **NSP proved unnecessary.** RoBERTa (2019) showed removing NSP, training longer, and using larger batches surpassed BERT significantly.

## 15. Influence on Later Research

| Model | Year | What It Fixed |
|:---|:---|:---|
| RoBERTa | 2019 | Removed NSP; trained longer; dynamic masking |
| ALBERT | 2019 | Parameter sharing; sentence-order prediction |
| DistilBERT | 2019 | Knowledge distillation; 40% smaller, 97% of performance |
| XLNet | 2019 | Permutation LM; no masking discrepancy |
| DeBERTa | 2020 | Disentangled attention; SOTA on GLUE |
| ELECTRA | 2020 | Generator-discriminator training; 4× more efficient than BERT |
