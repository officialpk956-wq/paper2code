# Learning Transferable Visual Models From Natural Language Supervision (CLIP)

**Radford et al. · ICML 2021 · arXiv:2103.00020 · OpenAI**

---

## 1. Overview

CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) is one of the most practically impactful papers in the history of computer vision. By training an image encoder and a text encoder jointly on 400 million image-text pairs scraped from the internet — using contrastive loss to align matching pairs and push apart non-matching pairs — CLIP learned a rich joint embedding space where images and their text descriptions occupy nearby positions.

The result: zero-shot image classification on any category you can describe in natural language, without a single labeled example for that category. On ImageNet, CLIP achieved 76.2% top-1 accuracy zero-shot — matching the performance of a fully supervised ResNet-50, despite never seeing a single ImageNet label during training.

CLIP became the foundational image understanding engine for Stable Diffusion, DALL-E 2, GPT-4V, and virtually every text-to-image and vision-language model released since 2021.

---

## 2. Historical Context

Before CLIP, computer vision models were trained with:
- **Supervised learning on labeled datasets** (ImageNet, COCO): require expensive human annotation; limited to pre-defined categories
- **Self-supervised learning** (SimCLR, MoCo): learned visual representations but still required task-specific fine-tuning
- **Transfer learning** (ImageNet → fine-tune): good representations but always required labeled target data

The field knew that vast amounts of image-text pairs existed on the internet (product listings, image captions, news photos). Nobody had successfully trained a vision model at scale using this noisy, naturally occurring supervision — until CLIP.

---

## 3. Research Problem

Can a vision model trained on **natural language supervision** (noisy internet image-text pairs) learn representations that transfer zero-shot to any downstream visual task, without labeled examples?

Two sub-problems:
1. What training objective effectively learns from noisy image-text data?
2. At what scale does this produce useful representations?

## 5. Why Previous Methods Failed

**Captioning objectives (predicting exact text from image) don't scale well.** The language space is enormous — predicting exact word sequences for millions of image-text pairs creates an intractable learning problem. You need to perfectly generate "A photo of a golden retriever playing fetch on a sunny afternoon at a dog park" — a near-impossible sequence prediction task.

**Contrastive learning on images alone** (SimCLR, MoCo) produces excellent visual representations but has no language grounding — you can't ask "which image shows a cat" because the model has no text encoder.

---

## 6. Core Innovation

**Contrastive image-text pre-training:**

For a batch of $N$ image-text pairs:
1. Encode all $N$ images with the image encoder → $N$ image embeddings $I_i$
2. Encode all $N$ texts with the text encoder → $N$ text embeddings $T_j$
3. Compute the $N \times N$ pairwise cosine similarity matrix
4. Train with symmetric cross-entropy loss to maximize similarity for matching pairs $(I_i, T_i)$ and minimize for non-matching pairs $(I_i, T_j)$ where $i \neq j$

The contrastive objective predicts *which* text matches *which* image in the batch — a much simpler task than generating exact text, but still sufficient to learn rich multimodal representations.

**Zero-shot inference:**

To classify an image into $K$ classes:
1. Construct $K$ text prompts: "A photo of a {class}"
2. Encode all $K$ prompts with the text encoder
3. Encode the query image with the image encoder
4. Take the class whose text embedding has the highest cosine similarity to the image embedding

No gradient updates. Pure similarity search.

## 7. Architecture Breakdown

CLIP trained five image encoder variants:

| Image Encoder | Parameters | Notes |
|:---|:---|:---|
| ResNet-50 | 38M | Modified: attention pooling instead of GAP |
| ResNet-101 | 57M | |
| ViT-B/32 | 88M | Patch size 32 |
| ViT-B/16 | 86M | Patch size 16 |
| **ViT-L/14** | **307M** | **Best performing** |

**Text Encoder:** Transformer (63M parameters), 12 layers, 512 width, 8 heads, context length 77 tokens, BPE vocabulary 49,152.

**Training objective (temperature-scaled contrastive loss):**

$$L = -\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^N \left[\log\frac{e^{\text{sim}(I_i, T_i)/\tau}}{\sum_j e^{\text{sim}(I_i, T_j)/\tau}} + \log\frac{e^{\text{sim}(T_i, I_i)/\tau}}{\sum_j e^{\text{sim}(T_j, I_j)/\tau}}\right]$$

Where $\tau$ is a learned temperature parameter.

---

## 8. Mathematical Foundations

**Cosine Similarity:**
$$\text{sim}(I, T) = \frac{I \cdot T}{\|I\| \|T\|}$$

**InfoNCE Loss (the contrastive objective):**
$$L_{i2t} = -\log\frac{\exp(\text{sim}(I_i, T_i)/\tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^N \exp(\text{sim}(I_i, T_j)/\tau)}$$

This is exactly cross-entropy over $N$ classes, where the "class" is the correct text match. Large batch sizes (32,768) are critical — larger $N$ means more negative examples, making the task harder and the representations more discriminative.

**Zero-shot prediction:**

$$p(y = k | x) = \frac{\exp(\text{sim}(I_x, T_k)/\tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^K \exp(\text{sim}(I_x, T_j)/\tau)}$$

## 9. Key Experiments

**Zero-shot ImageNet:**

| Model | Top-1 Accuracy | Training Data |
|:---|:---|:---|
| ResNet-50 (supervised) | 76.1% | 1.28M labeled ImageNet |
| Linear probe (BiT) | 84.9% | JFT-300M |
| **CLIP ViT-L/14 (zero-shot)** | **76.2%** | **400M noisy pairs, no ImageNet labels** |

**27 datasets, zero-shot vs. linear probe:**
- CLIP zero-shot outperforms ImageNet-supervised linear probes on 16/27 datasets
- Significantly better on distribution-shifted variants (ImageNet-V2, ImageNet-Sketch, ObjectNet)

## 10. Benchmark Results

| Task | CLIP Zero-Shot | Previous SOTA |
|:---|:---|:---|
| ImageNet Top-1 | 76.2% | 76.1% (supervised ResNet-50) |
| ImageNet-R | 88.9% | 76.7% (supervised) |
| ImageNet-Sketch | 60.8% | 25.7% (supervised) |
| STL-10 | 99.3% | 97.8% (supervised) |
| MNIST | 76.0% | 99.7% (supervised) |

**CLIP is notably weak on MNIST** — because internet image-text pairs rarely discuss handwritten digits in context. Zero-shot requires the pre-training distribution to cover the task.

## 11. Ablation Studies

| Batch Size | Contrastive Performance |
|:---|:---|
| 32K | Best (more negatives = harder task = better representations) |
| 8K | -2% |
| 2K | -4% |

Prompt engineering matters significantly:
- "cat" → 63.3% on ImageNet
- "A photo of a cat" → 63.9%
- Ensemble of 80 prompts → 76.2% (+12.9% improvement from prompt engineering)

## 12. Implementation Details

- **Dataset:** WebImageText (WIT) — 400M image-text pairs from internet search
- **Training:** 32 epochs (32K batch size); ~600M steps on 256 V100 GPUs for ViT-L/14
- **Training duration:** ~18 days for ViT-B/32; longer for ViT-L/14
- **Temperature $\tau$:** Learned; initialized to 0.07
- **Data augmentation:** Random square crop; color jitter (small)
- **No labeled data used in pre-training**

---

## 13. Engineering Insights

- **Batch size is the hyperparameter.** InfoNCE's effectiveness scales with batch size. The 32,768 batch CLIP used requires 256 GPUs just to hold the batch — this is why CLIP-level training wasn't accessible to academic labs until years later.
- **Prompt engineering is required for best zero-shot.** "A photo of a {label}" consistently outperforms bare label names. The 80-prompt ensemble CLIP uses for official evaluation is non-trivial engineering.
- **CLIP embeddings are universal features.** The image embeddings transfer to object detection (ViLD), segmentation (SAM uses CLIP-aligned representations), video understanding (VideoCLIP), and audio (AudioCLIP). No single feature extractor before CLIP had this breadth.
- **CLIP text encoder is the de facto standard for text conditioning.** Stable Diffusion, DALL-E 2, and most text-to-image models use CLIP's text encoder as the conditioning signal, frozen.

---

## 14. Limitations

- **Weak on fine-grained or specialized tasks.** Differentiating dog breeds, counting objects, reading text in images — tasks that don't appear in natural image-text pairs on the internet.
- **Biased toward internet data distribution.** Western-centric, English-centric, skewed toward common objects and concepts.
- **Not generative.** CLIP generates embeddings, not images. You need a generative model (diffusion, autoregressive) conditioned on CLIP embeddings.
- **Prompt sensitivity.** Small changes in prompt wording can dramatically affect zero-shot accuracy.
