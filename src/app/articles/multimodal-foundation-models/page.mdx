# Multimodal Foundation Models

<Callout type="tip" title="Key Insight">
Human intelligence isn't limited to reading text; we process the world through sight, sound, and language simultaneously. Multimodal foundation models replicate this by fusing visual encoders (like ViTs) with large language models, allowing the AI to "see" and "read" at the same time.
</Callout>

## The Modality Gap

Language models process discrete text tokens, while vision models process continuous pixel patches. The fundamental challenge of multimodality is bridging this gap so that an image of a dog and the word "dog" share the exact same semantic representation in the model's latent space.

Most multimodal models avoid training everything from scratch. Instead, they take a pre-trained Vision Encoder (like CLIP ViT) and a pre-trained LLM (like LLaMA), and freeze their weights. They then train a small "connector" network sitting between them to translate the vision features into the text token space.

## Evolution of the Connector

How you connect the vision model to the language model defines the architecture:
- **Flamingo (DeepMind):** Instead of feeding images into the input prompt, Flamingo inserted *cross-attention layers* deep inside the LLM. The text layers would pause and "look" at the vision features while generating text. It was the first model to demonstrate strong few-shot visual reasoning.
- **InstructBLIP:** Uses a **Q-Former** (Querying Transformer). It acts as an information bottleneck, forcing the vision features to compress down into exactly 32 output tokens that are most relevant to the user's text prompt, drastically reducing computational cost.
- **LLaVA (Large Language-and-Vision Assistant):** Took the simplest possible approach. It uses a basic two-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to project the vision features directly into the text embedding space. Because it was fine-tuned on GPT-4 generated multimodal instruction data, it achieved SOTA results with an incredibly simple architecture.

## Any-to-Any: Native Multimodality

Models like LLaVA are "Frankenstein" models—stitched together from separate vision and text models. This means they cannot natively output images, only text. 

The frontier is **Native Any-to-Any Multimodality**. Models like **GPT-4o** and **Gemini 1.5** are trained from the ground up on interleaved text, audio, and images. They process raw audio directly (rather than transcribing it to text first), which allows them to hear tone, emotion, and background noise, and respond with native voice generation in milliseconds.

## Video Understanding in VLMs

Video poses a massive challenge for VLMs because a 1-minute video at 30 FPS contains 1,800 images. If you feed 1,800 images into an LLM, it will instantly run out of memory. 

To process video, models rely on temporal downsampling (taking 1 frame per second) and spatial pooling (compressing the visual features over time). Gemini 1.5 handles this by treating video frames as an extremely long sequence of tokens, relying on highly optimized attention mechanisms to search through hours of video for a single visual detail.
