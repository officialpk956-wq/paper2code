# GAN Evolution: From Goodfellow to StyleGAN3

<Callout type="tip" title="Key Insight">
GANs work through adversarial training: a generator tries to fool a discriminator, and a discriminator tries to catch the generator. Neither wins — they improve each other until the generator produces outputs indistinguishable from real data. The challenge is making this training stable. Every major GAN architecture solved a specific stability or quality problem.
</Callout>

## The Original GAN (2014)

Ian Goodfellow's 2014 paper introduced generative adversarial networks as a minimax game between two networks:

$$\min_G \max_D \; \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{\text{data}}}[\log D(x)] + \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_z}[\log(1 - D(G(z)))]$$

The **discriminator** $D$ maximizes its ability to distinguish real from fake. The **generator** $G$ minimizes the discriminator's ability to distinguish — equivalently, it maximizes $\log D(G(z))$ to produce outputs the discriminator classifies as real.

The original GAN used fully connected layers and trained on simple datasets like MNIST. The key problems it left unsolved: **mode collapse** (the generator producing only one or a few output types to reliably fool the discriminator) and **training instability** (the discriminator becoming too strong, driving generator gradients to zero).

## DCGAN (2015): Making It Convolutional

**DCGAN (Radford et al., 2015)** replaced fully connected layers with transposed convolutions in the generator and strided convolutions in the discriminator. Additional changes: batch normalization in both networks, LeakyReLU in the discriminator, ReLU in the generator, no pooling layers.

These changes produced stable training on CIFAR-10 and LSUN bedroom images. DCGAN also discovered that the latent space $z$ has interpretable arithmetic: "man with glasses" - "man" + "woman" ≈ "woman with glasses." The latent space encodes semantic concepts in linear directions.

DCGAN became the standard baseline for evaluating GAN improvements. Its architecture choices (batch norm, strided convolutions, no fully connected layers) remain in most modern GAN architectures.

## CycleGAN (2017): Unpaired Image Translation

**CycleGAN (Zhu et al., 2017)** solved a fundamentally different problem: image-to-image translation without paired training data.

Previous approaches required paired datasets (e.g., the same horse photograph also rendered as a zebra). CycleGAN's key insight: use a **cycle consistency loss**. Train two generators ($G: X \to Y$ and $F: Y \to X$) simultaneously. A photo translated to a painting and back should recover the original: $F(G(x)) \approx x$. This cycle consistency constrains the translations to be semantically meaningful even without paired data.

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{cyc}}(G, F) = \mathbb{E}_x[\|F(G(x)) - x\|_1] + \mathbb{E}_y[\|G(F(y)) - y\|_1]$$

CycleGAN enabled: horse ↔ zebra, summer ↔ winter, photo ↔ painting (Monet style), apple ↔ orange. All without a single paired training example. The technique generalized broadly — any two image domains where you can collect unpaired examples.

## ProGAN (2018): Progressive Growing

**ProGAN (Karras et al., NVIDIA, 2018)** solved the challenge of generating high-resolution images (1024×1024) with a training strategy: **progressive growing**.

Start training with 4×4 images. Once stable, add a new layer for 8×8 resolution, fading it in with a learnable blend weight. Repeat: 16×16, 32×32, ..., 1024×1024. At each stage, the network only needs to add new structure on top of existing low-resolution features, rather than learning everything from scratch at full resolution.

Additional contributions: **minibatch standard deviation** (a feature added to the discriminator that measures within-batch variation, penalizing mode collapse where all generated images are similar), and **pixelwise feature normalization** (normalize each pixel's feature vector independently, preventing the generator from amplifying activations to dominate the discriminator).

ProGAN generated celebrity faces at 1024×1024 with unprecedented quality. The progressive training approach was adopted by StyleGAN and most subsequent high-resolution GANs.

## StyleGAN (2018–2021): Disentangling Style

**StyleGAN (Karras et al., NVIDIA, 2018)** replaced ProGAN's input noise $z$ with a mapping network that converts $z$ into an intermediate latent space $W$. This $W$ space is much more disentangled — individual directions in $W$ correspond to single perceptual attributes (age, hair color, pose) rather than the entangled combinations in $z$.

The $W$ latent controls the generator through **Adaptive Instance Normalization (AdaIN)** at each resolution level. Instead of injecting the latent once at the input, StyleGAN injects style at every layer, controlling different aspects of the image at different scales:

- **Coarse styles** (4–8px resolution layers): pose, face shape, hair style
- **Middle styles** (16–32px): facial features, eye shape
- **Fine styles** (64–1024px): color, microstructure, skin texture

This multi-scale style injection enables **style mixing**: use one latent for coarse style, another for fine style. The result: combine the pose of one face with the skin texture of another.

**StyleGAN2 (2019)** removed the characteristic "blob" artifacts from StyleGAN by replacing AdaIN with **weight modulation/demodulation** — mathematically equivalent to AdaIN but without the normalization artifacts. Added **path length regularization** that encourages perceptually smooth traversals of the $W$ space.

**StyleGAN3 (2021)** solved a subtle problem: StyleGAN2 images had "texture sticking" — when you move the camera or rotate a face, surface texture stays fixed in screen space rather than moving with the geometry. StyleGAN3's **alias-free operations** ensure that texture is attached to the underlying geometry, making generated videos much more coherent.

<Callout type="info" title="FID: Fréchet Inception Distance">
FID measures GAN quality by comparing the distribution of real images to generated images in a feature space extracted from InceptionNet. Lower FID = distributions more similar = better quality and diversity. FID is sensitive to both image quality (blurry images get bad FID) and mode coverage (mode collapse gets very bad FID even if individual images look sharp). It's not perfect but is the standard benchmark.
</Callout>

## After GANs: The Diffusion Transition

By 2022, **diffusion models** (DDPM, Stable Diffusion) surpassed GANs on image generation quality at scale. The reasons:

1. **Training stability** — diffusion models optimize a simple L2 loss against a fixed forward process. GANs require careful balance between generator and discriminator.
2. **Mode coverage** — diffusion models are trained to maximize likelihood across the full data distribution. GANs are prone to mode collapse.
3. **Text conditioning** — diffusion models integrate text conditioning naturally via cross-attention. GAN text conditioning (GigaGAN, StyleGAN-T) is technically challenging.

However, GANs retain advantages: **inference speed** (one forward pass vs. 50 U-Net calls) and **fine-grained control** (editing in W-space). Modern systems like GAN-distilled diffusion models and consistency models are attempting to combine the best of both.

## Practice

<Callout type="info">
The best GAN experiment: train a DCGAN on CelebA (celebrity faces) in 30 minutes on a single GPU using the PyTorch official tutorial. Watch mode collapse happen in real time by reducing the discriminator learning rate — the generator will start producing only one type of face. Understanding mode collapse experientially is more valuable than any written explanation.
</Callout>
