# Audio and Speech Models: From CNNs to Whisper

<Callout type="tip" title="Key Insight">
Audio processing has shifted from hand-crafted acoustic features (like MFCCs) and Hidden Markov Models to end-to-end deep learning. By treating audio spectrograms as images or continuous 1D sequences, models like Whisper and wav2vec 2.0 have achieved superhuman performance in speech-to-text tasks.
</Callout>

## The Shift to End-to-End Speech Recognition

Historically, automatic speech recognition (ASR) required complex, multi-stage pipelines: an acoustic model, a pronunciation dictionary, and a language model, all trained separately. Modern ASR uses end-to-end neural networks that map audio directly to text.

The primary input to these networks is typically a log-mel spectrogram—a visual representation of the audio frequencies over time, scaled logarithmically to match human hearing perception.

## wav2vec 2.0: Self-Supervised Speech

In 2020, Facebook AI released **wav2vec 2.0**, proving that speech models could be trained largely without labeled data. It relies on a contrastive self-supervised learning task.

1. **CNN Encoder:** Raw audio waveforms are passed through a multi-layer Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) to extract latent representations.
2. **Masking:** Certain spans of these representations are masked out (similar to BERT in NLP).
3. **Contrastive Loss:** A Transformer network attempts to predict the true quantized representations of the masked spans, distinguishing them from randomly sampled distractors.

This pre-training is incredibly powerful. By fine-tuning a pre-trained wav2vec 2.0 model on just **10 minutes** of labeled audio, it can achieve state-of-the-art (SOTA) results that previously required hundreds of hours of transcribed data.

## HuBERT: Clustering for Pseudo-Labels

Building on wav2vec 2.0, **HuBERT** (Hidden-Unit BERT) simplified the self-supervised objective. Instead of a complex contrastive loss, HuBERT runs a k-means clustering algorithm on the raw acoustic features to generate discrete "pseudo-labels." 

The model is then trained with a standard masked language modeling loss—predicting the correct k-means cluster for masked audio regions. This approach forced the model to learn both acoustic and linguistic representations simultaneously, matching or beating wav2vec 2.0.

## OpenAI Whisper: The Power of Scale

While wav2vec 2.0 and HuBERT focused on unsupervised learning, OpenAI's **Whisper** took the opposite approach: massive, weakly supervised learning. 

Whisper is an encoder-decoder Transformer. It takes 30-second chunks of log-mel spectrograms, processes them through a CNN patch embedding layer, and then passes them to a standard Transformer encoder-decoder to predict text.

What makes Whisper revolutionary is the dataset: **680,000 hours** of multilingual and multitask supervised data scraped from the web. The largest model (Whisper large-v3) contains 1.5 billion parameters and can transcribe, translate, and perform language identification across 99 different languages with near-human robustness to accents, background noise, and technical jargon.

## Decoders: CTC vs RNN-T vs Attention

When outputting text from audio, models must align the varying length of audio frames with the discrete output characters. 
- **CTC (Connectionist Temporal Classification):** Predicts a character (or a blank token) at every audio frame, then collapses repeats. It assumes outputs are conditionally independent, which is fast but limits language modeling capability.
- **Attention-based Decoders (like Whisper):** Cross-attention allows the text decoder to look anywhere in the audio encoder's output. Highly accurate, but struggles with long-form alignment.
- **RNN-T (Recurrent Neural Network Transducer):** An autoregressive streaming architecture used in Google's voice assistants. It combines an acoustic encoder and a text predictor, making it ideal for real-time dictation.
