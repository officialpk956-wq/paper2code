# Knowledge Distillation: Training Small Models from Large Ones

<Callout type="tip" title="Key Insight">
A model trained to mimic another model's output distribution learns more than a model trained on hard labels alone. When GPT-4 outputs [0.97, 0.02, 0.01] for [cat, dog, tiger], the 0.02 for "dog" and 0.01 for "tiger" contain information: cats are more dog-like than tiger-like. A student trained on these soft distributions inherits the teacher's uncertainty estimates, not just its classifications. This is why DistilBERT at 60% the size achieves 97% of BERT's performance.
</Callout>

## The Core Problem: Deployment vs. Capability

Large models are capable. Small models are deployable. The gap between them is the central tension of applied ML:

- **GPT-4**: ~1.8T parameters, estimated 8 A100s per inference, ~$0.01 per 1K tokens
- **GPT-4o mini**: competitive quality, 10× cheaper, 5× faster
- **DistilBERT**: 66M parameters (vs BERT's 110M), 40% smaller, 60% faster, 97% quality

Naive approaches to getting small models: train a smaller architecture from scratch (works, but loses information), prune unimportant weights (leaves a sparse network that's hard to accelerate), quantize to low precision (reduces memory but not compute). **Knowledge distillation** takes a fundamentally different path: use the large model as a teacher to guide the small model's training.

## Soft Labels: The Key Insight

**Hinton et al. (2015)** formalized knowledge distillation with the concept of **soft labels**. When a trained model outputs probabilities, these probabilities contain relational information invisible in the hard label.

For an image of a cat:
- **Hard label**: [1, 0, 0] (cat, dog, tiger)
- **Teacher soft label at T=1**: [0.97, 0.02, 0.01]
- **Teacher soft label at T=4**: [0.55, 0.30, 0.15]

The soft label at T=4 reveals that this particular cat image is somewhat dog-like (0.30 — perhaps it's a fluffy cat) and less tiger-like (0.15). A student trained on the T=4 distribution learns these relationships — it doesn't just learn "this is a cat," it learns "cats are related to dogs in a specific way."

The **temperature** $T$ controls information entropy:

$$p_i = \frac{\exp(z_i / T)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j / T)}$$

Higher temperature → softer distribution → more information about inter-class relationships. Lower temperature → approaches one-hot → less relational information.

**The distillation loss** combines soft label matching with original task loss:

$$\mathcal{L} = \alpha \cdot T^2 \cdot \text{KL}(p_T^{\text{teacher}} || p_T^{\text{student}}) + (1-\alpha) \cdot \text{CE}(y_{\text{hard}}, p_{\text{student}})$$

The $T^2$ factor compensates for the gradient magnitude reduction from soft distributions. Typical values: $\alpha = 0.9$, $T = 4$.

## Feature Distillation: Beyond Output Matching

Output-level distillation transfers what the teacher knows about the final decision. **Feature distillation** transfers how the teacher thinks — the internal representations at intermediate layers.

**FitNets (Romero et al., 2014)** match intermediate feature maps between teacher and student. A student layer is trained to produce features similar to a corresponding teacher layer (with a learned projection to handle dimension mismatch):

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{hint}} = \frac{1}{2}\|u_s(h_s) - h_t\|_F^2$$

Where $h_s$ and $h_t$ are student and teacher hidden activations, and $u_s$ is a learned projection (since the student layer has different dimensions).

**Attention transfer (Zagoruyko & Komodakis, 2017)** transfers attention maps — where in the image each layer activates strongly. The teacher's attention maps encode its "what matters" judgment, which is often as important as the final prediction.

## DistilBERT: Task-Agnostic BERT Distillation

**DistilBERT (Sanh et al., 2019)** distills BERT-base (12 layers, 110M params) into a 6-layer, 66M parameter student. The key design choices:

**Architecture**: half as many Transformer layers, same hidden dimension (768). Initialization from every other layer of BERT (layers 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10) — faster convergence than random initialization.

**Triple loss**:
1. Soft label KL loss (distillation on masked language model predictions)
2. Hard label cross-entropy (standard MLM loss)
3. Cosine embedding loss between teacher and student hidden states

**Training data**: same corpus as BERT (BooksCorpus + English Wikipedia), not task-specific data. This makes DistilBERT task-agnostic — one distilled model works for any downstream task, fine-tuned the same way as BERT.

Result: 40% smaller, 60% faster at inference, 97% of BERT-base performance on GLUE. Used in production by Hugging Face and many companies where latency matters more than last-mile accuracy.

## DeepSeek-R1 → Qwen-7B: LLM Reasoning Distillation

The most impressive recent distillation result: **DeepSeek distilled DeepSeek-R1** (reasoning model, 671B parameters) into Qwen-7B (7B parameters) and beat OpenAI's o1-mini on AIME 2024.

The approach differs from standard output distillation. Instead of soft probability matching, DeepSeek generated:
1. **Long-form reasoning traces** from R1 on math and coding problems
2. These traces include chain-of-thought, backtracking, and verification steps
3. Qwen-7B was fine-tuned via SFT on these traces — it learned to produce similar reasoning chains

The student doesn't just learn the final answer — it learns the reasoning process. A 7B model watching a 671B model "think through" a math problem and then producing similar reasoning chains achieves qualitatively superior results to a 7B model trained only on (problem, answer) pairs.

<Callout type="info" title="Why Reasoning Traces Transfer Better Than Soft Labels">
For math and coding, the final answer distribution is nearly one-hot (either correct or not). Soft label distillation provides little signal. But the reasoning trace — the sequence of intermediate steps — is rich with information about problem-solving strategy. A student that learns to produce similar traces learns the teacher's problem decomposition, not just its final outputs. This is the key insight behind the DeepSeek distillation success.
</Callout>

## Data-Free Distillation

Standard distillation requires the original training data to generate teacher outputs. Sometimes this data isn't available (privacy, licensing, lost). **Data-free distillation** generates synthetic samples using the teacher itself:

**DAFL (Data-Augmentation-Free Learning)** trains a generator network (similar to GAN) to produce images that maximize the teacher's class confidence and batch normalization statistics. The teacher is used both to guide the generator and to label the generated images.

**Fast-DFKD** combines the generator with an efficient meta-learning approach, achieving 90%+ of the performance of data-full distillation in minutes rather than hours.

These techniques matter for deploying models where the original training data can't be retained for privacy reasons (medical data, personal communications).

## Practice

<Callout type="info">
Implement distillation in 10 lines of PyTorch: take a trained ResNet-50 (teacher) and a ResNet-18 (student), run both on the same batch, compute KL divergence between temperature-softened outputs, add to the standard cross-entropy loss, and train the student. The student trained with distillation should outperform the same student trained from scratch with hard labels on any non-trivial dataset.
</Callout>
