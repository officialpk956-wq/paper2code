# Quantization and Pruning: Compressing AI

<Callout type="tip" title="Key Insight">
Modern LLMs and vision models are too large to fit in the RAM of standard computers or mobile phones. By systematically removing unnecessary connections (Pruning) and reducing the mathematical precision of the weights (Quantization), we can compress models by up to 80% with minimal loss in accuracy.
</Callout>

## Weight Quantization

Neural networks are traditionally trained using 32-bit floating-point numbers (FP32), which take up 4 bytes of memory per parameter. A 7-billion parameter model in FP32 requires 28GB of VRAM just to load!

**Quantization** reduces this precision. By mapping the continuous FP32 values into a smaller set of discrete buckets, we can store weights as 8-bit integers (INT8, 1 byte) or even 4-bit integers (INT4, half a byte). This shrinks the model size by 4x to 8x and drastically speeds up inference, since integer math is faster than floating-point math.

### Post-Training vs QAT
- **Post-Training Quantization (PTQ):** You take a fully trained FP32 model and simply round the weights down. Advanced algorithms like **GPTQ** or **AWQ** do this intelligently layer-by-layer to minimize the accuracy degradation.
- **Quantization-Aware Training (QAT):** The model is trained from scratch with "fake quantization" nodes inserted into the computational graph. The model explicitly learns to adapt to the lower precision during training, resulting in much higher accuracy than PTQ.

## Network Pruning

While quantization shrinks the size of the weights, **Pruning** removes the weights entirely. It is based on the observation that deep neural networks are vastly over-parameterized—many neurons contribute almost nothing to the final prediction.

- **Unstructured Pruning:** You look at all the weights in the network and simply delete any weight whose absolute magnitude is close to zero (magnitude thresholding). This creates a sparse matrix. However, standard GPUs are bad at processing sparse matrices, so this often doesn't yield actual speedups.
- **Structured Pruning:** You delete entire channels, filters, or layers. By physically removing entire rows of computation, you generate a smaller, dense matrix that runs incredibly fast on standard hardware.

## The Lottery Ticket Hypothesis

In 2018, Frankle and Carbin published the **Lottery Ticket Hypothesis**. They proved that inside every massive, randomly initialized neural network, there exists a tiny sub-network (the "winning ticket") that, if trained in isolation, can achieve the exact same accuracy as the massive network. 

The only reason we need massive networks is because we are buying millions of "lottery tickets" to guarantee we initialize a winning one. The goal of modern pruning research is to find ways to identify this winning ticket without having to train the massive network first.

## Knowledge Distillation Combination

Pruning and quantization are often combined with **Knowledge Distillation**. 

If you aggressively prune a large model (the Teacher), its accuracy drops. To fix this, you use Knowledge Distillation to force the small, pruned model (the Student) to mimic the exact output probability distribution (the soft labels) of the massive Teacher model. This allows the highly compressed model to retain the rich, nuanced reasoning capabilities of the original network.
