# Video Understanding: Beyond Still Images

<Callout type="tip" title="Key Insight">
Video is not just a sequence of static images; it contains both spatial appearance (what objects look like) and temporal motion (how things move). The challenge in video understanding is capturing this motion without being overwhelmed by the massive computational cost of processing dozens of frames per second.
</Callout>

## The Problem with 3D Convolutions

The most intuitive way to process a video is to add a time dimension to a standard Convolutional Neural Network (CNN). Instead of 2D convolutions ($H \times W$), you use 3D convolutions ($T \times H \times W$). However, 3D CNNs are notoriously heavy. They require vast amounts of memory and data to train without overfitting. 

To solve this, researchers developed decoupled architectures that separate spatial and temporal processing.

## Two-Stream Networks

The foundational approach to video understanding is the **Two-Stream Network**. It consists of two parallel pathways:
1. **Spatial Stream:** Processes individual RGB frames using a standard 2D CNN to understand scenes and objects.
2. **Temporal Stream:** Processes dense Optical Flow (a pre-computed map of pixel displacement between frames) to explicitly capture motion.

The outputs of both streams are fused late in the network to make a final prediction (like classifying a human action). This explicit separation made it much easier for the network to understand motion without relying on heavy 3D convolutions.

## SlowFast: Asymmetric Processing

The **SlowFast** architecture, developed by Facebook AI (now Meta), took the two-stream concept further by recognizing a biological truth about vision: spatial details change slowly, while motion changes rapidly.

SlowFast replaces optical flow with raw RGB frames but processes them at two different frame rates:
- **The Slow Pathway:** Operates at a low frame rate (e.g., 2 frames per second) but with a high channel capacity. It acts as the spatial workhorse, recognizing detailed objects and textures.
- **The Fast Pathway:** Operates at a high frame rate (e.g., 16 fps) but with very few channels (a lightweight capacity). It acts as the temporal workhorse, capturing rapid, fine-grained motion.

Because the Fast pathway is so lightweight, SlowFast achieves state-of-the-art accuracy with significantly fewer FLOPS than traditional 3D models.

## Transformers Enter Video: TimeSformer

As Transformers conquered NLP and 2D vision (ViT), they inevitably arrived at video. The **TimeSformer** architecture introduced *divided space-time attention*. 

Instead of computing self-attention across every patch in every frame simultaneously (which scales quadratically and instantly runs out of memory), TimeSformer applies attention alternately:
1. Compute spatial attention within a single frame.
2. Compute temporal attention across the same patch position over time.

This separation allows Transformers to process long video sequences efficiently without explicitly calculating optical flow.

## Self-Supervised Learning: VideoMAE

Supervised labeling of video datasets (like Kinetics-400) is incredibly expensive. **VideoMAE** (Masked Autoencoders for Video) applied the success of image MAEs to the temporal domain.

Because consecutive video frames contain highly redundant information, simply masking 75% of patches (as done in images) allows the model to easily cheat by looking at adjacent frames. VideoMAE solves this by applying an extreme **90% tube masking strategy**—hiding 90% of the video's spacetime cubes entirely.

By forcing the model to reconstruct the video from a tiny 10% glimpse, it learns deeply robust spatio-temporal representations. It can then be fine-tuned on small, labeled datasets to achieve SOTA action recognition performance.
